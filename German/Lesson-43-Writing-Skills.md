# Lesson 43 — Writing in German: Emails, Messaging, and Register Control

**Phase:** 7 — Skills Integration
**Status:** ✅ Done

---

## 1. The core idea

You mentioned basic reading/writing practice on your own — this lesson systematizes German writing across the registers you'll actually need: formal emails (building on Lesson 39's professional vocabulary), casual messaging (building on Lesson 35's discourse markers), and the general register-shifting skill that ties everything together, since written German often makes different choices than spoken German for the exact same idea.

## 2. Formal email structure — the complete template

Building directly on Lesson 39's phrases, here's the full skeleton:

```
Sehr geehrte/r Frau/Herr [Nachname],

[Opening line — often stating the reason for writing]
ich schreibe Ihnen bezüglich...
ich wollte mich erkundigen, ob...

[Main content — the actual request/information]

[Closing line before sign-off]
Für Rückfragen stehe ich gerne zur Verfügung.
(I'm happy to answer any follow-up questions.)
Vielen Dank im Voraus.
(Thanks in advance.)

Mit freundlichen Grüßen,
[Ihr Vorname Nachname]
```

**Register notes:** written formal German tends toward the "frame-first," precision-valued style from Lesson 17 — sentences are often longer and more structured than you'd produce in speech, and Konjunktiv II (Lesson 25) appears more frequently for politeness ("Ich würde mich freuen, wenn...").

## 3. Casual messaging (WhatsApp, texting friends)

Genuinely different register — short, fragment-friendly, heavy on Lesson 35's contractions and Lesson 32's particles:

- **Bin gleich da!** (Be right there! — subject "ich" dropped entirely, genuinely normal in casual texting)
- **Alles klar, bis später!** (Sounds good, see you later!)
- **Hab grad keine Zeit, melde mich später.** (Don't have time right now, I'll get back to you later — "habe" → "hab," "gerade" → "grad," subject dropped)
- **Kein Ding!** (No big deal! — even more casual than Lesson 34's "Kein Problem")
- Emojis and abbreviations (LG = Liebe Grüße, i.e., a casual "regards"; VG = Viele Grüße) function similarly to English texting conventions

**Mental model:** casual texting German often **drops the subject pronoun** when it's recoverable from context, similar to how Spanish can drop subject pronouns due to verb conjugation carrying the information — German doesn't have Spanish's grammatical justification for this (German verbs don't fully disambiguate person the way Spanish ones do), but casual written register does it anyway, purely as an informality marker. This is worth actively adopting in your own casual texting, since it's a genuine native-sounding move.

## 4. The written/spoken register gap: same idea, different choices

This is worth seeing side by side, pulling together several past lessons:

| Spoken/casual | Written/formal |
|---|---|
| Ich hab keine Zeit. | Ich habe leider keine Zeit. |
| Kannst du mir helfen? | Ich würde mich freuen, wenn Sie mir helfen könnten. |
| Ich denke, das ist gut. | Meiner Meinung nach ist dies positiv zu bewerten. |
| ...weil ich müde bin. | ...da ich müde bin. *("da" as a more formal/written alternative to "weil," genuinely common in written register — new vocabulary worth flagging here* |
| Das Auto von meinem Vater | Das Auto meines Vaters *(Genitiv, Lesson 12, written register)* |

**New item worth flagging:** **da** as a written-register alternative to **weil** — same verb-final subordinate clause mechanics from Lesson 2, just a more formal-sounding conjunction choice, common in reports, essays, and formal writing.

## 5. Structuring longer written pieces: paragraph-level organization

For anything beyond a short email (a report, an essay, a longer message), German written structure tends to value explicit signposting more than English/Spanish informal writing does — connecting to Lesson 39's academic connectors:

- Opening: **Im Folgenden möchte ich...** (In the following, I would like to...)
- Transitioning: **Darüber hinaus...** / **Außerdem...** (Furthermore.../Besides...)
- Contrasting: **Allerdings...** / **Jedoch...** (However...)
- Concluding: **Zusammenfassend lässt sich sagen, dass...** (In summary...)

**Mental model:** this connects directly to Lesson 17's frame-first thinking, scaled up to paragraph level — German formal writing likes to announce its own structure explicitly ("in the following, I will discuss X, then Y"), rather than letting structure emerge implicitly the way more casual English essay-writing sometimes does.

## 6. Progressively harder examples

1. Bin spät dran, bin in 10 Minuten da! *(casual texting — subject dropped twice)*
2. Sehr geehrte Frau Schmidt, ich schreibe Ihnen bezüglich meiner Anmeldung vom letzten Monat. *(formal email opener + Lesson 38's bureaucracy vocabulary)*
3. Da die Fristen sehr eng sind, bitte ich um eine schnelle Rückmeldung. *(da instead of weil, formal register, Lesson 38's Frist + Lesson 39's Rückmeldung)*
4. Zusammenfassend lässt sich sagen, dass die Zusammenarbeit trotz einiger Herausforderungen erfolgreich war. *(formal closing structure + Lesson 12's trotz + Lesson 39's Zusammenarbeit)*

## 7. Speaking exercise (writing-focused this lesson, so more of a drafting exercise)

Draft, out loud or in writing, one casual text message to a friend canceling plans, and one formal email to a colleague doing the same thing — noticing how differently you'd phrase the identical underlying message:

- Casual: *Kann heute leider nicht, muss was Wichtiges erledigen. Sorry!*
- Formal: *Ich möchte Ihnen leider mitteilen, dass ich heute aus wichtigen Gründen nicht teilnehmen kann.*

## 8. Thinking-in-German habit

Today, silently draft the opening line of a formal email you might realistically need to send here in Germany (to a landlord, an office, a doctor's practice), using this lesson's template.

## 9. Real-life mission

Write one real German sentence today in whichever register fits — a text to a German-speaking contact, or a formal email opener you save as a draft even if you don't send it yet. Actual written production, even low-stakes, builds this skill faster than passive review.
