# Lesson 15 — Verb + Preposition Combos: The Idioms That Don't Follow the Rules

**Phase:** 2 — The Case System (final lesson of this phase)
**Status:** ✅ Done

---

## 1. The core idea

Lesson 14 warned that some prepositions get "frozen" to a case by the verb they're attached to. This lesson covers that whole category — German verbs often come bundled with a fixed preposition and fixed case, essentially an idiom: learn each verb+preposition+case combo as one unit, like a phrasal verb.

This closes Phase 2 — the payoff: cases aren't abstract grammar, they're baked into thousands of everyday verb phrases.

**Mental model:** these are **verb-shaped Lego pieces pre-attached to a specific preposition-and-case connector**. Don't reason your way to "warten auf + Akkusativ" — just learn "warten" always comes with "auf" glued to it, wearing an accusative badge, as one inseparable chunk.

## 2. High-frequency verb + preposition combos

| Verb + Preposition | Case | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| warten auf | Akk | to wait for |
| sich freuen auf | Akk | to look forward to |
| sich freuen über | Akk | to be happy about (already happened) |
| denken an | Akk | to think of/about |
| sich interessieren für | Akk | to be interested in |
| sich erinnern an | Akk | to remember |
| sprechen über / von | Akk / Dat | to talk about |
| sich kümmern um | Akk | to take care of |
| Angst haben vor | Dat | to be afraid of |
| träumen von | Dat | to dream of/about |
| abhängen von | Dat | to depend on |
| helfen bei | Dat | to help with |

`sich freuen auf` vs. `sich freuen über`: **auf** for something still ahead (anticipation), **über** for something already happened (reaction). Ich freue mich **auf** das Wochenende (looking forward) vs. Ich freue mich **über** dein Geschenk (happy about a gift just received). No clean Spanish/English equivalent — both collapse into one phrase — worth actively noticing which one is needed.

⚠️ **Common Spanish-speaker mistake:** Spanish also has fixed verb+preposition idioms ("pensar en," "depender de," "tener miedo de"), tempting a direct preposition translation, which often fails:
- "pensar en" → tempts "denken in," but it's actually **denken an**.
- "depender de" → happens to match "abhängen von" — but don't rely on the pattern holding.

Treat every German verb+preposition combo as its own memorization unit.

## 3. Wo-compounds and da-compounds

To ask about or refer back to the object of one of these fixed prepositions for **things** (not people), German fuses the preposition with "wo-" (questions) or "da-" (answers/references):

- Worauf wartest du? (wo + auf → worauf, with an inserted -r-)
- Ich warte **darauf**. (da + auf → darauf)
- Woran denkst du?
- Ich denke **daran**.

**Important:** this fusion only happens for things/ideas, never people. For people, use normal preposition + pronoun:
- Worauf wartest du? — Ich warte darauf. (a thing — the bus, an answer)
- Auf wen wartest du? — Ich warte auf ihn. (a person — normal preposition + accusative pronoun)

**Mental model:** da-/wo- compounds are German's way of saying "it"/"what" when a preposition is glued to a thing rather than a person.

## 4. Progressively harder examples

1. Ich warte auf den Bus. → Ich warte darauf.
2. Woran erinnerst du dich? — Ich erinnere mich an meine Kindheit. (question + reflexive + fixed preposition)
3. Ich habe Angst davor, weil ich es nicht kontrollieren kann. (davor + weil)
4. Freust du dich schon auf den Urlaub, oder freust du dich mehr über das Ergebnis? (auf/über nuance)
5. Ich kann mich nicht darauf konzentrieren, weil es zu laut ist. (modal + nicht + da-compound + weil)

## 5. Speaking exercise

Convert the object into a da-compound, out loud:
- Ich denke oft an *meine Familie*. → Ich denke oft an *sie*. (person — stays a pronoun, no da-compound!)
- Ich denke oft an *die Zukunft*. → Ich denke oft ___. (thing — "daran")
- Ich warte auf *eine Antwort*. → Ich warte ___. (thing — "darauf")

Then produce one sentence with **sich freuen auf** about something you're anticipating, and one with **sich freuen über** about something good that recently happened.

## 6. Thinking-in-German habit

Narrate one thing you're looking forward to and one thing you're a little worried about:
> *Ich freue mich auf das Wochenende. Ich habe ein bisschen Angst vor meinem nächsten Deutschtest, aber ich übe jeden Tag.*

## 7. Real-life mission

Use one da-compound in a real conversation today — "Ich warte darauf" or "Ich denke oft daran" instead of repeating the full noun. A strong native-sounding move most learners skip.

## 8. Mini quiz

1. Fill in: "Ich interessiere mich ___ Musik."
2. Convert to a da-compound: "Ich spreche oft über meine Arbeit." → "Ich spreche oft ___."
3. Wo-compound or preposition+pronoun? "___ wartest du?" if waiting for your friend Anna.
4. Fill in: "Ich habe Angst ___ der Prüfung."
5. sich freuen auf or über: "Ich freue mich sehr ___ die gute Nachricht, die ich gerade bekommen habe."
