# 🇩🇪 German as a Second Native Language — Roadmap

**Learner profile:** Native Spanish speaker (Colombia), living in Germany long-term. English ~C1. German ~A2.2–B1.1.
**Current life mix:** Work in English, errands in German (basic), social life in English, heavy German audio input (podcasts/news), Duolingo daily, 2.5h/week live German class, light reading/writing on own.

**Diagnosis:** Strong passive input, very weak active production. Priority = speaking + thinking in German, not more passive exposure.

**Goal:** Not B2/C1/C2 as an exam target — German as a fully internalized second native language: thinking, working, socializing, dreaming in German.

## 🗺️ Full Roadmap

### Phase 0 — Foundations (how German is built, before diving into rules)
- History of German & Germanic languages; how German split from English's path
- How German organizes ideas differently from English/Spanish (verb-framing, compounding, abstraction style)
- The Satzklammer / verb bracket
- IPA overview, vowel system, umlauts, consonants, word/sentence stress, rhythm, connected speech

### Phase 1 — The Sentence Machine (core syntax)
- V2 rule deep dive & emphasis through word order
- Subordinate clauses & verb-final order (weil, dass, ob, wenn, als)
- Coordinating vs subordinating conjunctions
- Questions (W-questions, yes/no inversion)
- Negation: nicht vs kein, and where "nicht" goes
- Modal verbs + the double verb bracket
- Separable & inseparable verb prefixes
- Reflexive verbs
- Word order with two objects (Dativ before Akkusativ, pronoun rules)

### Phase 2 — The Case System (conceptual, not memorized)
- Why cases exist at all
- Nominativ & Akkusativ — subject vs direct object, article changes
- Dativ — indirect object, "to/for" logic, dative verbs
- Genitiv — possession, formal register, when it's dying out in spoken German
- Prepositions that fix a case (accusative preps, dative preps)
- Two-way (Wechselpräpositionen) prepositions — movement vs location
- Case + preposition + verb combos (fixed expressions)

### Phase 3 — Verbs in Depth
- Weak, strong, mixed verb patterns — ablaut series
- Perfekt vs Präteritum — when spoken Germans use which
- Plusquamperfekt
- Futur I & II (and how little Germans actually use Futur I)
- Passive voice (Vorgangspassiv vs Zustandspassiv)
- Reported speech & Konjunktiv I
- Konjunktiv II (hypotheticals, politeness, "würde" constructions)
- Participles as adjectives, extended participial phrases (Zeitungsdeutsch)
- Infinitive constructions (um...zu, ohne...zu, statt...zu)

### Phase 4 — Nouns, Adjectives, Gender
- Grammatical gender: patterns that predict gender
- Plural formation patterns
- Adjective endings via one unifying mental model
- Compound nouns: decomposition strategy, stress patterns, productivity

### Phase 5 — The Native Layer (what actually makes you sound German)
- Modal particles: doch, ja, mal, denn, wohl, eben, halt, eigentlich, nämlich, schon
- High-frequency collocations & lexical chunks (by theme)
- Idioms by frequency tier
- Discourse markers & spoken-language shortcuts
- Austrian & Swiss German differences
- Register control: formal vs informal, Sie vs du, professional vs casual writing

### Phase 6 — Vocabulary as Systems
- Word families & semantic fields (bureaucracy, health, work, emotions, banking, housing)
- Business/academic/technical vocabulary tracks
- Emotional & nuanced vocabulary — sarcasm, understatement
- Synonym clusters and when each variant is used

### Phase 7 — Skills Integration
- Listening strategy for fast native speech
- Writing: emails, professional writing, messaging register, academic writing
- "Thinking in German" habit system: daily narration, internal monologue drills
- Conversation simulations & role-play
- Immersion media curated to your level, upgraded progressively

### Ongoing, throughout every phase
- Daily/weekly spaced repetition review of past lessons
- One real-life mission per lesson
- Periodic diagnostic check-ins to re-target weak spots
