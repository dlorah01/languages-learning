# Lesson 6 — Modal Verbs: Building a Bigger Bracket

**Phase:** 1 — The Sentence Machine
**Status:** ✅ Done

---

## 1. The core idea

Modal verbs don't add a new verb position — they show the Satzklammer from Lesson 1 at full strength, with two verbs forming the bracket.

**Ich kann gut Deutsch sprechen.**
- `kann` (conjugated modal, position 2) ... [gut Deutsch] ... `sprechen` (infinitive, pushed to the end)

The modal verb conjugates normally and sits in position 2. The "real" verb stays in its infinitive form at the end — the Satzklammer structure from Lesson 1, now visible as two separate words instead of one verb splitting into pieces.

**Mental model:** Lesson 1's taco shell was one verb splitting into two pieces (`werde...geben`); modal verbs are a shell made of two completely different verbs — the modal carries the grammar (tense, person, mood), the infinitive carries the meaning and never changes form.

## 2. The six modal verbs and their real meanings

| Verb | Core meaning | Nuance |
|---|---|---|
| können | can / to be able to | ability, possibility |
| müssen | must / to have to | strong obligation, necessity |
| dürfen | may / to be allowed to | permission (negation = prohibition!) |
| sollen | should / to be supposed to | external expectation, advice |
| wollen | to want to | desire, intention |
| möchten | would like to | polite version of wollen |

⚠️ **Common Spanish-speaker mistake #1 — dürfen vs können:** Spanish/English often blur "can" (ability) and "may" (permission). German keeps them separate:
- Ich **kann** schwimmen. (physically able)
- Ich **darf** hier nicht rauchen. (not allowed — permission, not ability)

Critical in negation: **nicht dürfen** = "must not/forbidden," NOT "don't have to" (that's **nicht müssen**):
- Du **musst nicht** kommen. (optional)
- Du **darfst nicht** kommen. (forbidden)

Near-identical sentences, nearly opposite meanings — worth drilling specifically.

⚠️ **Common Spanish-speaker mistake #2 — wollen sounds ruder than intended:** `Ich will einen Kaffee` ("I want a coffee") can land as blunt/childish in a service context. `Ich möchte einen Kaffee, bitte` is the register for daily interactions — ordering, asking for things, politeness in shops. High-frequency real-life fix.

## 3. Progressively harder examples

1. Ich kann heute nicht kommen.
2. Ich muss morgen früh aufstehen, weil ich einen Termin habe. (modal + subordinate clause)
3. Darf ich hier parken, oder ist das verboten? (modal + question inversion)
4. Ich möchte nicht stören, aber ich habe eine Frage. (möchte + nicht + aber)
5. Man **sollte** jeden Tag ein bisschen Deutsch üben, auch wenn man keine Zeit hat. (sollte = softer "should"; "man" = generic "one/you")

## 4. Pronunciation note

Modal verbs are often contracted/de-stressed in fast spoken German — "kannst du" frequently collapses toward "kannste" in casual speech. Train your ear to recognize `müssen`, `können`, etc. often sound "swallowed" compared to textbook pronunciation.

## 5. Speaking exercise

Say these out loud, then answer about yourself:
- Was kannst du schon auf Deutsch machen?
- Was musst du diese Woche erledigen?
- Was möchtest du heute Abend machen?

Then produce the müssen/dürfen minimal pair with your own context:
> Ich muss heute nicht arbeiten. vs. Ich darf heute nicht arbeiten.

## 6. Thinking-in-German habit

Narrate your obligations and desires for the day using modals, silently:
> *Ich muss heute einkaufen gehen. Ich möchte auch ein bisschen Deutsch üben, aber ich habe nicht viel Zeit. Ich kann vielleicht morgen mehr machen.*

Most complex internal-monologue exercise yet — three modals, one nicht, one aber.

## 7. Real-life mission

Use **möchte** (not wollen) at least once today in a real request — ordering, asking a colleague, asking a favor.

## 8. Mini quiz

1. Translate, paying attention to dürfen vs müssen: "You don't have to pay." vs. "You're not allowed to pay."
2. Build a sentence: ich / können / gut / kochen
3. Fix if needed: "Ich will einen Kaffee" in a café, ordering politely — what should you say instead?
4. Combine with weil: "Ich muss früh aufstehen." + "Ich habe einen Flug." (use müssen and weil together)
