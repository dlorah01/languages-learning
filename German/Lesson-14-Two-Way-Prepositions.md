# Lesson 14 — Two-Way Prepositions: The Movement vs. Location Switch

**Phase:** 2 — The Case System
**Status:** ✅ Done

---

## 1. The core idea

Nine prepositions can take either Akkusativ or Dativ, based on one clean logical switch:

**Movement/direction (a change happening) → Akkusativ**
**Location/state (no change) → Dativ**

The nine: **an, auf, hinter, in, neben, über, unter, vor, zwischen**

- Ich lege das Buch **auf den Tisch**. (Akkusativ — the book is moving onto the table)
- Das Buch liegt **auf dem Tisch**. (Dativ — just sitting there, nothing changing)

**Mental model:** ask **"is something moving/changing, or just sitting/existing?"** — even abstract changes of state count as "movement." Akkusativ is the **arrow** (→, a change happening); Dativ is the **dot** (•, fixed in place).

## 2. Why this connects everything

Same "arrow vs. dot" logic as Lesson 4's **wohin** (where to — movement) vs. **wo** (where — location):
- **Wo** ist das Buch? — Es ist **auf dem Tisch**. (location question → dative)
- **Wohin** legst du das Buch? — Ich lege es **auf den Tisch**. (direction question → accusative)

Ask "wo or wohin?" for any sentence with one of these nine prepositions — the single most useful diagnostic trick here.

## 3. The nine prepositions, with typical pairs

| Preposition | Meaning | Location (Dativ) | Movement (Akkusativ) |
|---|---|---|---|
| in | in/into | Ich bin **im** Büro. (in dem → im) | Ich gehe **ins** Büro. (in das → ins) |
| an | at/on (vertical) | Das Bild hängt **an der** Wand. | Ich hänge das Bild **an die** Wand. |
| auf | on (horizontal) | Das Buch liegt **auf dem** Tisch. | Ich lege das Buch **auf den** Tisch. |
| über | over/above | Die Lampe hängt **über dem** Tisch. | Ich hänge die Lampe **über den** Tisch. |
| unter | under | Die Katze schläft **unter dem** Bett. | Die Katze läuft **unter das** Bett. |
| vor | in front of | Das Auto steht **vor dem** Haus. | Ich fahre das Auto **vor das** Haus. |
| hinter | behind | Der Garten ist **hinter dem** Haus. | Ich gehe **hinter das** Haus. |
| neben | next to | Der Stuhl steht **neben dem** Tisch. | Ich stelle den Stuhl **neben den** Tisch. |
| zwischen | between | Das Buch liegt **zwischen den** Büchern. | Ich lege das Buch **zwischen die** Bücher. |

`in` + `das` → `ins`; `in` + `dem` → `im`. Common in speech and worth using actively — "in dem Büro" instead of "im Büro" sounds noticeably stiff.

⚠️ **Common Spanish-speaker mistake:** Spanish "en" flattens this distinction entirely — "el libro está en la mesa" (location) and "pongo el libro en la mesa" (movement) both use "en." One of the areas where Spanish offers the least help — consciously apply the wo/wohin test until it's automatic.

## 4. The abstract-verb trap

Once comfortable with physical movement/location, the harder step is recognizing "movement" in abstract contexts:
- Ich denke **an dich**. (I think about/of you — accusative! Not physical, but conceptually "directed toward" you)
- Many verb+preposition combos are simply fixed and must be memorized as chunks, not derived from wo/wohin logic — "denken an," "warten auf," "sich freuen auf" are fixed idiomatic pairings.

Not every case decision reduces cleanly to wo/wohin — some two-way prepositions get "frozen" into a fixed case by a verb's idiom (warten **auf** + Akkusativ, always). This idiom list builds steadily across future lessons; for now, focus on the clean physical cases.

## 5. Progressively harder examples

1. Ich stelle die Tasche neben den Stuhl. (movement, accusative)
2. Die Tasche steht neben dem Stuhl. (location, dative)
3. Ich gehe heute nicht ins Büro, weil ich krank bin. (ins = in+das, movement + nicht + weil)
4. Kannst du das Bild über das Sofa hängen? (question + modal + movement accusative)
5. Ich denke oft an meine Familie in Kolumbien. (abstract "movement" usage of an + accusative)

## 6. Speaking exercise

Decide Akkusativ or Dativ using the wo/wohin test, out loud:
- Ich lege den Schlüssel ___ Tisch. (auf den — movement)
- Der Schlüssel liegt ___ Tisch. (auf dem — location)
- Ich gehe ___ Supermarkt. (in den — movement)
- Ich bin ___ Supermarkt. (im — location)

Then narrate where three objects around you are right now (dative/location), then describe moving one of them (accusative/movement).

## 7. Thinking-in-German habit

Narrate a short physical action using at least one movement and one location two-way preposition:
> *Ich stelle meine Tasche neben die Tür. Meine Schlüssel liegen auf dem Tisch.*

Especially good run in real time as you move around your apartment or office.

## 8. Real-life mission

Physically point to or move an object today while narrating it out loud with a two-way preposition — "Ich lege das auf den Tisch," "Es liegt im Schrank."

## 9. Mini quiz

1. Wo or wohin, and which case: "Die Kinder spielen ___ Garten." (im — dative, static)
2. Wo or wohin, and which case: "Die Kinder laufen ___ Garten." (in den — accusative, movement)
3. Contract correctly: "in dem" → ? / "in das" → ?
4. Fix if needed: "Ich denke an dir oft." (check the case)
5. Build a sentence: Buch / liegen / zwischen / die Zeitschriften (location, dative)
