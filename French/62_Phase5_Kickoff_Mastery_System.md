# Lesson 62 — Phase 5 Kickoff: Designing Your Personal Mastery System
### Phase 5: Mastery & Maintenance (C1 → C2+)

**Competency axes trained:** All eight axes (planning and habit design)
**Prerequisite:** Lesson 61 (Phase 4 capstone and readiness check)
**Time investment:** One read, then 45 minutes to build your system on paper. Revisit monthly.

---

## 1. What Changes in Phase 5 (Intuitive Explanation)

Up to now, a curriculum told you what to learn next. From here on, the biggest gains come from **volume, variety, and feedback**, not from new grammar rules. At C1 you already know almost all the grammar. What separates C1 from C2 (and from native-like intuition) is:

- **Breadth:** many registers, topics, accents, and text types.
- **Automaticity:** speed and ease, not just accuracy.
- **Precision:** the exact word, the right nuance, the natural collocation.
- **Resilience:** maintaining the skill without a course, for years.

So Phase 5 lessons shift from "learn this rule" to "build this capability and this habit." This first lesson designs the engine that will keep running when the lessons end.

---

## 2. The Plateau Problem (and Why It Happens)

Most learners stall around B2/C1 because:

1. **They can already communicate**, so pressure to improve disappears.
2. **Errors become invisible** to them (fossilization): nobody corrects fluent-sounding mistakes.
3. **Progress becomes slow and non-linear**, which feels like failure.
4. **Practice becomes comfortable repetition** of already-known language.

The cure is **deliberate practice at the edge of your ability**, with **feedback**, in **varied contexts**. Your system must build all three in.

---

## 3. The Eight-Axis Weekly Template

Return to the eight competency axes from your roadmap. A balanced week touches all of them, in small doses.

| Axis | Weekly minimum | Examples of activities |
|---|---|---|
| 🔊 Phonology | 10 min × 3 | shadowing, recording, minimal pairs |
| 🧩 Morphosyntax | 20 min × 1 | fix your recurring errors, write and correct sentences |
| 📚 Lexicon | 15 min × 4 | mining log review, flashcards with spaced repetition |
| 🎭 Pragmatics/Register | 20 min × 1 | role-plays, rewrite messages in other registers |
| 🔗 Discourse | 20 min × 1 | structured opinion or explanation, 2-minute talks |
| 👂 Listening | 30 min × 4 | podcasts, series, radio (active and passive) |
| 🗣️ Productive fluency | 30 min × 2 | conversation, voice notes, self-recording |
| 📖 Reading | 20 min × 5 | novel, press, essays |
| 🌍 Cultural/literary | 30 min × 1 | film, music, history, a classic text |

**Total: about 5–6 hours a week**, much of it enjoyable (listening, reading, culture). A 20-minute daily minimum keeps the habit alive on busy days.

---

## 4. The Three Loops of a Mastery System

### Loop 1: Input (daily)
Reading, listening, watching. Volume and enjoyment, at 70–90% comprehension (Lessons 55, 56).

### Loop 2: Output (several times a week)
Speaking and writing. Produce, record, and revise. **Output reveals gaps that input hides.**

### Loop 3: Feedback and Repair (weekly)
Find your errors, understand them, and fix them. Without this loop, output only reinforces mistakes.

**Weekly cycle:** *consume → mine → produce → get feedback → review → repeat.*

---

## 5. Getting Feedback: The Most Underrated Piece

| Source | Best for | Cost/effort | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tutor (e.g., italki, Preply, Alliance Française)** | grammar errors, pronunciation, structured correction | paid, 1–2 sessions/week | Ask for *corrections after the session*, not constant interruption |
| **Language exchange partner (Tandem, HelloTalk, local meetups)** | real conversation, cultural exchange | free | You will teach Spanish in return |
| **Writing correction (Lang-8-style communities, tutors, native friends, or AI tools)** | accuracy, style | free to cheap | Always request an explanation, not only a corrected text |
| **Self-recording** | rhythm, vocabulary gaps, fluency | free | Compare your recordings month to month |
| **Native-speaker friends/colleagues** | register, naturalness | free | Ask: "How would you say it?" instead of "Is this correct?" |

**Tip for tutors:** do not ask for "conversation practice" only. Give them a **specific mission**: "Correct my subjunctive," "Interview me as for a job," "Role-play a difficult phone call."

## 6. Your Personal Error Journal

Create a one-page document with four columns:

| What I said/wrote | What a native would say | Rule or reason | Count |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Je suis d'accord avec ça* (fine) / *Je suis accord* (error) | *Je suis d'accord* | fixed expression | 3 |
| *J'ai peur de que...* | *J'ai peur que...* | no *de* before *que* | 2 |

**Method:** log every error you notice or that someone corrects. After a month, **count recurrences**: your top five repeated errors are your highest-value targets. Fix them with 10 targeted sentences each, spaced over days 1, 3, 7, and 21.

---

## 7. Tracking Progress Without Fooling Yourself

Subjective feelings of progress are unreliable at C1. Use **concrete, repeatable measures**:

| Measure | How | Frequency |
|---|---|---|
| **Speaking sample** | record 2 minutes on the same prompt | monthly |
| **Listening test** | a 3-minute authentic clip: % comprehension | monthly |
| **Reading speed** | words per minute on a page of a novel | every 2 months |
| **Vocabulary** | number of mining-log items retained at day 21 | monthly |
| **Writing sample** | a 150-word text on the same prompt | every 2 months |
| **External check** | an official test (DELF B2/C1, DALF C1/C2, TCF) | optional, annually |

**Official certification:** the **DELF** (B1/B2) and **DALF** (C1/C2) diplomas are recognized worldwide and permanent. If you plan to study, work, or migrate to a French-speaking country, they are valuable. The **TCF** and **TEF** are score-based tests used for immigration (e.g., Canada/Québec) and some universities.

## 8. Habit Design: Making It Stick for Years

Use these evidence-based principles:

1. **Anchor to an existing routine:** podcasts on your commute, reading before bed, shadowing while brewing coffee.
2. **Lower the activation energy:** keep the book on the table, the podcast app on the home screen, the recorder one tap away.
3. **Minimum viable day:** on bad days, do the 10-minute version (one podcast segment or five pages), never zero. **Never miss twice.**
4. **Variety to avoid boredom:** rotate topics and formats. Follow your curiosity (cooking, football, tech, history).
5. **Make it social:** a weekly call, a book club, or a group chat in French turns obligation into pleasure.
6. **Reward and visibility:** keep a visible calendar or streak, but never punish yourself for gaps.
7. **Review quarterly:** every three months, re-run Section 7 and adjust your plan.

---

## 9. Immersion at Home: Turning Your Life Into French

At this stage, **changing your environment** beats willpower:

- Set your phone, computer, and apps to French.
- Follow French accounts on topics you already love (your field, hobbies, sports, news).
- Switch your music and playlists to French artists part of the time.
- Narrate your own life in French (inner monologue, voice notes, journal).
- Use French for **real tasks**: recipes, manuals, tutorials, product reviews, forums in your field.
- Cook, exercise, or commute with French audio.
- Write your **to-do lists and notes** in French.

**Rule:** French should become a **tool for living**, not only a subject you study.

---

## 10. Mental Model: Maintaining a Garden, Not Finishing a Building

Until now the program was like building a house: foundation, walls, roof. Phase 5 is a **garden**. It needs regular watering (daily input), pruning (error correction), seasonal planting (new topics and registers), and attention to what is wilting (skills you stopped using). A garden is never "done," but a little consistent care keeps it thriving for decades.

---

## 11. Common Mistakes

**Spanish speakers:** staying inside a comfortable "Spanish-French" bubble (speaking only with Spanish-speaking learners), relying on cognates and never expanding to unfamiliar vocabulary, avoiding speaking when perfectionism strikes.

**English speakers:** over-relying on English for explanations, favoring reading over speaking, not seeking correction.

**Both:** an overly ambitious plan that collapses in two weeks; consuming only learner content; never recording yourself; skipping the feedback loop; and treating a plateau as the end rather than a normal stage.

## 13. Speaking Practice

Record a **2-minute talk** in French answering: *Quels sont mes objectifs avec le français, pourquoi sont-ils importants pour moi, et comment vais-je organiser ma pratique ?* Keep this recording. In three months, answer the same question again and compare.
