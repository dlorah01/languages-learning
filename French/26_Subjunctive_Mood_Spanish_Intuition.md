# Lesson 26 — The Subjunctive Mood: Leveraging Your Spanish Intuition
### Phase 2: Expansion (A2 → B1)

**Competency axes trained:** 🧩 Morphosyntax (primary) · 🗣️ Productive fluency
**Prerequisite:** Phase 1 complete, especially Lesson 6 (conjugation patterns)
**Time investment:** One thorough read + this is a mood that clicks over months of exposure — revisit often

---

## 1. Why This Opens Phase 2, and Why You Have a Head Start (Intuitive Explanation)

The subjunctive terrifies English-only learners because English barely has one (a few fossilized traces like "if I were you"). You, however, have a **fully functioning subjunctive mood in Spanish** — you already know, instinctively, that "quiero que vengas" requires "vengas," not "vienes." This lesson's entire job is mapping that existing instinct onto French forms and triggers.

---

## 2. The Core Concept: Subjectivity, Not Just Grammar

The subjunctive marks a verb as **subjective** — expressing doubt, desire, emotion, necessity, or uncertainty — rather than stating an objective fact (indicative mood, everything you've learned so far). Compare:

> **Je sais qu'il vient.** — "I know he's coming." (indicative — objective fact)
> **Je veux qu'il vienne.** — "I want him to come." (subjunctive — subjective desire, outcome not guaranteed)

This is precisely the same distinction Spanish makes between "sé que viene" and "quiero que venga."

---

## 3. Formation: Present Subjunctive (regular pattern)

**Formula:** take the **ils/elles** form of the present indicative, drop **-ent**, add subjunctive endings:

| Person | Ending |
|---|---|
| que je | -e |
| que tu | -es |
| qu'il/elle | -e |
| que nous | -ions |
| que vous | -iez |
| qu'ils/elles | -ent |

**Example — parler:** ils parlent → parl- + e/es/e/ions/iez/ent → que je parle, que tu parles, qu'il parle, que nous parlions, que vous parliez, qu'ils parlent

Notice: **je/tu/il/ils forms sound identical to the present indicative** for -ER verbs (another instance of Lesson 6's "endings are often silent" phenomenon) — but **nous/vous forms borrow the imparfait's -ions/-iez endings** (Lesson 13 callback), making them the easiest forms to actually hear as distinctly subjunctive.

---

## 4. Irregular Subjunctive Stems (memorize — high frequency)

| Verb | Subjunctive stem |
|---|---|
| être | que je **sois**, que nous **soyons** (fully irregular) |
| avoir | que j'**aie**, que nous **ayons** (fully irregular) |
| faire | que je **fasse** (irregular stem, regular endings) |
| aller | que j'**aille**, que nous **allions** (stem changes for je/tu/il/ils, regular for nous/vous) |
| pouvoir | que je **puisse** |
| savoir | que je **sache** |
| vouloir | que je **veuille**, que nous **voulions** |

---

## 5. The Trigger List: What Requires the Subjunctive

Just as in Spanish, the subjunctive is triggered by the **main clause**, not chosen freely. Group the triggers conceptually (mirroring Spanish WEIRDO-style categories):

| Category | French trigger examples | Spanish parallel |
|---|---|---|
| Wish/desire | vouloir que, souhaiter que | querer que |
| Emotion | être content que, avoir peur que, regretter que | alegrarse de que, tener miedo de que |
| Doubt/uncertainty | douter que, ne pas être sûr que | dudar que, no estar seguro de que |
| Necessity | il faut que, il est nécessaire que | es necesario que |
| Opinion (negative/questioned) | je ne pense pas que, penses-tu que...? | no creo que, ¿crees que...? |
| Certain conjunctions | avant que, bien que, pour que, à moins que | antes de que, aunque, para que, a menos que |

**Key nuance, genuinely different from Spanish:** French **"je pense que"** (affirmative "I think that") takes the **indicative** ("je pense qu'il vient"), while the **negative** "je ne pense pas que" typically triggers the subjunctive ("je ne pense pas qu'il vienne") — Spanish behaves similarly here (creo que + indicative, no creo que + subjunctive), so this is actually another clean transfer, just worth stating explicitly.

---

## 6. Mental Model: The Subjunctive as "The Language of the Not-Yet-Real"

Picture the indicative as the mood for describing the world **as it factually is**, and the subjunctive as the mood for describing the world **as someone wishes, fears, doubts, or requires it to be** — a state that exists only in someone's mind, not yet (or not certainly) in reality. "Il faut qu'il vienne" doesn't confirm he's coming — it expresses a requirement about a still-uncertain future. This is exactly the mental space Spanish subjunctive already occupies for you.

---

## 7. Common Mistakes

**Spanish speakers:** genuinely minimal risk on the *concept* — your main task is learning French-specific trigger phrases and irregular stems, not relearning the underlying logic. Watch for exact trigger-phrase boundaries that don't map 1:1 (a small set of borderline verbs where French and Spanish diverge slightly on indicative vs. subjunctive — these get refined with exposure, not memorized upfront).

**English speakers:** since English lacks a real productive subjunctive, English speakers often try to avoid the construction entirely or default to the indicative — missing the subjunctive here is a very noticeable, persistent error.

---

## 8. Progressively Harder Examples

1. Il faut que tu partes maintenant. — "You have to leave now."
2. Je suis content que tu sois là. — "I'm happy you're here."
3. Je ne pense pas qu'il ait raison. — "I don't think he's right."
4. Bien qu'il pleuve, nous allons sortir. — "Even though it's raining, we're going out."
5. Je veux que vous fassiez attention avant que ce ne soit trop tard. — "I want you to be careful before it's too late." (two subjunctive triggers stacked)
