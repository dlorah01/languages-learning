# Lesson 83 — Deep Dive 3: Test Preparation — DELF B2, DALF C1/C2, and TCF
### Phase 5: Mastery & Maintenance — Optional Deep Dive

**Competency axes trained:** All eight axes, through exam-oriented practice
**Prerequisite:** Lesson 79 (final capstone), Lesson 64 (essay writing), Lesson 68 (spoken interaction), Lesson 74 (news French)
**Time investment:** One read, then an 8–12 week preparation cycle if you decide to take an exam.

> **Important:** exam formats, durations, fees, registration procedures, and score requirements are set by the official bodies and **change from time to time**. The details below are an orientation based on commonly published formats. **Always confirm the current rules and download the official sample papers** from the official sites (France Éducation international for DELF/DALF/TCF; the Chamber of Commerce and Industry for TEF; your national or provincial authority if the exam is for immigration or study).

---

## 1. Do You Need an Exam? (Intuitive Explanation)

You do not need a certificate to speak French well. But an exam can be valuable if you want:

- **Official proof of level** for study, work, or immigration.
- **A concrete target** that organizes your practice and gives a deadline.
- **External, objective feedback** (not just your own impression).
- **Motivation and structure** during the maintenance phase.

If none of these apply, you can skip this lesson or use it only as a source of exam-style practice.

## 2. The Main Exams Compared

| Exam | Who issues it | Type | Levels | Validity | Typical use |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **DELF** | French Ministry of Education (via France Éducation international) | Diploma, taken level by level | A1, A2, B1, B2 | **Lifetime** | study, work, general proof |
| **DALF** | same | Diploma | C1, C2 | **Lifetime** | university admissions, academic careers, high-level proof |
| **TCF** | France Éducation international | Standardized test with a score and level report (not a diploma) | A1–C2 (one test gives a level) | **2 years** | immigration, university applications |
| **TEF** | CCI Paris Île-de-France | Standardized test with a score | A1–C2 | **2 years** | immigration (e.g., Canada, France), admissions |
| **TCF Canada / TEF Canada / TCF Québec / TEFAQ** | same bodies (variants) | Tests adapted to Canadian or Québec requirements | – | usually 2 years | Canadian immigration or Québec procedures |

**DELF/DALF vs. TCF/TEF:**
- **DELF/DALF:** you pick **one level** and pass or fail it. Each level is a separate diploma. Good for demonstrating a specific level permanently.
- **TCF/TEF:** you take **one test** and receive a level (A1 to C2) and a score. Good when an institution requires a recent score.

**Which one to choose:** check the **requirements of the institution or authority** (university, employer, immigration office). Some accept only specific exams and a minimum score or level.

## 3. DELF/DALF Structure (Overview)

All DELF and DALF exams test **four skills**:

| Code | Skill (French) | English |
|---|---|---|
| **CO** | Compréhension de l'oral | Listening |
| **CE** | Compréhension des écrits | Reading |
| **PE** | Production écrite | Writing |
| **PO** | Production orale | Speaking |

### Scoring
- Each skill is typically scored out of **25 points**, total **100**.
- To pass, you generally need **at least 50/100** overall and a **minimum of about 5/25 in each skill** (verify the current rule).
- **Both dimensions matter:** a very strong skill cannot fully compensate for a very weak one.

### Approximate task types
> Durations and exact tasks vary by exam version; treat these as indicative.

| Exam | CO | CE | PE | PO |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **DELF B2** | 2–3 recordings (interviews, bulletins, reports); multiple-choice and short answers; ~30 min | 2 texts (analysis of a long text and a text of opinion); ~60 min | **Argumentative essay** (~250 words) based on a topic/letter/article; ~60 min | **Monologue and debate**: present and defend a point of view on a text-based topic after ~30 min preparation; ~20 min |
| **DALF C1** | documents of a longer, more complex type; ~40 min | long, complex text with analytical questions; ~50 min | **Synthesis of documents** and **argued essay**; long written session (~2 h 30) | **Presentation (exposé)** based on a document and **discussion** with the jury, after ~1 h preparation; ~30 min |
| **DALF C2** | longer, denser audio documents; ~1 h | **Reading and writing combined** in one long session (~3 h 30): synthesis and a personal text based on documents | (combined above) | **Oral from a topic/quote**, prepared for ~1 h; ~30 min presentation and discussion |

**Typical competencies assessed:**
- **Listening/reading:** gist, detail, implicit meaning, author's attitude, structure of arguments.
- **Writing:** compliance with the task, coherence and cohesion, lexical range and accuracy, grammatical control, sociolinguistic adequacy (register).
- **Speaking:** ability to present, argue, interact, correct yourself, and maintain a natural flow; lexical and grammatical range; phonology.

## 4. TCF/TEF Structure (Overview)

### TCF (general version, indicative)
| Section | Format | Duration (approx.) |
|---|---|---|
| **Compréhension orale** | multiple-choice, 29 questions | 25 min |
| **Maîtrise des structures de la langue** | multiple-choice, 18 questions | 15 min |
| **Compréhension écrite** | multiple-choice, 29 questions | 45 min |
| **Expression écrite** (often required for admissions/immigration) | 3 tasks | 60 min |
| **Expression orale** (often required) | 3 tasks, recorded or with an examiner | 12 min |

The multiple-choice sections give a score and a CEFR level. Writing and speaking are scored separately.

### TEF (general version, indicative)
Sections: reading, listening, vocabulary and grammar (in some versions), writing, speaking. Check the current format on the official TEF site, as the structure differs between TEF, TEF Canada, and TEFAQ.

**Key difference from DELF/DALF:** multiple-choice sections are **speeded** and **adaptive in difficulty**. Speed of comprehension and exam technique matter more.

## 5. How to Choose a Level to Aim For

Use your Lesson 79 profile and the following guidance.

| Profile (capstone total) | Reasonable target |
|---|---|
| 16–21 (B2) | **DELF B2** (or TCF reaching B2) |
| 22–27 (B2+/C1) | **DALF C1** after targeted preparation |
| 28–32 (C1+) | **DALF C1** with confidence; **DALF C2** is a major step requiring extensive reading/writing at an academic level |

**C2 is not just "more C1."** DALF C2 expects academic-level synthesis, sophisticated argumentation, a wide range of registers, and command of subtle shades of meaning. Most educated native speakers would find it demanding. Consider C1 first unless you have a strong reason.

**Always take a mock test** (past papers, official samples) under real timing before registering, to calibrate.

## 6. A 10-Week Preparation Plan (Adaptable to B2 or C1)

Assume 6–8 hours per week; adjust to your timetable. Scale up if you have a deadline.

| Week | Focus | Activities |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | Diagnose | take a full past paper under timed conditions; score yourself or have it scored; list the weakest skill; read the official grid |
| **2** | Listening + reading skills | practice with past CO/CE tasks; learn question types; train note-taking and skimming |
| **3** | Writing structure | learn the essay structure for your level (Section 7); write one essay and get corrections |
| **4** | Speaking structure | learn the exposé/monologue structure; record two presentations; self-evaluate with the grid |
| **5** | Vocabulary and connectors | build themed lexicons (education, environment, technology, work, health, culture, society); drill connectors (Lesson 64) |
| **6** | Grammar clinic | target recurring errors from your journal: tenses, subjunctive, participles, prepositions (Lessons 58, 70) |
| **7** | Mock exam 1 | full timed exam; review in detail; update your plan |
| **8** | Targeted repair | work on the two weakest tasks; more feedback from a tutor |
| **9** | Mock exam 2 | full timed exam under realistic conditions; focus on timing and stress management |
| **10** | Taper and polish | light review; practice oral with a partner; prepare materials; sleep and logistics |

## 7. Skill-by-Skill Strategies

### 7.1 Listening (CO)
- **Read the questions first** during the preview time; predict what you will hear.
- **Take notes of key words** (names, numbers, opinions), not sentences.
- **Listen for markers:** contrast (*mais, pourtant, cependant*), cause (*car, parce que*), conclusion (*donc, en somme*), speaker attitude (*malheureusement, heureusement, il semblerait que*).
- **Don't dwell on a missed answer;** use the second listening for verification.
- **Train with authentic material:** *franceinfo*, RFI, *France Culture*, Radio Canada (Lessons 56, 74).
- **Practice with variation:** accents from France, Québec, Belgium, Switzerland, Africa (Lesson 63).

### 7.2 Reading (CE)
- **Skim first** for structure (title, subheadings, first and last sentences), then read for details.
- **Identify the genre** and the author's stance (Lesson 74).
- **Questions about implicit meaning:** look for tone, irony, concessions, and attribution verbs.
- **Vocabulary in context:** infer from roots, word families, and connectors.
- **Time management:** allocate minutes per text; mark and return to difficult questions.
- **Practice paraphrasing** answers; wording on the page rarely matches the question exactly.

### 7.3 Writing (PE)

**B2: Argumentative essay (~250 words) or formal letter**
1. **Understand the prompt:** identify the thesis to discuss or the type of letter.
2. **Plan in 5 minutes:** 2–3 arguments with examples, a counter-argument, a conclusion.
3. **Structure:** introduction (context + position), body (2–3 paragraphs: argument + explanation + example), conclusion (summary + opening).
4. **Use connectors** (Lesson 64): *D'abord, de plus, en outre, cependant, en revanche, certes… mais, par conséquent, en conclusion.*
5. **Respect the register** and word limit (usually ±10%).
6. **Revise** (Lesson 70): verbs and subjects, participle agreement, homophones, accents, punctuation.

**C1/C2: Synthesis and argued essay**
- **Synthesis of documents:** summarize the main ideas from several documents **without adding your opinion**, in a **new, coherent structure** (not document by document), using your own words. Group by themes, show relationships (agreement, contrast).
  - Typical formulas: *Les documents s'accordent sur…, Le premier document met en avant…, tandis que le second souligne…, Il ressort de l'ensemble que…*
- **Argued essay:** a developed position with nuanced arguments, counter-arguments, examples, and a controlled style (concession, nominalization, rhetorical devices, Lessons 64–65).
- **Quality markers:** precision, variety, subtlety, accurate register, cohesion, avoidance of repetition.

### 7.4 Speaking (PO)

**B2 monologue + debate**
1. **Preparation time:** read the text; underline the key issue; plan 3 points (position, arguments, example); write key words, not sentences.
2. **Structure:** introduction (presentation of the text and the issue), development (your position with 2–3 arguments), conclusion.
3. **Debate:** react to the examiner's questions, defend your view politely, use concession and clarification phrases (Lesson 66).
4. **Useful formulas:** *Le texte aborde la question de…, À mon avis…, Certes… mais…, Je ne partage pas tout à fait cet avis car…, Si je comprends bien votre question…*

**C1/C2 exposé + discussion**
- **Structure the exposé** (introduction with *problématique*, plan in two or three parts, conclusion). Respect the timing (usually 10 minutes for the C1/C2 exposé; check exact time).
- **Use documents** as springboards, not as scripts.
- **Show nuance:** concessions, shades of opinion, relevant examples, cultural references (Lesson 69).
- **Discussion:** be responsive, accept challenges, rephrase, ask for clarification, maintain a calm, natural flow.

**General oral tips:**
- **Speak slowly enough** to be clear; prioritize rhythm and group-final intonation (Lesson 59).
- **Use fillers and repair phrases** naturally (Lessons 41, 68): *comment dire…, disons que…, enfin, autrement dit…*
- **Never stay silent;** use circumlocution (Lesson 71).
- **Maintain eye contact** and a courteous manner.

### 7.5 TCF/TEF multiple-choice tips
- **Time per question** is short: answer quickly and move on.
- **Eliminate** obviously wrong choices.
- **For grammar/structure:** rely on instinct from reading volume, plus quick checks (agreement, tense, preposition).
- **For listening:** the first answer you hear in a distractor can be a trap; wait for the speaker's conclusion.
- **Never leave blanks** (check whether wrong answers are penalized; in TCF they are not).

## 8. Exam-Day Logistics and Mindset

**Before:**
- Register early; confirm the center, date, and required documents (valid ID).
- Check the center's rules: pens, dictionaries (generally not allowed), phones.
- Do a **final mock** a week before; avoid heavy cramming the night before.
- Plan transport and arrive early.

**During:**
- Read instructions carefully; manage time with a watch.
- For writing: **plan first**, then write; keep 5–10 minutes to proofread.
- For speaking: take a moment to breathe; smile; speak clearly.

**After:**
- Results and diplomas arrive after some weeks (DELF/DALF) or sooner (TCF/TEF, depending on the center). If you don't pass a skill threshold, you can retake the exam.

**Mindset:** treat the exam as a performance with rules. Even native speakers need to prepare for the format.

## 9. Exam-Style Practice Prompts (Original, for Self-Practice)

> These are original prompts modeled on typical exam tasks; for official samples, use the official websites.

### B2 argumentative essay (250 words, 55 minutes)
*Dans un article, un journaliste affirme que les réseaux sociaux nuisent à la qualité des relations humaines. Vous êtes en désaccord. Vous rédigez une lettre à la rédaction pour exprimer votre point de vue en vous appuyant sur des arguments et des exemples.*

### B2 oral monologue (after 30 min preparation, 5–7 minutes)
*Texte (à choisir) : un article sur l'éducation en ligne. Présentez le thème, donnez votre opinion et répondez aux questions de l'examinateur : « L'école du futur sera-t-elle entièrement numérique ? »*

### C1 synthesis (about 200 words)
*À partir de trois courts documents (que vous choisissez) sur l'impact du télétravail, rédigez une synthèse structurée en 200 mots maximum, sans opinion personnelle.*

### C1 argued essay (about 250–300 words)
*« La liberté d'expression doit-elle avoir des limites ? » Rédigez un essai argumenté, avec une introduction, un développement structuré et une conclusion.*

### C1/C2 oral exposé
*À partir d'un court extrait sur le rôle des langues minoritaires dans la culture, préparez un exposé de 10 minutes avec une problématique claire, un plan en deux parties, et préparez-vous à débattre.*

### Quick listening drill
*Chaque jour pendant deux semaines : écoutez un bulletin franceinfo de 5 minutes ; prenez des notes ; résumez en 6 phrases ; vérifiez avec la transcription.*

## 10. Evaluating Yourself: Writing and Speaking Checklists

### Writing checklist (adapt to B2/C1)
- [ ] I answered exactly the task (type of text, position, length).
- [ ] The plan is visible: introduction, 2–3 clear arguments, conclusion.
- [ ] Each paragraph has one idea, an explanation, and an example.
- [ ] I used varied connectors accurately.
- [ ] My vocabulary is precise and varied (no repeated weak verbs).
- [ ] Agreement, tense, mood (subjunctive), prepositions, and accents are accurate.
- [ ] The register is appropriate (formal letter vs. essay).
- [ ] I respected the word limit.

### Speaking checklist
- [ ] Clear introduction of the topic and my position.
- [ ] Structured development with signposts.
- [ ] Natural rhythm; few long pauses; effective repair.
- [ ] Varied vocabulary and structures, including subjunctive and concession.
- [ ] Clear pronunciation and intonation.
- [ ] I interacted well in the discussion (listened, rephrased, defended politely).

**Get external eyes:** a tutor, an exam coach, or a Francophone friend with a rubric. Many exam centers and language schools offer mock oral exams and essay corrections.

## 11. Mental Model: Exams as a Game With Known Rules

An exam is a game with a **published rulebook** (the grid) and **recurring levels** (task types). You already have the skills; the game rewards those who **know the rules and manage the clock**. Preparation has two layers: **skill** (everything in Lessons 1–82) and **game technique** (formats, timing, structures, scoring criteria). A great player who doesn't know the rules can lose; a good player who knows them well can play up to their ability. Train both.

## 12. Common Mistakes

**Spanish speakers:**
- Writing long, loosely connected sentences; use shorter, controlled structures with connectors.
- Cognates and false friends (*actuellement, éventuellement, assister*) in essays.
- Missing French essay conventions: the *problématique*, the plan, and the *ouverture*.
- Overusing gerunds and calques.
- Speaking too fast with Spanish rhythm; slow down and group in rhythmic units.

**English speakers:**
- Too informal or too direct a tone; noun stacks; missing connectors.
- Overusing the passive or "I think" structures.
- Ignoring participle agreement and homophones in written tasks.

**Both:**
- Writing off-topic or ignoring word limits.
- Summarizing document by document in the synthesis instead of by themes.
- Adding personal opinions to a synthesis.
- Running out of time because of lack of timed practice.
- Memorizing templates so rigid that they sound artificial.
- Skipping the oral practice until the last week.

## 13. Progressively Harder Practice (Beyond the Plan)

1. **Past papers weekly:** one full skill per week under timed conditions.
2. **Dictée and shadowing** to build listening-to-writing accuracy.
3. **Essay bank:** write five essays on standard exam topics (education, environment, technology, work, culture, health, society) and compare with model answers.
4. **Synthesis drills:** write a 150-word synthesis of three short articles weekly.
5. **Oral simulation:** record a 10-minute exposé and 5-minute debate with a partner; evaluate with the grid.
6. **Vocabulary themes:** build 7 themed sets (30–40 items each) with collocations and example sentences.
7. **Mock exam day:** schedule a full simulated exam with an exam-like environment.
8. **Review loops:** log errors by skill; revisit on days 2, 7, and 21.
