# Lesson 23 — Reflexive Verbs: Daily Routines and Se Faire/Se Mettre
### Phase 1: Core System (A1 → A2)

**Competency axes trained:** 🧩 Morphosyntax (primary) · 📚 Lexicon (secondary)
**Prerequisite:** Lesson 3 (pronoun placement), Lesson 12 (passé composé — reflexives always use être)
**Time investment:** One thorough read + daily routine narration practice

---

## 1. Why Reflexives Feel Familiar (Intuitive Explanation)

Good news: **you already have this entire grammatical category installed from Spanish.** Reflexive verbs — where the subject acts on itself (se laver = "to wash oneself," compare Spanish lavarse) — work almost identically in French and Spanish, both structurally and semantically. This lesson is mostly about learning French forms for a concept you already fully control.

---

## 2. The Structure: Reflexive Pronoun + Verb

| Person | Reflexive pronoun | Example (se laver) |
|---|---|---|
| je | me | je me lave |
| tu | te | tu te laves |
| il/elle/on | se | il se lave |
| nous | nous | nous nous lavons |
| vous | vous | vous vous lavez |
| ils/elles | se | ils se lavent |

Notice the reflexive pronoun sits in the exact "moon" position from Lesson 3's mental model — glued directly before the verb, just like object pronouns.

---

## 3. High-Frequency Daily-Routine Reflexives

| French | English |
|---|---|
| se réveiller | to wake up |
| se lever | to get up |
| se laver | to wash (oneself) |
| s'habiller | to get dressed |
| se coucher | to go to bed |
| se doucher | to shower |
| se brosser (les dents) | to brush (one's teeth) |
| se reposer | to rest |

**Direct Spanish parallel:** despertarse, levantarse, lavarse, vestirse, acostarse — nearly a 1:1 conceptual and structural map. This is one of the cleanest transfers in the whole program.

---

## 4. Negation and Questions With Reflexives (Lesson 3 + 9 applied)

Negation wraps around the whole reflexive-pronoun-plus-verb cluster:
> **Je ne me lève pas tôt.** — "I don't get up early."

Questions work as expected:
> **Est-ce que tu te lèves tôt?** — "Do you get up early?"

---

## 5. Reflexives in the Passé Composé: Always Être (Lesson 12 callback)

**Critical rule:** all reflexive verbs use **être** as their auxiliary in compound tenses — no exceptions, unlike the memorized être-verb list from Lesson 12 (aller, venir, etc.), reflexives are automatically être-verbs simply by being reflexive.

> **Elle s'est réveillée à sept heures.** — "She woke up at 7." (feminine agreement, since être-verb — Lesson 12's agreement rule applies)
> **Ils se sont couchés tard.** — "They went to bed late." (plural agreement)

**Word order note:** the reflexive pronoun stays before the auxiliary "être," not before the past participle: se + est + réveillée, not est + se + réveillée.

---

## 6. Se Faire and Se Mettre: Reflexive Extensions of Lesson 17's Verbs

**Se mettre à + infinitive** = "to start [doing something]" (previewed in Lesson 17):
> **Il s'est mis à pleurer.** — "He started crying."

**Se faire** has several idiomatic uses:
> **se faire des amis** — "to make friends" (reflexive "faire," not just "faire des amis" — the reflexive adds a sense of "for oneself")
> **se faire mal** — "to hurt oneself"
> **se faire à quelque chose** — "to get used to something"

---

## 7. Mental Model: The Verb Looking in a Mirror

Picture a reflexive verb as an ordinary verb standing in front of a mirror: the reflexive pronoun (me/te/se...) is the mirror reflecting the action back onto the subject itself. "Laver" (to wash [something/someone else]) becomes "se laver" (to wash oneself) the moment you place that mirror-pronoun in front of it. This is exactly the same mental image Spanish "lavarse" already encodes for you — you're just learning the French mirror-pronoun set.

---

## 8. Common Mistakes

**Spanish speakers:** minimal risk here — mostly just building fluency with the specific French pronoun forms (me/te/se vs. Spanish me/te/se — actually nearly identical!) and the position rules already learned in Lesson 3.

**English speakers:** forgetting to include the reflexive pronoun at all, since English rarely marks reflexivity explicitly ("I wake up" vs. French's obligatory "je me réveille"); forgetting être as the automatic auxiliary for ALL reflexives in the passé composé.

---

## 9. Progressively Harder Examples

1. Je me réveille à six heures. — "I wake up at 6."
2. Elle ne se maquille pas le week-end. — "She doesn't put on makeup on weekends."
3. Nous nous sommes levés tard ce matin. — "We got up late this morning." (passé composé, être, plural agreement)
4. Il s'est mis à courir quand il a vu le bus. — "He started running when he saw the bus."
5. Je me suis fait des amis très rapidement quand je me suis installé ici. — "I made friends very quickly when I settled in here."
