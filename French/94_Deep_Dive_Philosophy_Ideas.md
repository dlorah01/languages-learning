## 1. Why Study Abstract French?

French has a strong tradition of public intellectual life: philosophy is a **school subject** (the *baccalauréat* includes a philosophy exam in the final year), essays and debates appear in mainstream newspapers and radio, and **ideas are part of everyday cultural conversation**. To read editorials, essays, interviews, and academic work comfortably, you need to understand how French handles abstraction.

**Mental model: abstract French is a grammar of nouns, distinctions, and logical links.** The language turns actions into nouns (*nominalization*), names concepts with suffixes (*-ité, -isme, -tion*), and constantly marks **how one idea relates to another** (opposition, consequence, condition, concession). Once you can see these three layers, dense texts become tractable.

| Layer | What to look for |
|---|---|
| **1. Concepts** | key nouns and their definitions |
| **2. Distinctions** | pairs and contrasts (*être/paraître, essence/existence, liberté/licence*) |
| **3. Logical movement** | connectors and structure: thesis → objection → resolution |

### 2.4 False-friend and nuance alerts
| Word | Careful |
|---|---|
| **la science** | broader than English "science": can mean knowledge or discipline; *les sciences humaines* (humanities and social sciences) |
| **sensible** | sensitive (not "sensible"); *sensé* = sensible |
| **l'intelligence** | also "understanding" (*l'intelligence d'un texte*) |
| **conscient / conscience** | check which sense |
| **le sens** | meaning, direction, sense (many senses): *dans ce sens, en un sens* |
| **la raison** | *avoir raison* = to be right; *raison d'être* = reason for being |
| **moral / la morale** | *la morale* = morality/ethics; *le moral* = morale (state of mind) |
| **éthique / morale** | often distinguished; check the author's usage |
| **actuel** | current, present (not "actual") |
| **la fin** | end *and* purpose (*la fin justifie les moyens*) |

## 3. How Abstract French Builds Sentences

### 3.1 Nominalization
Where English or Spanish might use a verb, French uses a noun phrase.

| Concrete | Abstract (nominalized) |
|---|---|
| *Quand on pense à la mort, on se sent angoissé.* | *La pensée de la mort suscite l'angoisse.* |
| *Les hommes veulent être libres.* | *L'aspiration des hommes à la liberté…* |
| *Il faut comprendre ce qu'est la justice.* | *La compréhension de la nature de la justice s'impose.* |

Reading tip: **unpack** the noun into a verb to get the meaning (*la mise en question de* = *le fait de remettre en question*).

### 3.2 Suffixes that make concepts (review Lesson 84)
*-ité* (*liberté, finalité, causalité*), *-isme* (*empirisme, rationalisme, existentialisme*), *-tion/-sion* (*perception, abstraction*), *-ance/-ence* (*conscience, existence*), *-ude* (*solitude, certitude*), *-esse* (*sagesse*), *-ité* with *-bilité* (*possibilité, responsabilité*).

### 3.3 Definitions and clarifications
| Move | French frame |
|---|---|
| **Define** | *Entendons par X…; On appelle X…; Par X, il faut comprendre…; X désigne…* |
| **Reformulate** | *c'est-à-dire, autrement dit, en d'autres termes, à savoir* |
| **Exemplify** | *ainsi, par exemple, comme dans le cas de…* |
| **Distinguish** | *il faut distinguer X de Y; X ne se confond pas avec Y; non pas X mais Y* |
| **Limit** | *du moins, dans la mesure où, en tant que, au sens où* |

### 3.4 Logical moves
| Move | Connectors |
|---|---|
| **Consequence** | *dès lors, il s'ensuit que, par conséquent, de là* |
| **Condition** | *à condition que (+ subj.), pourvu que, dans la mesure où, si tant est que (+ subj.)* |
| **Opposition** | *or, pourtant, néanmoins, en revanche, tandis que* |
| **Concession** | *certes… mais, sans doute… mais, il est vrai que… cependant* |
| **Cause** | *car, en effet, puisque, du fait que* |
| **Hypothesis** | *supposons que (+ subj.), à supposer que (+ subj.)* |
| **Conclusion** | *en définitive, au total, on voit donc que* |

**Notice *or*:** in argumentative French, *or* (conjunction, not "gold") introduces a new premise that advances the reasoning: *Tout homme est mortel ; or Socrate est un homme ; donc Socrate est mortel.*

### 3.5 Characteristic constructions
- **Impersonal and passive:** *Il s'agit de…, Il convient de…, Il y va de…, Il va de soi que…, Il ressort que…*
- **Cleft and emphasis:** *C'est précisément parce que… que…; Ce n'est pas X, c'est Y qui…*
- **Demonstratives to refer back:** *ce dernier, celui-ci, cela, ce que, ce qui; le premier… le second…*
- **Relative chains:** *ce à quoi renvoie ce concept, ce dont il est question* (Lesson 27).
- **Subjunctive in hypothetical or concessive clauses:** *quoi qu'on en pense, bien que ce soit, à moins que…*

## 5. The French Philosophical Exercises

Two exercises structure French intellectual training and shape how essays are written (Lesson 64):

### 5.1 La dissertation
A structured argument answering a **question**. Typical question forms:
- *Qu'est-ce que… ?* (definition)
- *Peut-on… ?* / *Doit-on… ?* / *Faut-il… ?* (possibility, obligation)
- *Suffit-il de… pour… ?* (sufficiency)
- *Est-il vrai que… ?* (assessment)

**Structure:**
1. **Introduction:** hook (*accroche*), definition of terms, **problématique** (the tension that makes the question a problem), announcement of the plan (*annonce du plan*).
2. **Development:** commonly three parts:
   - **Plan dialectique:** thesis → antithesis → synthesis ("*Oui, mais…* / *non, car…* / *dépasser*").
   - **Plan progressif or analytique:** step-by-step deepening.
3. **Conclusion:** answer to the problem, sometimes an opening (*ouverture*).

### 5.2 L'explication de texte
A close commentary on a short passage: **identify the thesis**, **trace the movement** (the logical steps), **explain key concepts**, and **assess** the argument. A model: *Dans ce texte, X soutient que… Pour cela, il procède en trois temps…*

You do not need to take these exams, but reading with this structure in mind helps you spot the **problem**, **the plan**, and **the movement**.

## 6. Strategies for Reading Dense Abstract Texts

| Strategy | How |
|---|---|
| **Find the question first** | what problem is the author addressing? Look at the title, the introduction, and any "*La question est de savoir si…*" |
| **Mark the connectors** | circle *or, mais, donc, car, certes, en effet*; they reveal the argument's skeleton |
| **Summarize each paragraph in one line** | a reverse outline of someone else's text (Lesson 90) |
| **Underline definitions** | *on appelle, il faut entendre par, c'est-à-dire* |
| **Unpack nominalizations** | translate noun phrases into verbs mentally |
| **Track key terms** | note when a word changes meaning mid-text (a common philosophical move) |
| **Distinguish author from opponent** | philosophical texts often present an opposing view before refuting it (*On objectera que… Certes… mais…*) |
| **Read short chunks, slowly, twice** | density beats length |
| **Use a specialized dictionary** | e.g., a philosophy dictionary or encyclopedia entry; the general dictionary is often not enough |
| **Paraphrase in simple French** | if you can restate the idea simply, you've understood it |
| **Compare translations** | where available, check a Spanish or English translation of a hard sentence *after* attempting it in French |

**Time budget:** a dense page may take 20–30 minutes at first. This is normal. Aim for understanding rather than speed.

## 7. Speaking About Ideas

Expressions for discussing abstract topics (see also Lesson 89):

| Function | Phrases |
|---|---|
| **Pose a problem** | *La question qui se pose est de savoir si…; Cela soulève le problème de…* |
| **Give your view** | *Il me semble que…; Selon moi…; Je soutiendrais que…* |
| **Question a premise** | *Cela suppose que…; Est-ce si évident ?; Tout dépend de ce qu'on entend par…* |
| **Clarify** | *Qu'entendez-vous par… ?; Pouvez-vous préciser… ?* |
| **Give a counterexample** | *Prenons un contre-exemple…* |
| **Nuance** | *C'est vrai dans une certaine mesure, mais…; Cela dépend du point de vue…* |
| **Synthesize** | *Si l'on tient compte de ces deux points de vue, on peut dire que…* |

**Tone:** French intellectual conversation values **precision, structure, and a touch of irony**. Disagreeing with ideas is normal; attacking persons is not (Lesson 89).

## 8. Common Mistakes

**Spanish speakers:**
- Reading *conciencia/conscience* too fast: French merges moral and psychological senses.
- Assuming cognates have identical meaning (*sensible, actuel, éventuellement, science*).
- Under-using *or*, *dès lors*, *il s'ensuit*, which are the backbone of French reasoning.
- Producing long Spanish-style sentences full of subordinate clauses when writing abstractly; break them into clear steps (Lesson 90).
- Forgetting the French habit of **defining terms** before arguing.

**English speakers:**
- Translating "I feel that" as *je sens que* (physical sensation) instead of *j'ai le sentiment que / il me semble que*.
- Missing that *or* is a logical conjunction.

**Both:**
- Reading for vocabulary only, not structure.
- Memorizing names instead of understanding arguments.
- Treating French thinkers as a single "theory" bloc; they disagree with each other.
- Writing opinion without a defined problem or a counter-argument.

## 9. Four-Week Deep-Dive Plan

| Week | Focus | Activities |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Vocabulary and structure** | build the toolkit | learn Section 2 vocabulary; list 20 connectors from Section 3; read one short opinion piece and mark the argument skeleton |
| **2. Classic text** | clarity | read a short chapter of Descartes (*Discours de la méthode*, part 1 or 2) or a few essays of Montaigne with an annotated edition; summarize each paragraph in one line |
| **3. Modern text** | movement | read a short extract of Camus (*Le Mythe de Sisyphe*) or Beauvoir; do an *explication* (thesis, movement, concepts) of one page |
| **4. Production** | argument | write a 600-word dissertation-style answer to a question such as *Faut-il toujours dire la vérité ?*; deliver a 5-minute oral version |

**Reading support:** annotated school editions (*éditions scolaires*), podcasts and radio programmes on philosophy (public cultural radio), and encyclopedic entries.

## 10. Progressively Harder Practice

1. **Vocabulary (10 min):** explain in simple French the difference between *la conscience* (moral) and *la conscience* (psychological), and between *la morale* and *le moral*.
2. **Unpack:** rewrite as verbal sentences: *La prise de conscience de la finitude conditionne la quête de sens.* → *Quand on prend conscience qu'on est mortel, on cherche un sens à sa vie.*
3. **Connector map:** take an editorial of 400 words, mark all connectors, and diagram the argument in 5 lines.
4. **Definition drill:** write definitions (one sentence each) for *liberté, égalité, justice, bonheur, devoir*.
5. **Questions:** formulate three *problématiques* from these topics: technology and freedom; work and happiness; truth and opinion.
6. **Summarize and respond:** read a 1-page opinion piece; write 100 words summarizing and 100 words responding.
7. **Oral exposé:** speak for 4 minutes on *Peut-on être heureux sans être libre ?*, with a defined problem, two perspectives, and a conclusion.
