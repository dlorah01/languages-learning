# Lesson 92 — Deep Dive 12: Your Personal Capstone Project
### Phase 5: Mastery & Maintenance — Optional Deep Dive

**Competency axes trained:** All eight axes, integrated · especially ✍️ Writing, 🗣️ Productive fluency, 🧱 Discourse, 🌍 Cultural competence
**Prerequisites:** Lesson 79 (final capstone), Lesson 80 (maintenance mode), and ideally two or three other deep dives
**Time investment:** 4–8 weeks, at the pace of Lesson 80's maintenance cycle (roughly 3–5 hours per week).

---

## 1. Why a Project?

Courses teach **skills**; projects build **ownership**. A real project in French forces everything to work together under real constraints: a purpose, an audience, a deadline, and a result other people can judge. Research on language learning consistently shows that meaningful, goal-oriented use of the language is where fluency consolidates.

**Mental model: the project is an integration test.** You have built the components (pronunciation, grammar, lexicon, register, discourse, listening, speaking, culture). The project runs them all together, reveals the weakest component, and gives you a concrete artifact as evidence.

| Benefit | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **Integration** | skills are used together, not in isolation |
| **Motivation** | you care about the result |
| **Evidence** | a portfolio shows real ability beyond test scores |
| **Diagnosis** | weak spots appear naturally |
| **Habit** | a project creates a durable reason to keep using French |

---

## 2. Choosing Your Project

Pick **one** project that matches your interests and your goals (professional, cultural, personal). Aim for something you will finish in 4–8 weeks.

| Category | Examples | Skills emphasized |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Spoken** | a 10-minute talk or presentation in French; a recorded podcast episode; a video explainer | speaking, listening, discourse |
| **B. Written** | an article or essay of 1,000–1,500 words; a personal blog series; a long, well-edited cover letter and portfolio | writing, discourse, register |
| **C. Technical** | French documentation for a project of yours; a README, a tutorial, a short technical report | technical writing (Lesson 91), lexicon |
| **D. Community** | contribute to a French-language open-source project or forum; answer questions; translate documentation (Lesson 78) | professional French, translation, pragmatics |
| **E. Cultural** | a guided study of one author, film director, or region, with a written or spoken review | literary/cultural competence |
| **F. Translation** | translate (and annotate) a short text, public domain or with permission, from Spanish or English into French | translation, lexicon, style |
| **G. Social** | host a regular conversation group; run a language exchange with a clear plan; organize a small event | interaction, pragmatics |
| **H. Test** | prepare and take a DELF/DALF exam (Lesson 83) | all skills under exam conditions |

**Selection criteria (score each 1–5):** Interest · Usefulness for your goals · Feasibility in your time · Requires real audience or feedback · Uses your weakest axes.

**Tip:** choose a project that stretches one weak axis. If your listening is the weakest, favor a podcast interview; if writing, an article.

---

## 3. Scoping: Make It Finishable

Most projects fail from **scope**, not from effort. Use this checklist:

1. **One sentence:** *Je vais [produire quoi] pour [qui] afin de [objectif], d'ici [date].*
2. **Audience:** who will read, hear, or use it?
3. **Deliverable:** exact format and length.
4. **Success criteria:** how will you judge it? (see Section 6)
5. **Constraints:** time per week, tools, language register.
6. **Cut list:** write three things you will *not* do.

> **Example:** *Je vais écrire un article de 1 200 mots sur l'histoire du café en Colombie pour un blog francophone de voyage, afin de montrer que je peux écrire dans un registre journalistique, d'ici le 20 décembre.*

---

## 4. A Six-to-Eight-Week Plan

| Week | Phase | Key tasks |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Define** | choose the project; write the one-sentence goal; gather reference material (3–5 models in French of the same type) |
| **2** | **Research and language prep** | read/listen to the models; build a project glossary (40–80 terms with gender and collocations); list useful chunks and structures |
| **3** | **Outline and first draft** (or first recording) | produce a rough version quickly, errors tolerated (Lesson 90 pipeline) |
| **4** | **Macro revision / first feedback** | restructure; get feedback from a tutor, teacher, or native speaker |
| **5** | **Micro revision / second draft** | sentence-level polish; address feedback; check register and typography |
| **6** | **Production / rehearsal** | final version; for spoken projects, 3 rehearsals with recording |
| **7** | **Delivery** | publish, present, or submit; collect reactions |
| **8** | **Reflection** | review: what worked, what didn't, what's next (Section 8) |

For shorter projects compress Weeks 2–3 and 5–6.

## 5. Support Systems

### 5.1 Feedback
Feedback is what turns a project into a learning engine.

| Source | What to ask |
|---|---|
| **Tutor/teacher** | grammar, register, structure; ask for corrections plus *explanations* |
| **Native-speaker friend or exchange partner** | naturalness: *Est-ce que ça sonne naturel ? Qu'est-ce qu'un francophone dirait ?* |
| **Language communities and forums** | review of specific passages |
| **Correction tools** | spelling, grammar, style suggestions (always review critically; Lesson 90) |
| **Self-review** | read aloud; compare with models; use a checklist |

**How to ask well:** give context (audience, purpose), indicate what you want checked (e.g., "register" or "connectors"), and keep the request small.

### 5.2 Francophone contact points (adapt to your location)
- **Alliance française** and **Institut français** centers often run courses, conversation clubs, cultural events, and exam registration; check whether one operates in your city (there are Alliance française branches in several Colombian cities, but confirm current offerings).
- **Language-exchange platforms and local meetups** for conversation partners.
- **Online communities** around your professional field (forums, open-source projects, Discord/Slack communities in French).
- **Francophone media and cultural events** (film festivals, online talks).

### 5.3 Accountability
- Fix two **weekly work blocks** in your calendar.
- Share the goal with a partner or tutor.
- Keep a short **project log** (date, what I did, what blocked me, next step).

## 6. Evaluating Your Work: A Rubric

Score each criterion 1 (weak) to 4 (strong), ideally with a second reader.

| Criterion | What to look for |
|---|---|
| **Message** | clear purpose, main idea, adapted to audience |
| **Organization** | logical structure, signposting, transitions (Lessons 64, 89, 90) |
| **Language accuracy** | grammar, agreement, spelling, typography (Lessons 70, 85) |
| **Range** | vocabulary variety, precise verbs, collocations (Lesson 49) |
| **Register** | consistent and appropriate (Lessons 47, 85) |
| **Naturalness** | sounds like French, not translated (calques, rhythm) |
| **Delivery** (spoken) | pronunciation, rhythm, fluency, repair strategies (Lessons 59, 89) |
| **Cultural fit** | references, politeness, conventions |
| **Impact** | does it do its job for the audience? |

Alongside the rubric, compare with the CEFR C1/C2 descriptors (Lesson 79) to identify which aspects still sit at B2 and which have reached C1. Be honest: that mismatch is the roadmap for your next cycle.

---

## 7. Building Your Portfolio

A **portfolio** (*dossier de réalisations*) documents ability beyond certificates.

| Item | Contents |
|---|---|
| **Project deliverable** | the final text, recording, or code with documentation |
| **Process evidence** | first draft vs. final draft, with notes on key changes |
| **Feedback record** | comments received and how you responded |
| **Reflection** | 300–400 words in French: what you learned, what remains hard |
| **Recording sample** | 2–3 minutes of you speaking about the project |
| **Error log** | recurring errors and fixes |

A portfolio is useful for study, for professional contexts (showing French in a CV or interview), and as a before-and-after record of progress.

---

## 8. Reflection and Next Cycle

At the end of the project, spend one hour on a structured **réflexion**:

1. **Réussites:** what went better than expected?
2. **Difficultés:** where did you slow down, freeze, or avoid structures?
3. **Erreurs récurrentes:** top three error patterns.
4. **Lacunes lexicales:** which words were missing in action?
5. **Prochaine étape:** one skill to target next, with a concrete plan.

Then connect to Lesson 80 maintenance mode: update your monthly review cycle so that the weakest axis gets extra attention for the next four weeks.

**Questions for your long-term plan**
- Which of the eight axes is now strongest? Weakest?
- What is my next project (a harder one)?
- What recurring habits keep my French alive (reading, listening, speaking, writing)?
- Do I want an external benchmark (DELF/DALF or TCF; Lesson 83) in the next 6–12 months?
- Where do I want to use French in the next year (work, travel, study, friendships)?

---

## 9. Common Pitfalls

| Pitfall | Fix |
|---|---|
| **Project too big** | cut scope; deliver a smaller finished project rather than an unfinished large one |
| **Perfectionism** | set a deadline; ship at 90 percent; revise next cycle |
| **No audience** | find at least one real reader or listener |
| **Writing in your native language first and translating** | outline and draft in French from the start (Lesson 71) |
| **Skipping feedback** | build at least two feedback points into the plan |
| **Avoiding hard structures** | include 3–5 targeted structures you want to master (Lesson 85) |
| **No reflection** | schedule the final hour in advance |
| **Over-reliance on tools** | use correction tools after your own revision, not instead of it |
| **Dropping the habit after delivery** | immediately schedule the next small project |

## 10. Project Templates (Choose and Adapt)

### Template A — Spoken talk (10 minutes)
1. Hook (30 s) → 2. Context and question (1 min) → 3. Three points with examples (6 min) → 4. Counter-argument (1 min) → 5. Conclusion and opening (1 min) → 6. Questions.
Rehearse three times; record; remove filler clusters; prepare 5 anticipated questions.

### Template B — Article (1,200 words)
Title and *chapeau* (lead, 40 words) → intro with hook and problem → three sections with subheadings → short conclusion → sources. Revise in three passes (macro, meso, micro).

### Template C — Technical documentation
Overview → prerequisites → quick start → detailed guide → FAQ → contribution notes. Terminology glossary at the end. Review with a French-speaking engineer if possible.

### Template D — Cultural study
Choose one author, filmmaker, or region. Consume 3–4 works or sources; write a 900-word review or give a 7-minute talk covering context, themes, style, and your reaction; use at least 15 new vocabulary items from the material.

### Template E — Translation with commentary
Translate a 400–600 word passage into French; write a 300-word commentary in French explaining five translation decisions (Lesson 78).

## 11. Sample Project Brief (Original Example)

> **Projet :** Documenter en français un petit outil de visualisation de données que j'ai développé.
> **Public :** développeurs francophones qui découvrent l'outil.
> **Livrables :** un README de 600 mots, un tutoriel de 5 étapes, un glossaire de 25 termes, un enregistrement de 3 minutes présentant l'outil.
> **Critères de réussite :** un lecteur francophone peut installer et utiliser l'outil sans aide ; le texte est relu par au moins une personne ; moins de 5 erreurs de langue repérées par la relecture.
> **Échéance :** 8 semaines. **Rythme :** 4 heures par semaine.
> **Hors périmètre :** traduction de la documentation complète ; site web.
