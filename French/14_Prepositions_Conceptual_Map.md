# Lesson 14 — Prepositions: The Conceptual Map
### Phase 1: Core System (A1 → A2)

**Competency axes trained:** 📚 Lexicon (primary) · 🧩 Morphosyntax (secondary)
**Prerequisite:** Lessons 6–8 (verbs, articles, gender) — prepositions frequently contract with articles
**Time investment:** One thorough read + ongoing exposure; prepositions are absorbed gradually through use, not memorized as a list

---

## 1. Why Prepositions Resist List-Learning (Intuitive Explanation)

Prepositions are famously the hardest vocabulary category to translate word-for-word between any two languages, because each language carves up spatial and abstract relationships slightly differently. Instead of a translation list (which will mislead you constantly), this lesson gives you the **conceptual core** of each major French preposition, so you can predict usage in new situations rather than memorizing every fixed phrase individually.

---

## 2. À vs. De — The Two Workhorses

**À** — conceptual core: **direction toward / location at / attachment to**
> je vais à Paris (going toward), je suis à la maison (location at), il pense à toi (mental direction toward)

**De** — conceptual core: **origin / possession / composition / separation from**
> je viens de Bogotá (origin), le livre de Marie (possession), une tasse de café (composition — "made of/containing"), loin de la ville (separation from)

**Critical contraction rule** (connects to Lesson 8's articles): à/de + le/les contract into fixed forms:

| Combination | Contracted form |
|---|---|
| à + le | au |
| à + les | aux |
| de + le | du |
| de + les | des |

> je vais **au** marché (à + le marché), je parle **aux** enfants (à + les enfants), je viens **du** Canada (de + le Canada)

Note: à/de + la/l' do **not** contract (à la maison, de l'eau) — only the masculine "le" and plural "les" contract.

---

## 3. Spanish Comparison — Where It Helps and Where It Doesn't

Spanish's a/de system is conceptually close (a = direction/location, de = origin/possession), so your base intuition transfers reasonably well. But watch for these divergences:

- Spanish personal "a" (voy a ver a Juan) has no French equivalent — French doesn't mark direct objects with a preposition.
- French "penser à" (to think about) vs. Spanish "pensar en" — different preposition for the same concept, a genuine false-friend trap.
- French "de" covers more ground than Spanish "de" alone in some possessive/compositional uses, occasionally overlapping with contexts where Spanish might use "con" or nothing at all.

---

## 4. Location Prepositions: En, Dans, Chez, Sur, Sous

| Preposition | Conceptual core | Example |
|---|---|---|
| en | inside a general/abstract space, or with most countries (feminine ones especially) | en France, en voiture, en été |
| dans | inside a concrete, bounded space | dans la boîte, dans la voiture (also valid — nuance: dans = more literally "inside," en = more like "by means of/within" for transport) |
| chez | at the place/home of a person | chez moi, chez le médecin |
| sur | on top of / about (topic) | sur la table, un livre sur l'histoire |
| sous | under | sous la table |

**Chez deserves special attention** — it has **no direct one-word equivalent in English or Spanish**. It means "at [person]'s place" and covers home, business, or even metaphorical "in [someone]'s way of thinking":
> chez moi (at my place), chez le dentiste (at the dentist's [office]), chez les Français (among/in French people [culturally])

---

## 5. Country/Place Preposition Logic (a genuine system, not arbitrary)

| Rule | Example |
|---|---|
| Feminine countries → en | en France, en Colombie |
| Masculine countries → au | au Canada, au Japon |
| Plural countries → aux | aux États-Unis |
| Cities → à (no article needed) | à Paris, à Bogotá |

This maps onto Lesson 8's gender system directly — country gender determines the preposition, another payoff of having internalized noun gender.

---

## 6. Mental Model: Prepositions as Relationship Verbs in Miniature

Rather than memorizing prepositions as static "translation units," think of each one as encoding a **type of relationship** between two things — direction, origin, containment, ownership, topic. When you hit a new preposition usage you haven't memorized, ask: *what relationship is being expressed here?* — direction, location, origin, or composition — and pick the preposition whose conceptual core matches, rather than searching for a word-for-word translation from Spanish or English.

---

## 7. Common Mistakes

**Spanish speakers:** transferring "pensar en" directly as "penser en" instead of the correct "penser à"; forgetting the à/de contraction rules and saying "à le" instead of "au."

**English speakers:** over-relying on literal translation for prepositions generally (English preposition logic diverges the most from French of the three languages); missing the country-gender-preposition link since English uses "in/to" uniformly regardless of country gender.

---

## 8. Progressively Harder Examples

1. Je vais au Canada en été. — "I'm going to Canada in the summer."
2. Elle pense à son travail. — "She's thinking about her work." (not "pense en")
3. Nous habitons chez mes parents pour le moment. — "We're living at my parents' place for now."
4. Le livre est sur la table, à côté du café. — "The book is on the table, next to the coffee."
5. Je viens de rentrer du bureau, et je vais chez le médecin cet après-midi. — "I just got back from the office, and I'm going to the doctor's this afternoon."

---

## 9. Collocations

- **à côté de** — "next to" (contracted: à côté du bureau)
- **en face de** — "across from"
- **au lieu de** — "instead of"
- **grâce à** — "thanks to"
