## 1. Why This Domain (Intuitive Explanation)

Health vocabulary is essential practical vocabulary — describing symptoms, understanding a doctor, or simply saying how you feel. It also extends Lesson 7's avoir-idiom pattern into a whole new productive domain.

**Irregular plural flag:** "l'œil" (eye) → "les yeux" (eyes) is a genuinely irregular plural, worth memorizing as an exception to Lesson 8's regular -s pattern.

## 3. Describing Pain: Avoir Mal À (Lesson 7 + Lesson 14 combined)

**Formula:** avoir mal à + definite article + body part (with à/le/les contractions from Lesson 14!)

> **J'ai mal à la tête.** — "I have a headache."
> **J'ai mal au dos.** — "My back hurts." (à + le → au, Lesson 14 callback)
> **J'ai mal aux dents.** — "My teeth hurt." (à + les → aux)

This is a beautiful payoff of combining two earlier lessons: the avoir-idiom pattern (Lesson 7) plus the à-contraction system (Lesson 14) into one highly productive, extremely common structure.

## 5. At the Doctor's: Useful Structures

> **Qu'est-ce qui ne va pas?** — "What's wrong?" (a fixed, idiomatic question structure)
> **Depuis quand avez-vous mal?** — "Since when have you had pain?" (formal "vous," Lesson 16 callback — medical contexts always default to vous)
> **Je me sens fatigué(e) depuis quelques jours.** — "I've been feeling tired for a few days." (reflexive + depuis for duration)

**"Depuis" for duration — a genuine structural note:** French uses "depuis" + present tense to describe an action/state that started in the past and continues now — unlike English's present perfect continuous ("I have been feeling"), French simply uses present tense + depuis: **je me sens fatigué depuis trois jours** (not a literal "I have felt" construction). This mirrors Spanish's own "desde hace" pattern reasonably well, worth flagging as a genuine structural point rather than pure vocabulary.

---

## 6. Mental Model: The Body as a Map of Avoir-Mal-À Locations

Picture the body itself as a map where "avoir mal à + [location]" can point to nearly any spot — the structure is completely productive once you know the pattern and the contraction rules. This is efficient: one grammatical structure, dozens of vocabulary items, near-infinite combinations, all instantly comprehensible to a French speaker.

---

## 7. Common Mistakes

**Spanish speakers:** occasionally using "tener dolor de" logic too literally, missing the "à + article" contraction requirement in avoir mal à; forgetting the irregular œil/yeux plural.

**English speakers:** trying to build a present-perfect-continuous equivalent for "depuis" sentences instead of French's simpler present + depuis structure.

---

## 8. Progressively Harder Examples

1. J'ai mal à la gorge. — "My throat hurts."
2. Elle a de la fièvre depuis hier. — "She's had a fever since yesterday."
3. Qu'est-ce qui ne va pas? — J'ai mal au ventre et je me sens fatigué. — "What's wrong? — My stomach hurts and I feel tired."
4. Le médecin m'a donné une ordonnance pour un rhume. — "The doctor gave me a prescription for a cold."
5. Je tousse et j'ai mal à la tête depuis trois jours, donc je vais à la pharmacie. — "I've been coughing and had a headache for three days, so I'm going to the pharmacy."
