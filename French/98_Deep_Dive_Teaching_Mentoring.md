## 1. Why Teach to Learn?

Explaining something to someone else is one of the strongest ways to consolidate it. To teach a rule, you must **retrieve** it, **organize** it, find **examples**, and anticipate **errors**. Research on learning describes this as the "protégé effect": preparing to teach, even a hypothetical learner, improves your own understanding and memory.

You also have a rare advantage: you are a **Spanish speaker who has learned French as an adult**. You know, from the inside, which things surprise Spanish-speaking beginners. Most native French teachers do not.

**Mental model: teaching is compression plus translation.** You compress a rule into its essential core, then translate it into the learner's frame of reference (Spanish), using only what they need now.

| Benefit to you | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| **Deeper rule knowledge** | you must define, exemplify, and contrast |
| **Spotting your own gaps** | you can't explain what you don't fully understand |
| **Fluency practice** | explaining aloud in French is high-quality speaking practice |
| **Metalinguistic awareness** | you learn the vocabulary of grammar in French |
| **Contribution** | you help others, strengthen your community, and may open professional options |

## 2. Anatomy of a Good Explanation

### 2.1 The six-part structure
1. **Hook / why it matters:** one sentence on when the learner will need this.
2. **The core rule:** stated in the simplest accurate way.
3. **Contrast with Spanish (and English):** what is the same, what differs.
4. **Examples:** 3–5, from clearly correct to subtly tricky, in realistic context.
5. **Common errors:** the 2–3 mistakes Spanish speakers make.
6. **Practice:** a short task that uses the rule immediately.

**Example — the partitive article (explained for a Spanish-speaking beginner)**

> **Why:** you need it every time you talk about food or quantities.
> **Core:** to say "some" of something uncountable, French uses *du / de la / de l'* (*Je mange du pain*).
> **Contrast:** Spanish simply omits the article (*como pan*); French cannot.
> **Examples:** *Je bois de l'eau. Il y a de la soupe. Tu veux du café ?* After negation: *Je ne mange pas de pain.*
> **Errors:** *Je mange pain* ✘; *Je ne mange pas du pain* ✘ (after negation, *de*).
> **Practice:** complete: *Elle boit ___ lait. Il n'y a pas ___ sucre.*

### 2.2 Principles
| Principle | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **One idea at a time** | don't pile exceptions on a first explanation |
| **Rule first, exceptions later** | give the 80 percent pattern before the 20 percent |
| **Examples before abstraction (or both)** | many learners grasp patterns from examples faster than from terminology |
| **Use the learner's language when needed** | for beginners, brief Spanish explanations are efficient; shift to French as the learner grows |
| **Be honest about uncertainty** | *Je ne suis pas sûr(e), je vérifie.* Looking things up is a professional habit |
| **Make it memorable** | a mental model, an image, a mnemonic (use sparingly) |
| **Check understanding** | ask the learner to produce an example, not just say "yes, I understand" |

### 2.3 Avoiding the "curse of knowledge"
Experts forget what it was like not to know. Warning signs: using jargon without defining it, skipping steps, answering a different question than the one asked. Remedy: remember **your own confusion** when you first met the rule; ask what the learner *already* knows.

## 3. What Spanish Speakers Need Most (Your Transfer Map)

### 3.1 Positive transfer (help the learner use what they know)
- Large shared vocabulary (cognates): *nation, important, université, difficile* (Lesson 84).
- Similar verb systems: person/number endings, imparfait vs passé composé (≈ *imperfecto* vs *pretérito*), subjunctive concept (Lesson 26).
- Grammatical gender and agreement.
- Similar object-pronoun logic (although order and forms differ, Lesson 24).

### 3.2 Negative transfer (the predictable errors)
| Area | Spanish habit | French reality |
|---|---|---|
| **Pronunciation** | vowels are pure and stable | French has nasal vowels, *u* vs *ou*, *eu*, silent endings (Lessons 1, 4) |
| **Written vs spoken** | spelling ≈ sound | many silent letters, many homophones |
| **Subject pronouns** | often omitted (pro-drop) | required: *Je parle*, never *Parle* |
| **Gender** | *el problema, la mano, el día* | *le problème (m.), la main (f.), le jour (m.)*, many differences (Lesson 8) |
| **Articles** | omitted in many contexts | needed: *J'ai des amis; Je mange du pain* |
| **Negation** | *no* before the verb | two-part *ne… pas* |
| **Questions** | inverted punctuation, intonation | *est-ce que*, inversion, intonation |
| **Preposition + infinitive** | *Voy a comer* | *Je vais manger* (no preposition after *aller*); *commencer à / finir de* |
| **Being and having idioms** | *tener hambre / calor / razón* | French also uses *avoir*: *avoir faim, chaud, raison* (positive transfer!) |
| **Ser / estar** | two verbs | French *être* covers both; the distinction is lexical |
| **Saber / conocer** | two verbs | *savoir / connaître* (similar) |
| **Por / para** | two prepositions | French uses *pour, par, à cause de, grâce à*, etc. |
| **Personal *a*** | *Veo a Juan* | no equivalent: *Je vois Jean* |
| **Reflexive verbs** | similar, but not identical | some verbs differ: *se souvenir* vs *recordar*, *s'en aller* |
| **False friends** | *embarazada, librería, actual, constipado* | *enceinte, librairie, actuel, enrhumé* (Lesson 70) |
| **Past tenses** | pretérito perfecto vs indefinido | *passé composé* does both; regional differences in Spanish |
| **Time expressions** | *hace dos años, desde hace* | *il y a deux ans, depuis* |
| **Relative pronouns** | *que, quien, cuyo* | *qui, que, dont, où, lequel* (Lesson 27) |
| **Adjective position** | mostly after the noun | mostly after, but many fixed before (*un grand homme* vs *un homme grand*) |

*Keep a personal "top 20" list of the mistakes **you** made. They are probably the same mistakes your learners will make.*

## 4. Correcting Errors Well

Poor correction demotivates; good correction teaches.

### 4.1 Techniques
| Technique | Description | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Recast** | repeat correctly without stopping the flow | Learner: *Hier je vais au cinéma.* → You: *Ah, tu es allé au cinéma hier ? C'était bien ?* |
| **Elicitation** | prompt self-correction | *Hier… ? Qu'est-ce qu'on dit pour le passé ?* |
| **Metalinguistic clue** | hint at the rule | *Attention au temps du verbe.* |
| **Delayed correction** | note errors, review after the activity | write 3–5 errors on a list, go through them at the end |
| **Explicit correction** | state the correct form and the reason | *On dit « je suis allé » ; avec « aller », on utilise l'auxiliaire « être ».* |
| **Ignore** | skip small errors that don't impede communication | especially during fluency practice |

### 4.2 Choosing what to correct
Prioritize errors that are (1) **frequent**, (2) **blocking communication**, (3) **related to the current lesson goal**, or (4) **likely to fossilize**. During free conversation, limit corrections to 3–5 per session so the learner keeps talking.

### 4.3 The feedback sandwich (use sparingly, genuinely)
Specific praise → one or two precise corrections → a clear next step. Avoid vague praise (*très bien !*) and vague criticism (*ce n'est pas naturel*): say *quoi* and *pourquoi*.

## 5. Designing a Lesson or a Session

### 5.1 A simple 60-minute plan
| Time | Activity | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 0–5 min | **Warm-up** (small talk, review question) | activate language |
| 5–15 min | **Presentation** of a target structure, with examples | noticing |
| 15–30 min | **Controlled practice** (gap fill, transformation, short dialogues) | accuracy |
| 30–50 min | **Communicative task** (role-play, discussion, information gap) | fluency |
| 50–55 min | **Feedback** (delayed corrections, praise) | consolidation |
| 55–60 min | **Wrap-up and homework** | transfer |

This is a flexible PPP (Presentation–Practice–Production) pattern. Alternatives: **task-based** (start with the task, analyze language afterwards) or **lexical** approaches (teach chunks first).

### 5.2 Choosing goals
Write the goal as a **can-do** statement: *À la fin de la séance, l'apprenant peut commander dans un restaurant et demander l'addition.* Align it with CEFR descriptors (Lesson 79).

### 5.3 Activities that work
| Type | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Information gap** | each learner has half the information (map, schedule) and must ask questions |
| **Role-play** | bakery, hotel, job interview (Lessons 95, 96) |
| **Picture description** | describe, compare, guess |
| **Dictation / running dictation** | listening and spelling |
| **Error hunt** | find and fix mistakes in a text |
| **Transformation** | rewrite from present to past; from informal to formal |
| **Discussion with prepared questions** | ask open questions, one idea at a time |
| **Storytelling** | retell a short story with prompts (Lesson 68) |
| **Games** | quizzes, word chains, taboo-style descriptions |

### 5.4 Pace and wait time
After asking a question, **wait** (3–5 seconds). Silence is thinking time. Resist the urge to answer yourself.

## 6. Facilitating Conversation Practice

If you run a conversation partnership or a small group:

1. **Prepare 5–10 open questions** on a theme (travel, work, food, plans).
2. **Start simple** and escalate: facts → opinions → hypotheticals.
3. **Use follow-ups:** *Pourquoi ? Par exemple ? Et ensuite ?*
4. **Balance talk time:** learners should talk about 70–80 percent of the session.
5. **Model language** but don't dominate.
6. **Keep a list** of useful phrases that came up; send it afterwards.
7. **Tune your own speech:** speak naturally but clearly; slow down slightly, simplify structures without turning to baby talk; avoid sudden switches to Spanish unless necessary.
8. **Be patient and kind:** learners are exposed; mistakes are normal.

**Questions that open conversation:** *Qu'est-ce que tu as fait ce week-end ? Si tu pouvais voyager n'importe où, où irais-tu ? Qu'est-ce qui t'a surpris(e) en France/Colombie ? Quelle est ta routine du matin ?*

## 7. Explaining Grammar Terms in French

To teach in French, you need the metalanguage (the language about language). Learn these.

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **un nom, un verbe, un adjectif, un adverbe** | noun, verb, adjective, adverb |
| **un déterminant, un article (défini / indéfini / partitif)** | determiner, article (definite / indefinite / partitive) |
| **un pronom (personnel, relatif, démonstratif, possessif)** | pronoun types |
| **une préposition, une conjonction** | preposition, conjunction |
| **le sujet, le complément d'objet direct (COD) / indirect (COI)** | subject, direct / indirect object |
| **le genre (masculin / féminin), le nombre (singulier / pluriel)** | gender, number |
| **l'accord, accorder** | agreement, to agree |
| **le temps (présent, passé composé, imparfait, futur…)** | tense |
| **le mode (indicatif, subjonctif, conditionnel, impératif)** | mood |
| **l'auxiliaire (m.), le participe passé** | auxiliary, past participle |
| **le radical, la terminaison** | stem, ending |
| **un verbe régulier / irrégulier** | regular / irregular verb |
| **une proposition principale / subordonnée / relative** | main / subordinate / relative clause |
| **affirmatif, négatif, interrogatif** | affirmative, negative, interrogative |
| **la ponctuation** | punctuation |
| **une exception, une règle** | exception, rule |

Useful teaching frames: *On utilise… quand… ; Il faut accorder… avec… ; La règle, c'est que… ; Attention : il y a une exception ; Regarde la terminaison ; Remplace par… pour vérifier.*

**Tests you can teach:** to find a COD, ask *quoi ? / qui ?* after the verb; to choose between *a / à*, replace with *avait*; to tell *ou / où*, replace with *ou bien*; to recognize the participle agreement, replace with a masculine or feminine noun.

## 8. Ethics, Scope, and Next Steps

| Topic | Guidance |
|---|---|
| **Honesty about your role** | you are a peer tutor or language partner, not a certified teacher, unless you are. Don't claim qualifications you don't have |
| **Accuracy** | verify rules you are unsure about (grammar references, Lesson 90 tools); say *je vérifie* |
| **Respect for the learner** | privacy, patience, no ridicule |
| **Payment and professional teaching** | requirements differ by country; formal teaching of French as a foreign language (*FLE*) often involves specific qualifications (for example, diplomas in FLE; check current options with official bodies such as universities, the Alliance française, or the Institut français) |
| **Boundaries** | keep sessions focused on language; set expectations on schedule, cancellation, and goals |
| **Learning for yourself** | keep your own study going; teaching shouldn't replace practice of your weak areas |

**Pathways** (if interested): peer tutoring and language exchanges; volunteering in community or migrant-support organizations that teach French; study for an FLE qualification; tutoring Spanish speakers online; writing your own explanations or guides (Lesson 92 capstone).

## 9. Common Mistakes

**New mentors:**
- Over-explaining; giving a lecture instead of letting the learner practice.
- Teaching rare exceptions too early.
- Correcting every error, stopping the flow.
- Speaking too much (teacher talk time above 50 percent).
- Using jargon the learner doesn't know.
- Giving rules that are too simple and false ("always") when they are tendencies.
- Not preparing examples.
- Failing to check understanding.
- Comparing the learner to others.

**Spanish-speaking mentors specifically:**
- Assuming that every Spanish pattern parallels French; check each time.
- Forgetting how hard French pronunciation and spelling are for beginners.
- Over-relying on Spanish explanations and under-using French.

## 10. Four-Week Deep-Dive Plan

| Week | Focus | Activities |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Rule cards** | explain | write six-part explanations (Section 2.1) for five rules you find tricky (e.g., passé composé auxiliary, partitive, object pronouns, *depuis*, subjunctive triggers); say each aloud in 90 seconds |
| **2. Error map** | transfer | build your top-20 list of Spanish-speaker errors with a one-line fix and an example each |
| **3. Session design** | plan | write a 60-minute lesson plan for a beginner on one theme (ordering food); include a role-play and a feedback list; if possible, teach it to a friend or language partner |
| **4. Practice and reflect** | teach | run one real session (30–60 minutes); record or take notes; reflect: what worked, what confused, what to change |

**Checklist (tick each):** clear goal? rule stated simply? contrast with Spanish? realistic examples? learners talking most of the time? corrections prioritized? understanding checked? feedback specific? time managed?

## 11. Progressively Harder Practice

1. **Explain in 60 seconds (aloud):** the difference between *depuis* and *il y a*.
2. **Write an explanation (6-part)** of *le passé composé avec être* for a Spanish-speaking beginner.
3. **Error analysis:** correct and explain: *Je suis allé a la escuela ayer. Elle a tombé. J'ai 20 ans depuis 2 ans. Nous sommes mangé.* (these mix Spanish and French errors on purpose)
4. **Design an information-gap task** about a weekly schedule.
5. **Plan five open questions** for a 20-minute conversation on travel.
6. **Explain a grammar term** (e.g., *le COD*) in simple French to someone who has never heard it.
7. **Teach-back:** teach a friend a 15-minute mini-lesson in French, then ask for feedback on your clarity.
