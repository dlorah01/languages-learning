# Lesson 53 — Technical and Professional Vocabulary for Your Field
### Phase 4: Native Naturalness (B2 → C1)

**Competency axes trained:** 📚 Lexicon (primary) · 🔗 Discourse competence · 🗣️ Productive fluency
**Prerequisite:** Lesson 33 (academic and technical discourse), Lesson 49 (collocations), Lesson 32 (professional register)

## 1. Why This Lesson Is a Method, Not a Word List (Intuitive Explanation)

Your stated goal includes explaining complex technical topics in French. I do not know your specific field, and a generic word list would waste your time. So this lesson teaches you a **method** that works for any field (software, engineering, finance, medicine, law, science), plus the **cross-field backbone** of technical French that every field shares. You then apply it to your own domain.

**Good news from your profile:** technical vocabulary is where your English and Spanish pay the biggest dividends. A very large share of technical French is Latin or Greek in origin, so it is recognizable from English and Spanish (*le système*, *la méthode*, *l'algorithme*, *la structure*, *la fonction*, *le processus*).

## 3. Explaining a Concept in French: Four Reusable Moves

These sentence frames let you explain **any** technical concept with intermediate vocabulary.

### Move 1: Define
- **X est un/une** [category] **qui** [function]. — *Un algorithme est une suite d'instructions qui permet de résoudre un problème.*
- **On appelle X** [definition]. — *On appelle « API » une interface qui permet à deux logiciels de communiquer.*
- **X désigne** [definition]. — more formal.

### Move 2: Explain how it works
- **Le principe est le suivant :** ...
- **Cela fonctionne ainsi :** d'abord..., ensuite..., enfin...
- **Concrètement,** ... (very natural and common spoken connector)
- **Autrement dit,** ... (rephrase)

### Move 3: Give an example or analogy
- **Par exemple, / Prenons l'exemple de...**
- **C'est un peu comme** + noun. — *C'est un peu comme une bibliothèque où chaque livre a une adresse.*
- **On peut comparer X à Y.**

### Move 4: State limits and consequences
- **Cependant, ... / Le problème, c'est que...**
- **Cela implique que... / Par conséquent, ...** (and mind the mood: *il faut que* + subjunctive)
- **En revanche,** ... (contrast)

**Complete model explanation (45 seconds, spoken):**

> *Un cache est une mémoire rapide qui permet d'éviter de refaire le même calcul. Le principe est le suivant : la première fois, on calcule le résultat et on le stocke ; ensuite, on le relit directement. C'est un peu comme noter un numéro de téléphone au lieu de le chercher à chaque fois. En revanche, il faut que les données restent à jour, sinon on risque de lire des informations périmées.*

Notice the architecture: **définition → principe → analogie → limite**. It is exactly the explicit scaffolding from Lesson 33.

## 4. Building Your Own Field Lexicon (the Two-Week Method)

You will construct a mini-dictionary for **your own field**, entirely through French sources.

**Step 1: Collect (days 1–4).** Read or listen to **real French material in your field**: a French documentation page, a technical blog, a vulgarisation video, a French Wikipedia article on a concept you already know well. Highlight every term you would need to explain the topic.

**Step 2: Organize as word families, not a list.** For each core noun, capture:
- the noun with its **gender** (*le serveur*, *la base de données*)
- its **verb** (*héberger*, *interroger*)
- its **common adjectives** (*distribué*, *performant*)
- its **collocations** (*déployer un serveur*, *interroger une base*)
- the **English term** (many fields keep English terms; note which: *le cloud*, *le workflow*, *le feedback*)

**Step 3: Learn the "franglais" boundary.** In many technical fields, French speakers use English terms freely (*le frontend*, *le debugging*, *le sprint*) and also have an official French term (*le débogage*, *le code source*). Note **which is used where**: companies and conversations often prefer the English term; official documents, teaching, and Quebec French prefer the French one.

**Step 4: Teach it back (days 5–14).** Every day, explain **one concept** from your field aloud in French for 60 seconds, recording yourself. This is retrieval practice, spontaneous production, and thinking-in-French training in one exercise. Re-record the same concept after a week and compare.

**Template for each vocabulary entry:**

```
TERME : la base de données (f.) [bas də done]
FAMILLE : interroger une base, alimenter une base, une requête
COLLOCATIONS : une base de données relationnelle, migrer une base de données
EN : database / ES : base de datos
REGISTRE : neutre, courant dans tous les contextes professionnels
PHRASE : Nous avons migré la base de données vers un nouveau serveur.
```

## 5. Pronunciation Alerts for Technical Vocabulary

Technical words are long and Latinate, and they hide pronunciation traps (Lessons 1, 4, 5):

| Word | IPA | Trap |
|---|---|---|
| un algorithme | [alɡɔʁitm] | the final *e* is silent; stress the last syllable |
| une fonction | [fɔ̃ksjɔ̃] | two nasal vowels, *-tion* as [sjɔ̃] |
| une analyse | [analiz] | *y* and *s* → [z] between vowels |
| un paramètre | [paʁamɛtʁ] | final cluster [tʁ] |
| la sécurité | [sekyʁite] | the French [y], not [u] |
| un processus | [pʁɔsesys] | final *s* **is** pronounced here (exception!) |
| le matériel | [mateʁjɛl] | "hardware"; do not confuse with *le logiciel* ("software") |

**False friends to flag (English/Spanish bridge):**
- *actuel(lement)* = current(ly), **not** "actual(ly)" (use *en fait / réellement*). Spanish *actual* has the right meaning, so you benefit here.
- *une démonstration* = a demo/proof; *démontrer* = to prove.
- *un ingénieur* (not "engineer" only: *ingénieur* is a protected, diploma-based title in France).
- *éventuellement* = possibly, **not** "eventually" (*finalement*, *à terme*).
- *supporter* = to tolerate (Lesson 39), so for technical "support" use *prendre en charge* (a system *prend en charge* a format).

## 6. Mental Model: A Scaffold You Reuse, a Lexicon You Grow

Picture technical French as two layers. The **scaffold** (the verbs, frames, and connectors above) is small, fixed, and reusable across every topic. The **lexicon** (your field's nouns) is large but grows quickly because it is mostly Latin/Greek and you already know the concepts. You do not need to learn "technical French" from zero: you need the scaffold once and a growing, organized lexicon of your own domain, built through real French content.

## 7. Common Mistakes

**Spanish speakers:** assuming every cognate is safe (*eventual*, *actual*, *realizar* ≠ *réaliser* in all uses); dropping articles when listing technical terms (French wants *le*, *la*, *les* in nearly all noun phrases); copying Spanish word order for adjectives (*un système informatique* is correct, but check each case).

**English speakers:** using noun stacks ("database migration tool") that French cannot do. French needs prepositions: *un outil de migration de base de données*. Also forgetting gender.

**Both:** underusing French connectors when explaining (Lesson 33), producing correct but "flat" explanations with no signposting.

## 8. Progressively Harder Examples

1. Ce logiciel permet de gérer les stocks en temps réel. — "This software makes it possible to manage inventory in real time."
2. Le système repose sur trois composants principaux. — "The system relies on three main components."
3. Concrètement, il suffit de déployer la nouvelle version, puis de vérifier que tout fonctionne. — "Concretely, you just deploy the new version and then check that everything works."
4. Cette méthode consiste à diviser le problème en étapes plus petites, ce qui permet de corriger les erreurs plus facilement. — "This method consists in splitting the problem into smaller steps, which makes it easier to fix errors."
5. Bien que la solution soit performante, il faut que nous la testions davantage avant de la mettre en production, car un dysfonctionnement coûterait cher. — "Although the solution performs well, we need to test it further before putting it into production, because a malfunction would be costly."

## 9. Speaking Practice (the Daily 60 Seconds)

Choose **one concept** from your field. Using the four moves (define → how it works → example/analogy → limit), explain it aloud for 60 seconds. Record it. Listen back and note: (a) where you reached for English, (b) where you hesitated, (c) which collocation or connector you missed. Those gaps are tomorrow's study list.

---

## 10. Listening and Reading Recommendations (Immersion Ladder)

Pick material **in your own field**: French-language documentation and technical blogs (*Journal du Net*, official documentation with a French locale), French YouTube channels that popularize science or technology, and French Wikipedia articles. **Difficulty:** B2 with a glossary. **Goal:** collect terms in context and hear how professionals actually phrase explanations. Read first, then listen, because reading gives you spelling and gender, and listening shows you rhythm and reductions (Lesson 45).
