# Lesson 85 — Deep Dive 5: Advanced Grammar Clinic
### Phase 5: Mastery & Maintenance — Optional Deep Dive

**Competency axes trained:** 🧩 Morphosyntax (primary) · ✍️ Writing · 📖 Reading · 🎭 Register
**Prerequisites:** Lessons 42 (sentence architecture), 58 (subjunctive mastery), 64 (advanced written French), 70 (grammar fine points)
**Time investment:** One read, then a four-week deep-dive cycle (Lesson 80, Section 7).

---

## 1. Purpose and Mental Model

By C1 you can form almost any sentence. What separates a very advanced writer from a fluent one is **control of rare, marked structures**: knowing that they exist, recognizing them instantly in literary and formal texts, and using a small number of them **correctly and sparingly**.

**Mental model: grammar as a register dial.** Every structure in this lesson is a *choice* that moves the text toward the formal-written end of the dial. Using one in a casual chat sounds theatrical; never recognizing one in a novel makes reading slow. Your goal is **full recognition, selective production**.

| Level of control | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Recognize** | You read it without effort and know what it signals. |
| **Produce (formal writing)** | You can use it correctly in essays, reports, letters. |
| **Avoid (speech)** | You know it would sound stilted in conversation. |

## 2. Stylistic Inversion (*Inversion stylistique*)

Beyond the question inversion you know (*Où va-t-il ?*), French can invert subject and verb for style, in written or formal registers.

### 2.1 Mandatory or near-mandatory inversion after certain adverbs (written)
After sentence-initial adverbs of limitation or consequence, the subject (especially a pronoun) is inverted:

| Adverb | Example | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **peut-être** | *Peut-être viendra-t-il.* / *Peut-être qu'il viendra.* (spoken) | Perhaps he will come |
| **sans doute** | *Sans doute a-t-elle raison.* | Probably she is right |
| **aussi** (= therefore) | *Il était malade ; aussi est-il resté chez lui.* | so, hence |
| **ainsi** | *Ainsi vécurent-ils longtemps.* | Thus... |
| **à peine** | *À peine était-il arrivé qu'il repartit.* | Hardly had he arrived when... |
| **du moins / au moins** | *Du moins savons-nous la vérité.* | At least... |
| **en vain** | *En vain essaya-t-il de la convaincre.* | In vain... |
| **tout au plus** | *Tout au plus peut-on espérer un délai.* | At most... |

> **Watch out:** *aussi* meaning "also" does **not** trigger inversion (*Il est aussi gentil.*). Only *aussi* = "therefore" at the beginning of a clause does.

### 2.2 Inversion in subordinate and relative clauses (elegant, not mandatory)
- **After *que* in relative clauses with a heavy subject:** *Le livre que vient de publier mon collègue…* (instead of *que mon collègue vient de publier*).
- **After *où*, *dont*, *comme*, *ainsi que*:** *Les villes où se déroulent les festivals… ; Comme le disait Montaigne…*
- **In the subjunctive of concession/hypothesis:** *Quoi qu'il fasse…; Vienne le jour où…; Advienne que pourra* (frozen literary form: "come what may").
- **Après *tel* or fronted complements:** *Telle fut sa réponse.* *Dans cette maison vivait un vieil homme.* *Sont exclus les mineurs.* (very common in official texts)

### 2.3 The *-t-* euphonic consonant
When inversion puts a vowel-ending verb form (3rd person singular) before *il/elle/on*, insert **-t-**: *A-t-il compris ? Parle-t-elle ? Y a-t-il… ?* (Lesson 9).

### 2.4 Why use it?
Inversion shifts weight to the end of the sentence, where French likes new or important information (Lesson 42, end-weight principle). In formal prose it also adds rhythm.

**Spanish contrast:** Spanish freely places the subject after the verb (*Llegó Juan*); French does not. Inversion is the **exception** that must be licensed by context, not a free option.

## 3. Rare Tenses and Literary Forms

### 3.1 The *passé antérieur* (past anterior)
**Form:** *passé simple* of *avoir/être* + past participle (*j'eus fini, il fut parti*).
**Use:** an action completed **immediately before** another past action expressed in the *passé simple*, after a time conjunction:

- *Dès qu'il eut fini, il partit.* (As soon as he had finished, he left.)
- *Quand elle fut arrivée, la fête commença.*
- Conjunctions: *quand, lorsque, dès que, aussitôt que, après que, à peine… que*.

**Today:** only in formal writing and narrative. In everyday contexts replace it with the *plus-que-parfait* (*Dès qu'il avait fini, il est parti*) or the *passé composé* with *dès que* (*Dès qu'il a eu fini…* , the "surcomposé", see 3.2).

### 3.2 The *passé surcomposé* (double compound)
**Form:** *avoir* (compound form) + past participle: *j'ai eu fini, il a eu terminé.*
**Use:** spoken or informal equivalent of the *passé antérieur*, for completion before another *passé composé* action: *Quand j'ai eu fini, je suis parti.* It is common in regional and spoken French (e.g., southern France, Switzerland) and still regarded as informal in writing.

### 3.3 The *imparfait du subjonctif* and *plus-que-parfait du subjonctif*
Reviewed in Lesson 58. Recognize forms in -ât/-ît/-ût (*qu'il fût, qu'il fît, qu'il eût, qu'il aimât*). Produce only in deliberately literary or ironic registers. The third-person singular forms are the most common.

### 3.4 The *passé simple* beyond third person
You know *il alla, ils furent*. The first and second persons (*je parlai, nous allâmes, vous fûtes*) are rare even in novels except in first-person narration. Recognize them, don't produce them.

### 3.5 The *futur antérieur* for probability and the *conditionnel passé* for reported or unconfirmed facts
Less rare but worth refining:
- *Il aura oublié.* (He must have forgotten.) — assumption about the past.
- *Selon la presse, le ministre aurait démissionné.* (According to the press, the minister has reportedly resigned.) — journalistic conditional (Lesson 74).

## 4. Absolute Constructions and Participial Clauses

### 4.1 Absolute participle clause (*proposition participiale*)
A participle clause with **its own subject**, different from the main clause subject, expressing time, cause, or condition:

| Example | Equivalent |
|---|---|
| *Les invités partis, nous avons rangé la maison.* | After the guests had left… |
| *Le soleil se couchant, ils rentrèrent.* | As the sun set… |
| *Sa mère étant malade, elle a annulé le voyage.* | Because her mother was ill… |
| *Une fois la décision prise, il n'a plus hésité.* | Once the decision was made… |
| *Tous comptes faits, c'est un bon projet.* | All things considered… |

> **Rule:** the participle clause's subject must be **different** from the main-clause subject. With the same subject use the gerund or a *participe passé* clause (*Arrivé à Paris, il appela sa sœur*).

### 4.2 Detached participle and the dangling-modifier rule
In a sentence like *Arrivé à Paris, il appela sa sœur*, the participle refers to the subject of the main clause. Writing *Arrivé à Paris, la ville lui parut immense* is acceptable if "la ville lui parut" is understood as perceptual, but *Arrivé à Paris, son téléphone sonna* is wrong (the phone did not arrive).

### 4.3 Gerund vs. present participle (*gérondif* vs. *participe présent*)
| Form | Function | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **en + -ant** (gérondif) | adverbial (manner, time, means), same subject as main verb | *Elle chante en cuisinant.* |
| **-ant** (participe présent, no *en*) | verbal, replaces a relative or causal clause; invariable | *Un homme portant un chapeau* (= qui porte); *Étant malade, il…* |
| **-ant** adjective (*adjectif verbal*) | agrees in gender/number | *Une histoire passionnante; des enfants obéissants* |

**Agreement test:** if it can be replaced by *qui + verb* and takes an object → invariable participle (*des gens parlant français*). If it describes a quality and takes *très* → agrees (*des gens très intéressants*).

**Classic spelling pairs:** *différant/différent, précédant/précédent, négligeant/négligent, excellant/excellent, fatiguant/fatigant, convainquant/convaincant, communiquant/communicant.* (The participle keeps the verb's stem spelling, the adjective often differs.)

## 5. The *ne explétif* (Expletive *ne*)

A *ne* with **no negative meaning**, used in formal writing after certain verbs and conjunctions that carry an implicit idea of negation, fear, or prevention:

| Trigger | Example (formal) |
|---|---|
| **craindre / avoir peur / redouter que** | *Je crains qu'il ne vienne.* (I fear he may come.) |
| **empêcher / éviter que** | *Évitons que cela ne se reproduise.* |
| **à moins que** | *Je viendrai, à moins qu'il ne pleuve.* |
| **avant que** | *Partons avant qu'il ne fasse nuit.* |
| **de peur que / de crainte que** | *Il parle bas de peur qu'on ne l'entende.* |
| **douter / nier que** (in negative/interrogative) | *Je ne doute pas qu'il ne soit sincère.* |
| **comparatives of inequality** | *Il est plus malin que je ne pensais.* |

**Key facts**
- It is **optional** in modern French and the Académie française regards it as correct but not obligatory. It is increasingly omitted even in careful writing.
- It is **not** a true negation: *Je crains qu'il ne vienne* ≠ *Je crains qu'il ne vienne pas*. The second means "I fear he won't come".
- It must be distinguished from the real negation *ne… pas*. When in doubt, don't use it.

**Spanish contrast:** Spanish has no such device (*Temo que venga*). English has none either. Treat it as a French-specific formal flourish: **recognize always, produce rarely**.

## 6. Agreement Subtleties

### 6.1 Agreement of the past participle (review and extension from Lesson 70)
| Case | Rule | Example |
|---|---|---|
| *être* auxiliary | agrees with the subject | *Elles sont parties.* |
| *avoir* + direct object **before** the verb | agrees with the object | *Les lettres que j'ai écrites.* |
| pronominal verbs | agrees with the reflexive pronoun when it is a direct object | *Elle s'est lavée* / *Elle s'est lavé les mains* (the direct object *les mains* follows) |
| *faire* + infinitive | participle **invariable** | *Elle s'est fait couper les cheveux.* *Les fleurs que j'ai fait livrer.* |
| *laissé + infinitive* | invariable (since 1990 spelling, more flexible) | *Elle s'est laissé convaincre.* |
| verb + *en* | invariable | *Des erreurs, il en a fait.* |
| quantity *(coûté, pesé, vécu, couru, duré)* | invariable when meaning amount | *Les 20 euros que ce livre m'a coûté.* but figurative uses may agree: *Les efforts que ce travail m'a coûtés.* |

### 6.2 Agreement after collective subjects and quantity expressions
| Expression | Agreement | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **la plupart de** + plural | plural verb | *La plupart des étudiants sont venus.* |
| **un grand nombre de / beaucoup de** | plural | *Beaucoup de gens pensent…* |
| **la majorité de** + plural | singular or plural (both accepted) | *La majorité des gens pense / pensent…* |
| **un des… qui** | plural if the action concerns all; singular if only one | *C'est un des élèves qui ont réussi.* (several succeeded) vs. *C'est le seul élève qui ait réussi.* (only one) |
| **c'est / ce sont** | *c'est* + plural is accepted in speech; *ce sont* is formal with plural third person | *Ce sont mes amis.* / *C'est mes amis* (colloquial) |
| **Plus d'un** | singular | *Plus d'un étudiant a protesté.* |
| **Ni l'un ni l'autre** | singular or plural | *Ni l'un ni l'autre n'est venu / ne sont venus.* |
| **Le peu de** | depends on meaning | *Le peu d'effort qu'il a fait l'a sauvé* (effort exists) vs. *Le peu d'effort qu'il a fait ne l'a pas sauvé* (barely any) |
| Collective noun + complement | ambiguous, follow meaning | *Une foule d'enthousiastes l'acclamait / l'acclamaient.* |

### 6.3 *Tout* (variable or invariable)
- Adverb meaning "completely" before an adjective: invariable, except before a feminine adjective starting with a consonant or *h aspiré*: *Elle est tout heureuse* but *Elle est toute contente; toutes petites.*
- Adjective or pronoun: agrees (*tous les jours; toutes les filles; Ils sont tous venus*).

### 6.4 Adjective agreement subtleties
- **Colors from nouns** are invariable (*des chemises orange, marron, citron*), but not those used regularly as adjectives (*rose, mauve, pourpre* agree).
- **Compound colors** are invariable (*des yeux bleu clair, vert foncé*).
- **Demi/nu/feu** before the noun: invariable and hyphenated (*une demi-heure, nu-pieds, feu la reine*); after the noun: agree (*une heure et demie; pieds nus*).
- **Adjectives modifying several nouns:** the masculine plural prevails (*un garçon et une fille intelligents*); the adjective may agree with the nearest noun in literary style.

## 7. Register Logic in Grammar Choices

Grammar choices telegraph register. Compare the same idea in three registers:

| Register | Example |
|---|---|
| **Familier (spoken)** | *Tu sais s'il va venir ? / Y a pas de souci, on se capte demain.* |
| **Standard** | *Savez-vous s'il va venir ? / Il n'y a pas de problème, on se rappelle demain.* |
| **Soutenu (formal written)** | *Sauriez-vous me dire s'il viendra ? / Il n'y a nulle difficulté ; nous nous entretiendrons demain.* |

### 7.1 Features that raise register
- Inversion (*Est-ce là ce que vous souhaitiez ?*)
- Subjunctive in concession or doubt (*Bien qu'il soit…, Quoi qu'il en soit…*)
- *Ne* alone (without *pas*) with *pouvoir, savoir, oser, cesser*: *Il ne cesse de pleurer; Je ne saurais dire; Il n'ose parler.*
- *Ne… que* and *ne… guère, ne… point* (archaic or regional *point*).
- *Dont* and *duquel/auquel* relative forms.
- Relative clause with *lequel* after a preposition (*la raison pour laquelle…*).
- Absolute participle constructions.
- Pluperfect/passé antérieur narration.
- Nominalization (*la mise en œuvre de, la prise en compte de*), covered in Lesson 64.

### 7.2 Features that lower register
- *ne* dropping (*J'sais pas, c'est pas grave*).
- Question by intonation only (*Tu viens ?*) and *est-ce que* in the spoken norm.
- Dislocation (*Moi, je… ; Ça, c'est…; Mon frère, il…*).
- *on* replacing *nous*.
- *ça* replacing *cela*.
- Tag *non ?*, *hein ?*, *quoi ?* and fillers (Lesson 41).
- Verlan and slang (Lesson 47).

### 7.3 Decision rule
Ask three questions before choosing a structure:
1. **Reader/listener:** who are they and what relationship do I have to them?
2. **Medium:** spoken, written, official?
3. **Effect:** do I want to sound neutral, warm, authoritative, ironic?

When unsure, choose **standard** (neutral, safe) and add **one** marked feature at most per sentence.

## 8. Common Mistakes

**Spanish speakers:**
- Placing the subject after the verb as in Spanish (*Viene Pierre* → ✗ *Vient Pierre*; ✔ *Pierre vient*) outside licensed contexts.
- Using the subjunctive after *après que* (an error also common among natives; indicative is the norm, Lesson 58).
- Forgetting the -t- in inversion (*A-t-il…*).
- Applying Spanish gerund habits: *Estoy trabajando* ≠ *Je suis en train de travailler* (not *en travaillant*); gérondif is for manner/time, not for progressive aspect.
- Overusing *ne explétif* because "it sounds French".

**English speakers:**
- Calques of "-ing" clauses: *Marchant dans la rue, mon chapeau est tombé* (✗ dangling).
- Inversion errors in subordinate clauses.
- Confusing participle and adjective agreement (*une histoire amusant* ✗ → *amusante*).

**Both:**
- Over-using rare tenses in conversation (sounds like a textbook).
- Mixing registers within one sentence (*Sauriez-vous me dire si ça marche pas ?*).

## 9. Diagnostic: Fix the Sentence

Correct each sentence (some may be correct).

1. *Peut-être il viendra-t-il demain.*
2. *Dès qu'il eut fini, il est parti.*
3. *Je crains qu'il ne vient pas.*
4. *Arrivé à la gare, son train était déjà parti.*
5. *Les fleurs qu'elle a fait livrées sont arrivées.*
6. *Elles se sont téléphoné.*
7. *Une des étudiantes qui a réussi l'examen est ma sœur.* (meaning: several succeeded)
8. *Elle est tout contente.*
9. *Les enfants, fatiguants, se sont couchés tôt.*
10. *Aussi est-il resté, car il pleuvait.*

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. *Peut-être viendra-t-il demain.* or *Peut-être qu'il viendra demain.* 2. Mixed tenses: *Dès qu'il eut fini, il partit.* (or *Dès qu'il a eu fini, il est parti.*) 3. *Je crains qu'il ne vienne.* (subjunctive; *ne* without *pas*). 4. Dangling: *Arrivé à la gare, il constata que son train était déjà parti.* 5. *Les fleurs qu'elle a fait livrer…* (*faire* + infinitive: invariable, and *livrer* stays infinitive). 6. Correct: *Elles se sont téléphoné* (the pronoun is an indirect object: *téléphoner à quelqu'un*), so no agreement. 7. *Une des étudiantes qui ont réussi l'examen…* (plural verb if several succeeded). 8. *Elle est toute contente.* (tout agrees before a feminine adjective starting with a consonant). 9. *fatigués* (participle used as a predicate) or, if you mean "tiring," they are not tiring: *Les enfants, fatigués, se sont couchés tôt.* 10. Correct only if *aussi* means "therefore"; but the *car* clause gives the reason, so the logic is inverted: *Il pleuvait ; aussi est-il resté.*
</details>

## 10. Four-Week Deep-Dive Plan

| Week | Focus | Activities |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Recognize** | inversion, rare tenses | mark every inversion and passé antérieur/simple in 10 pages of a novel or an editorial; classify each by trigger |
| **2. Control** | participles, agreement | write 20 sentences with absolute clauses and gerunds; check agreement using the tables above and a reference grammar (*Grevisse*, *Bescherelle*, Académie française site) |
| **3. Produce** | register shifting | rewrite three paragraphs (one email, one news-style text, one literary passage of your own invention) across the three registers in Section 7 |
| **4. Consolidate** | error log and retrieval | review your error log (Lesson 70 method); redo the diagnostic in Section 9 without notes; write a 250-word formal text using at most 5 marked structures, correctly |

**Reference tools:** *Grevisse, Le Bon Usage*; *Bescherelle: La grammaire pour tous*; the Académie française "Dire, ne pas dire" pages; *Banque de dépannage linguistique* (Office québécois de la langue française) for fast, reliable answers.

## 11. Progressively Harder Practice

1. **Transform (15 min):** convert 10 standard sentences into formal ones using inversion, *ne* alone, or an absolute clause.
2. **Neutralize:** take a paragraph of formal prose and rewrite it in standard spoken French.
3. **Participle pairs:** write one sentence for each of the pairs *différant/différent*, *précédant/précédent*, *fatiguant/fatigant*.
4. **Agreement drill:** write 10 sentences with *faire* + infinitive, pronominal verbs, and *en* constructions, then check the agreement rules.
5. **Passé antérieur narrative:** write a 100-word narrative using *passé simple* and *passé antérieur* with *dès que* and *quand*.
6. **Editor's pass:** review a text you wrote months ago, find 10 improvements in structure or register.
7. **Teach-back:** explain the *ne explétif* rule aloud, in French, in under one minute, with three examples.

## 12. Mini Quiz (active recall)

1. After which adverbs does written French require inversion? Give three.
2. What is the difference between the *passé antérieur* and the *passé surcomposé*?
3. When is a present participle invariable?
4. What does the *ne explétif* mean? Does it negate the sentence?
5. Why is *Les lettres qu'il a fait écrire* invariable?
6. Name three features that lower register and three that raise it.

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. *peut-être, sans doute, à peine, ainsi, aussi (therefore), en vain, du moins.* 2. The *passé antérieur* is the literary form (passé simple of the auxiliary + participle, *il eut fini*); the *passé surcomposé* is its spoken/regional equivalent built on the passé composé (*il a eu fini*). 3. When it functions as a verb (replaces a relative or causal clause), it is invariable; as an adjective it agrees. 4. It has no negative meaning; it is a formal stylistic marker after verbs of fear, prevention, or conjunctions such as *à moins que* and *avant que*. 5. With *faire* + infinitive, the participle *fait* does not agree. 6. Lower: dropping *ne*, dislocation, *on* for *nous*, slang, tags. Raise: inversion, *ne* alone, subjunctive in concessions, absolute clauses, *lequel*/*dont* constructions.
</details>
