# Lesson 10 — The First 100 High-Frequency Words as Word Families
### Phase 1: Core System (A1 → A2)

**Competency axes trained:** 📚 Lexicon (primary) · 🗣️ Productive fluency
**Prerequisite:** Lessons 6–9 (verb system, articles, negation/questions)
**Time investment:** This is a reference + drill lesson — return to it across the week, don't try to absorb all 100 in one sitting

---

## 1. Why Word Families, Not Word Lists (Intuitive Explanation)

A raw vocabulary list ("bonjour = hello, chat = cat, manger = to eat...") is the least efficient way for an analytical learner like you to build vocabulary — each word sits isolated, with nothing to hook it to. Instead, this lesson organizes the highest-frequency ~100 words of French into **semantic families**: clusters of related words that reinforce each other. When you learn "manger" (to eat), you'll learn it alongside "la nourriture" (food), "le repas" (meal), "avoir faim" (Lesson 7) — one cluster, five words, each making the others easier to retain.

## 2. Family 1: Time & Frequency

| French | English | Spanish (for the bridge) |
|---|---|---|
| aujourd'hui | today | hoy |
| demain | tomorrow | mañana |
| hier | yesterday | ayer |
| maintenant | now | ahora |
| toujours | always | siempre |
| souvent | often | a menudo |
| parfois | sometimes | a veces |
| jamais | never | nunca |
| tôt / tard | early / late | temprano / tarde |

**False friend flag:** *toujours* looks like it should mean "every day" (tous les jours) but actually means "always" in most contexts — a subtle but important distinction.

## 3. Family 2: People & Relationships

| French | English |
|---|---|
| la famille | family |

| l'ami(e) | friend |

**Note:** *les parents* can mean either "parents" or "relatives" depending on context — a genuine ambiguity to watch for.

## 4. Family 3: Everyday Verbs (beyond the four pillars)

| French | English | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| vouloir | to want | irregular — full conjugation in a later lesson |
| pouvoir | to be able to/can | irregular |
| devoir | to have to/must | irregular |
| savoir | to know (facts/how to) | contrast with connaître below |
| connaître | to know (people/places) | Spanish splits this too: saber/conocer — direct parallel! |
| voir | to see |
| dire | to say/tell |
| penser | to think |
| croire | to believe |
| aimer | to like/love |

**Huge win from Spanish:** the savoir/connaître split maps almost perfectly onto Spanish saber/conocer — this is one of the cleanest direct transfers in the whole language, unlike English, which uses "to know" for both and gives English speakers real trouble here.

## 6. Family 5: Basic Descriptors (Adjectives)

| French | English | Note |
|---|---|---|
| grand(e) | big/tall | BAGS — goes before noun |
| petit(e) | small | BAGS — goes before noun |
| bon(ne) | good | BAGS — goes before noun |
| mauvais(e) | bad | goes before noun |
| beau/belle | beautiful | BAGS |
| nouveau/nouvelle | new | BAGS |
| difficile | difficult | goes after noun |
| facile | easy | goes after noun |
| important(e) | important | goes after noun |

This is your first real preview of the BAGS list mentioned in Lesson 3 — notice most of the "before the noun" exceptions are extremely short, high-frequency, everyday words, while longer/more technical adjectives (difficile, important) follow the "default after the noun" rule.

---

## 7. Family 6: Discourse Connectors (small words, huge value)

| French | English |
|---|---|
| et | and |
| mais | but |
| ou | or |
| parce que | because |
| donc | so/therefore |
| alors | so/then |
| aussi | also/too |
| encore | again/still |

These aren't "advanced" grammar — they're the glue that turns isolated sentences into actual conversation. Prioritize these heavily; they punch far above their weight in real communication.

---

## 8. Mental Model: Building a Web, Not a Chain

A list learns like a chain — break one link, lose the rest. A semantic web (what we just built) means forgetting one word still leaves you with 5–6 neighboring words that can help you reconstruct or paraphrase it. This is also exactly how native speakers' mental lexicons are organized — not alphabetically, but associatively.

---

## 9. Common Mistakes

**Spanish speakers:** confusing *savoir/connaître* boundaries occasionally even though the split exists in Spanish too, because the exact verbs that trigger each aren't 100% identical between the two languages; using *toujours* to mean "every day."

**English speakers:** collapsing savoir/connaître into one verb by English habit; placing adjectives like "difficile/important" before the noun by English word-order instinct.

## 10. Speaking/Thinking Practice

Pick one family above and build 3 original sentences combining at least 2 words from that family. Say them aloud.

**Thinking-in-French seed:** silently narrate your current moment using Family 1 + Family 4: "maintenant, je... " (now, I...) — even a fragment counts.
