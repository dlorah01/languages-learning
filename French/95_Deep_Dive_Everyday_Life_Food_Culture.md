# Lesson 95 — Deep Dive 15: Everyday Life, Food, and Culture in Practice
### Phase 5: Mastery & Maintenance — Optional Deep Dive

**Prerequisites:** Lesson 16 (tu/vous), Lesson 21 (first 200 words), Lesson 47 (full register command), Lesson 63 (Francophone variation), Lesson 69 (cultural literacy)

## 1. Why Daily-Life French Still Matters at C1

Advanced learners often master abstract topics but stumble over **ordering at a bakery, renting a flat, or being a guest at dinner**. These situations rely on **fixed formulas, rituals, and vocabulary** that textbooks skip. Natives judge you less by grammar than by whether you handle these small moments smoothly.

**Mental model: daily life is a set of scripts.** Each situation (bakery, restaurant, market, transport, dinner invitation, greeting neighbours) has a predictable sequence of moves and formulas. Learn the script once; run it automatically.

## 2. Greetings and Social Rituals

### 2.1 The universal rule: greet first
In shops, offices, lifts, waiting rooms, and doctors' surgeries, say **Bonjour** (and **Bonsoir** after about 6 pm) when you enter and **Au revoir** when you leave. Entering a shop and starting with a request without greeting seems rude.

| Moment | Formulas |
|---|---|
| **Entering** | *Bonjour, madame / monsieur.* / *Bonjour tout le monde.* |
| **Asking politely** | *Je voudrais…, s'il vous plaît.* / *Auriez-vous… ?* |
| **Thanking** | *Merci beaucoup.* / *Je vous remercie.* |
| **Leaving** | *Merci, bonne journée !* / *Bonne soirée !* / *Au revoir.* |
| **Wishing** | *Bon week-end !* / *Bonne continuation !* / *Bon courage !* / *Bonnes vacances !* |
| **Informal parting** | *À tout à l'heure, à plus, à demain, salut, ciao* |
| **Responding to thanks** | *De rien.* / *Je vous en prie.* / *Pas de souci.* |

*Bon courage* is said to someone facing a task (work, studying, a hard day); *bonne continuation* means "keep going well" and is used when parting.

### 2.2 La bise (cheek kisses)
Among friends and family (and often colleagues in informal workplaces), greeting with **la bise** is common. The number of kisses varies by region (commonly two in much of France; three or four in some areas) and by country (Belgium and Switzerland have their own customs). Practices differ by person and context, and handshakes are more common in formal or first-time contexts. **Follow the other person's lead.** (Public-health habits and personal preferences also change practice; it is fine to offer a handshake.)

### 2.3 Tu or vous in daily life
Shops, officials, and strangers: *vous*. Young people among themselves, friends, family, and many informal workplaces: *tu*. When in doubt, use *vous* and wait to be invited: *On peut se tutoyer ?* (Lesson 16).

## 3. Meals, Table, and Restaurant

### 3.1 Names of meals (France versus other regions)
| Meal | France | Belgium, Switzerland, Québec |
|---|---|---|
| Breakfast | *le petit-déjeuner* | *le déjeuner* |
| Lunch | *le déjeuner* | *le dîner* |
| Afternoon snack | *le goûter* (especially for children) | *la collation* (Québec), *le goûter* |
| Dinner | *le dîner* | *le souper* |

**Caution:** *déjeuner* and *dîner* shift by region. If someone invites you to *dîner*, confirm the time.

*L'apéritif* (*l'apéro*): drinks and nibbles before the meal; a social ritual, often lasting an hour.

### 3.2 At the restaurant
| Situation | Phrases |
|---|---|
| **Booking** | *Je voudrais réserver une table pour quatre personnes, à 20 heures.* |
| **Arriving** | *J'ai réservé au nom de… / Avez-vous une table pour deux ?* |
| **Asking for the menu** | *La carte, s'il vous plaît.* |
| **Ordering** | *Je vais prendre…; Pour moi, ce sera…; Je prendrai…* |
| **Asking** | *Qu'est-ce que vous me conseillez ?; C'est quoi, exactement ?* |
| **Dietary needs** | *Je suis végétarien(ne). / Je suis allergique à… / Est-ce qu'il y a du gluten/des arachides ?* |
| **Water** | *Une carafe d'eau, s'il vous plaît* (tap water, free) vs. *une bouteille d'eau* (paid) |
| **During** | *C'est délicieux. / C'est très bon.* |
| **Paying** | *L'addition, s'il vous plaît.* / *On peut payer séparément ?* |
| **Tip** | in France, service is normally included in the price (*service compris*); leaving a small extra amount is optional and appreciated, not obligatory |

**Terms to know:** *une entrée* (starter, **not** the main course), *un plat* (main course), *un dessert*, *le plat du jour* (dish of the day), *la formule* (fixed-price combination, e.g., *plat + dessert*), *le menu* (a set-price meal; **not** the list of dishes, which is *la carte*), *à la carte*, *un digestif*, *un café* (an espresso unless specified), *un café crème / un noisette*.

*Bon appétit !* is said before eating. Toasts: *Santé !* / *À la vôtre !* / *Tchin-tchin !* (Make eye contact when toasting.)

### 3.3 Cooking vocabulary
| Verb | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **éplucher** | to peel |
| **hacher / émincer** | to chop / to slice thinly |
| **couper en dés / en rondelles** | dice / slice in rounds |
| **faire revenir** | to sauté lightly |
| **faire sauter** | to stir-fry / toss |
| **mijoter** | to simmer |
| **faire bouillir** | to boil |
| **faire cuire (au four)** | to cook (bake) |
| **saisir** | to sear |
| **déglacer** | to deglaze |
| **battre / fouetter** | to beat / whisk |
| **pétrir** | to knead |
| **assaisonner** | to season |
| **égoutter** | to drain |
| **napper** | to coat with sauce |
| **faire fondre** | to melt |
| **laisser reposer** | to let rest |

**Measures:** *une cuillère à soupe* (tablespoon), *une cuillère à café* (teaspoon), *une pincée*, *une noix de beurre* (a knob of butter), *une gousse d'ail* (clove), *un brin de persil*, *un filet d'huile d'olive*, *un verre de*, *100 grammes de*. Temperature in °C, *thermostat* numbers for ovens (*thermostat 6* ≈ 180 °C).

### 3.4 Bakery, market, and shop
| Situation | Formulas |
|---|---|
| **Bakery** | *Bonjour, une baguette tradition, s'il vous plaît.* / *Deux croissants et un pain au chocolat.* (in the southwest, *chocolatine*; in Belgium, *couque au chocolat* is a related regional term) |
| **Market** | *C'est combien le kilo ?; Je vais prendre un demi-kilo de tomates; Elles sont mûres ?* |
| **Cheese shop** | *Un morceau de comté, pas trop fort / affiné / à point pour ce soir.* |
| **Butcher** | *Deux tranches de jambon, coupées fin.* |
| **Payment** | *Je peux payer par carte ?; Vous acceptez le sans-contact ?; Je n'ai pas de monnaie.* |
| **Bag?** | *Vous voulez un sac ?* |
| **Closing** | *Ce sera tout ?* → *Oui, ce sera tout, merci.* |

Typical store rhythms: many small shops close at lunchtime (*fermé de 12 h à 14 h*) and on Sundays or Mondays; larger supermarkets (*grandes surfaces*) have longer hours. Hours vary by town and country.

## 4. Housing and Daily Practicalities

### 4.1 Housing vocabulary
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **un appartement, un studio, un T2 / un F2, un deux-pièces** | flat, studio, two-room flat (T2/F2 = living room + 1 bedroom) |
| **le loyer, les charges** | rent, service charges (*charges comprises* = included) |
| **le bail** | lease |
| **le dépôt de garantie / la caution** | security deposit / guarantor |
| **l'état des lieux** | condition report at move-in/move-out |
| **le propriétaire / le locataire** | landlord / tenant |
| **une agence immobilière** | real-estate agency |
| **le syndic** | building manager (co-ownership) |
| **le gardien / la gardienne** | caretaker |
| **le digicode, l'interphone** | entry code, intercom |
| **le rez-de-chaussée, le premier étage** | ground floor, first floor (the floor **above** the ground floor) |
| **un ascenseur, un escalier** | lift, stairs |
| **le chauffage, l'eau chaude, l'électricité** | heating, hot water, electricity |
| **meublé / non meublé** | furnished / unfurnished |
| **un déménagement, déménager** | a move, to move house |

Note: floor numbering follows the European system: the **rez-de-chaussée** is the ground floor; *le premier étage* is one up. (Lesson 75 for administrative aspects: proof of address, *attestation d'assurance*, *contrat d'énergie*.)

### 4.2 Transport
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **le métro, le RER, le tramway, le bus** | metro, regional express (Paris), tram, bus |
| **le TER, le TGV, les Intercités** | regional train, high-speed train, intercity |
| **la SNCF** | French national rail operator |
| **un billet, un ticket, un titre de transport** | ticket |
| **un abonnement, un pass** | subscription |
| **valider / composter** | validate (a ticket) |
| **une correspondance** | connection/transfer |
| **un quai, une voie** | platform (by location), track |
| **un retard, une grève, un incident voyageur** | delay, strike, "passenger incident" |
| **aller simple / aller-retour** | one-way / return |
| **un covoiturage** | carpool |
| **le vélo en libre-service** | bike share |

Useful phrases: *Un aller-retour pour Lyon, s'il vous plaît; Quel est le prochain train pour… ?; De quel quai part le train ?; Le train a du retard.; Excusez-moi, c'est bien la ligne 4 ?*

### 4.3 Time off and holidays
*les vacances* (holidays), *les congés (payés)* (paid leave), *les RTT* (reduction-of-working-time days in France), *un jour férié* (public holiday), *faire le pont* (to take the day between a holiday and a weekend), *la rentrée* (return to school/work in September, a cultural moment), *le week-end*.

## 6. Being a Guest and Hosting

| Situation | Customs and phrases |
|---|---|
| **Invitation** | *Tu es libre samedi soir ? On t'invite à dîner.* / *On se fait un apéro chez nous.* |
| **Arrival time** | for a dinner, arriving 10–15 minutes after the stated time is normal in many circles in France; for a business meeting or formal event, be punctual |
| **What to bring** | wine, dessert, flowers (avoid chrysanthemums in France, associated with cemeteries; ask for local advice elsewhere); say *Je n'ai pas voulu venir les mains vides* |
| **At the table** | wait for the host: *On peut commencer ?*; compliment the food; accept a second helping: *Je reprendrais volontiers…* ; decline: *Merci, je n'en peux plus* (I'm full) |
| **Wine and water** | glasses are filled by the host; *Un peu d'eau ?* |
| **After the meal** | *café* and conversation can last long; leaving too early is unusual |
| **Thanks the next day** | *Merci encore pour cette excellente soirée !* |
| **Hosting** | *Installez-vous. / Faites comme chez vous. / Servez-vous. / Je vous sers un verre ?* |

Conversation topics: food, travel, culture, work, local news are common; direct questions about salary or politics are considered private among new acquaintances, although debate of ideas among friends is often lively (Lessons 87, 89).

## 8. Spanish ↔ French: Daily Life Contrasts

| Topic | Spanish / Latin American habit | French habit |
|---|---|---|
| **Meal times** | later dinners in Spain; varied in Latin America | dinner typically 19:30–21:00 in France |
| **Greeting** | *beso* customs differ by country; *abrazo* common in Latin America | *bise* varies; handshake in formal contexts |
| **Politeness formulas** | frequent warmth markers | *s'il vous plaît / merci / bonjour* are essential and expected |
| **Tipping** | varies, often 10 % in Colombia | included in France; small extra optional |
| **Water in restaurants** | bottled often | *carafe d'eau* is free on request in France |
| **Menu** | *menú del día* | *le menu* (set meal) vs. *la carte* (list) |
| **First floor** | *planta baja / primer piso* | *rez-de-chaussée / premier étage* (same logic) |

**False friends in daily life:** *une entrée* (starter, not main course), *le menu* (set meal, not the list), *une librairie* (bookshop, not library), *embarrassée* (flustered, not pregnant), *un car* (a coach/intercity bus, not a car: *une voiture*), *la carte* (card, map, or menu list), *la salle* (room) vs. *sale* (dirty).

## 9. Common Mistakes

**Spanish speakers:**
- Starting a request without *Bonjour*.
- Using *tu* with shopkeepers or staff.
- Saying *Je voudrais un café* when you want a "café con leche": ask *un café crème* or *un café au lait* depending on place.
- Translating "con mucho gusto" literally; use *Avec plaisir* or *Volontiers*.
- Mixing *dîner* and *déjeuner* across regions.
- Confusing *le menu* and *la carte*.

**English speakers:**
- Overusing *s'il vous plaît* but omitting *Bonjour*.
- Saying *Je suis plein* (vulgar sense for animals/pregnant women) for "I'm full": say *Je n'ai plus faim* or *Je suis rassasié(e)*.
- Calquing "How are you?" as an obligatory opener: *Ça va ?* is a greeting and expects a brief answer.

**Both:**
- Over-literal use of formal vocabulary in casual settings (and vice versa).
- Ignoring shop opening hours and local rhythms.

## 10. Four-Week Deep-Dive Plan

| Week | Focus | Activities |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Scripts** | greetings and shops | write and rehearse four scripts (bakery, market, café, pharmacy) aloud; run them in front of a mirror or with a partner |
| **2. Table** | restaurant and cooking | role-play ordering, allergy questions, and paying; cook a French recipe while narrating each step aloud in French |
| **3. Home and travel** | housing and transport | role-play a flat visit with questions on rent and charges; plan a train trip in French (route, tickets, delays) |
| **4. Social** | invitations and customs | host or attend a (real or simulated) dinner in French; write a thank-you message the day after (Lesson 51) |

**Resources:** French-language cooking videos and blogs (with captions), supermarket flyers, municipal websites, restaurant menus, train booking pages, real-estate listings, and recipe books from different Francophone regions.

## 11. Progressively Harder Practice

1. **Scripts (10 min):** write the dialogue for buying bread and two pastries and paying by card.
2. **Menu decoding:** read a restaurant menu online and list 15 dishes you could not translate at sight; look up the preparation words.
3. **Recipe aloud:** read a recipe and then explain it from memory in French without the text.
4. **Housing ad:** read a rental ad and prepare five questions to ask the landlord.
5. **Complaint at a restaurant:** write and say a polite complaint (*Excusez-moi, le plat est froid / Il y a une erreur sur l'addition*).
6. **Invitation:** write a text message to invite a friend for an *apéro* and the reply accepting.
7. **Culture note:** compare one daily ritual in Colombia with France (200 words in French).
