# Lesson 90 — Deep Dive 10: The Writing Studio — Long-Form, Editing, and Personal Style

**Prerequisites:** Lesson 51 (writing emails and short texts), Lesson 64 (essays and argumentation), Lesson 65 (rhetoric), Lesson 70 (orthography), Lesson 85 (advanced grammar)
**Time investment:** One read, then a four-week deep-dive cycle (Lesson 80, Section 7).

---

## 1. From Correct to Distinctive

At C1 your French is largely correct. The next gap is not mistakes but **texture**: the text is clear, yet it sounds translated, flat, or repetitive. Closing that gap is mostly **editing**, not writing. Professional writers produce a rough draft quickly and then revise in layers.

**Mental model: writing is a pipeline with separate stages.** Mixing stages (polishing commas while still deciding what to say) is the main cause of slow, painful writing.

| Stage | Question | Output |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Plan** | What do I want the reader to understand or do? | outline (3–7 points) |
| **2. Draft** | Can I get the ideas down without stopping? | rough text, errors tolerated |
| **3. Macro-revision** | Is the structure right? | reordered, cut, or expanded sections |
| **4. Meso-revision** | Do paragraphs and transitions work? | tightened paragraphs |
| **5. Micro-revision** | Are sentences and words precise? | polished sentences |
| **6. Proofreading** | Are spelling, grammar, and typography correct? | clean final text |

Do the stages **in this order**, and do not skip back.

---

## 2. Macro-Revision: Structure

Ask of every long text:
1. **Can I state the main point in one sentence?** If not, the text is not ready.
2. **Does each section serve that point?** Cut or move what doesn't.
3. **Is the order logical?** (chronological, problem → solution, general → specific, cause → effect)
4. **Is the opening effective?** In French essays and reports, an *accroche* (hook) followed by the *problématique* (the question the text addresses) is standard (Lesson 64).
5. **Is the ending more than a repetition?** A good conclusion answers the question and may open a perspective (*ouverture*).

**Reverse outline:** after drafting, write one sentence per paragraph summarizing it. Read the list: it reveals gaps, repetitions, and misplaced paragraphs.

---

## 3. Meso-Revision: Paragraphs and Transitions

A paragraph (*paragraphe*) carries one idea: a topic sentence, support, example, and a link to the next paragraph.

| Problem | Symptom | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| **Two ideas in one paragraph** | the paragraph drifts | split it |
| **Idea spread over several paragraphs** | thin, repetitive paragraphs | merge them |
| **Missing transitions** | abrupt jumps | add a connector or a bridging sentence |
| **Mechanical connectors** | *premièrement, deuxièmement, troisièmement* in every text | vary or remove: often the logic itself links the sentences |
| **Connector overload** | *de plus, en outre, par ailleurs* every sentence | keep only those that mark a real logical step |

**Useful connectors by logic (review):** addition (*de plus, en outre*), contrast (*pourtant, en revanche, cependant*), cause (*car, puisque, parce que*), consequence (*donc, par conséquent, c'est pourquoi*), concession (*certes… mais, bien que*), illustration (*par exemple, ainsi*), conclusion (*en somme, bref*).

> **Tip:** French prose often relies on **implicit logic** between sentences. Too many connectors can sound heavy; a well-placed one is stronger than five.

## 4. Micro-Revision: Sentences and Words

### 4.1 Replace "balloon verbs" with precise verbs
The verbs *être, avoir, faire, mettre, aller, prendre, donner* and constructions with *il y a / c'est* are essential but overused in drafts. Replace them when a sharper verb exists.

| Weak draft | Stronger revision |
|---|---|
| *Il y a beaucoup de problèmes dans ce projet.* | *Ce projet souffre de nombreux problèmes.* |
| *Cette loi fait une grande différence.* | *Cette loi change profondément la situation.* |
| *Elle a fait une présentation de ses résultats.* | *Elle a présenté ses résultats.* |
| *On a mis en place un système.* | *Nous avons instauré un système.* |
| *C'est un document qui contient les règles.* | *Ce document énonce les règles.* |
| *Il a pris la décision de partir.* | *Il a décidé de partir.* |

### 4.2 Balance verbs and nouns (nominalization)
French formal writing favors nouns (*la mise en œuvre de, la prise en compte de*), but a chain of nominalizations becomes heavy:

> *La réalisation de l'évaluation de la mise en place du dispositif a été effectuée.* ✘
> *Nous avons évalué la mise en place du dispositif.* ✔

**Rule:** if three *de* appear in a row, convert one noun back into a verb.

### 4.3 Cut the padding
| Padding | Tighter |
|---|---|
| *à l'heure actuelle* | *aujourd'hui, actuellement* |
| *dans le cas où* | *si* |
| *en raison du fait que* | *parce que* |
| *il est important de noter que* | (often delete) |
| *afin de pouvoir* | *pour* |
| *le fait que* | often delete or restructure |
| *une grande majorité de* | *la plupart de* |
| *au niveau de* | *pour, dans, en matière de* |

### 4.4 Vary sentence length and rhythm
A text made only of long sentences tires the reader; only short ones sound choppy. Mix them: a long sentence developing an idea, then a short one that lands the point. French likes **rhythm in threes** (*clair, précis, efficace*) and **balanced clauses** (*non seulement… mais aussi…*).

### 4.5 Avoid repetition (but not at the cost of clarity)
- Use pronouns, demonstratives (*ce dernier, celui-ci*, sparingly), and synonyms (*le texte, le document, l'article*).
- Don't force synonyms in technical writing: repeat the key term to stay precise.
- Check repeated sentence openers: *Il … Il … Il …*

### 4.6 Concrete beats abstract
Replace vague abstractions with images, numbers, or examples.

| Vague | Concrete |
|---|---|
| *Cette situation pose de nombreuses difficultés.* | *Cette situation oblige chaque famille à renoncer à une partie de son salaire.* (only if true in your text) |
| *Le service est de qualité.* | *Le service répond en moins d'une heure.* |

### 4.7 Control the voice
- **Active voice** is the default; use the passive when the agent is unknown or unimportant (Lesson 31).
- **Avoid the vague *on***: *On pense que…* (who?). Use it deliberately for common sense or for *nous* in an informal tone.
- **Avoid stacked relatives:** *le projet que le comité que nous avons nommé a approuvé*; break it up.

### 4.8 Calques and false friends (check list)
Check typical calques from Spanish and English: *réaliser* (to realize vs. *se rendre compte*), *éventuellement*, *actuellement*, *supporter*, *assister*, *librairie/bibliothèque*, *sensible*, *introduire* (*présenter*), *aplicar* → *appliquer / poser sa candidature*, *realizar* → *réaliser / effectuer*, *en base a* → *sur la base de, d'après* (Lessons 70, 76, 78).

## 5. Typography and Punctuation in French

French typography differs from English and Spanish in several visible ways.

| Feature | French norm | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Space before `: ; ? !`** | a (non-breaking) space before and after | *Quelle heure est-il ? Voici : …* |
| **Quotation marks** | *guillemets français* « » with spaces inside | *Il a dit : « Je viens. »* |
| **Quotation within quotation** | “ ” inside « » | « Il m'a dit “non”. » |
| **Dialogue** | dash (*tiret cadratin*) for turns, or « » | *— Tu viens ? — Oui.* |
| **Capitalization** | days, months, languages, nationalities-as-adjectives lowercase; nationalities-as-nouns capitalized | *lundi, janvier, le français; un Français, un livre français* |
| **Titles of works** | usually only the first word and proper nouns are capitalized, but many established titles keep traditional capitals | *La recherche du temps perdu* (modern style) vs. *Le Petit Prince* (traditional); check a reference |
| **Numbers** | decimal comma; space as thousands separator | *3,5 ; 12 000 €* |
| **Abbreviations** | *M.* (Monsieur), *Mme*, *Dr*, *n° 5*, *1er, 2e* | |
| **Ellipsis** | three points, no space before | *Et alors…* |
| **Lists** | semicolons between items, full stop at the end | |

*Note:* the rules for non-breaking spaces and capitalization have regional variants (Quebec, for instance, has its own conventions for some cases). In professional work follow the house style or the Académie française and the *Lexique des règles typographiques* of the Imprimerie nationale.

## 6. Finding Your Voice

Voice = the recognizable pattern of your choices: sentence length, vocabulary, humor, directness, imagery.

### 6.1 Methods
| Method | How |
|---|---|
| **Pastiche** | imitate a writer's style on a new topic (a paragraph a week); you learn their moves |
| **Active reading** | when a sentence impresses you, copy it by hand, then analyze *why* it works |
| **Style journal** | 5 sentences a day in French about anything; polish one |
| **Constraint writing** | write 100 words without the verb *être*, or in one sentence, or with only short sentences |
| **Register shifts** | rewrite the same paragraph formal, neutral, and informal (Lesson 85, Section 7) |
| **Read aloud** | the ear catches awkward rhythm the eye misses |
| **Translation back** | translate your French paragraph literally into Spanish/English; where it sounds odd, your French may be calqued |

### 6.2 Voice and authenticity
At C1–C2 you should sound like **yourself in French**, not like a textbook or a native you imitate. A voice grows from many small decisions you make consciously, then unconsciously.

## 7. Long-Form Writing Strategies

For texts of 1,000+ words (reports, articles, personal projects):

1. **Outline hierarchically** (parts, sections, key points).
2. **Write the easiest section first** to build momentum; the introduction last.
3. **Use signposting** (*Dans un premier temps…, J'aborderai ensuite…*) and **summary sentences** at section ends.
4. **Keep a term bank:** decide the key terms and keep them consistent.
5. **Track sources** and cite correctly (*selon X (année)*, *d'après*, footnotes).
6. **Schedule revision passes** (day 1 draft, day 2 macro, day 3 micro, day 4 proofreading).
7. **Get feedback** from a tutor, a native reader, or a correction tool, and log recurring problems.

## 8. Tools (Use as Assistants, Not Authors)

| Tool type | Use |
|---|---|
| **Grammar and style checkers** (e.g., LanguageTool, Antidote, Bon Patron; check which suit you and their terms) | catch errors and suggest style fixes; **review every suggestion** critically |
| **Dictionaries** (Larousse, Robert, CNRTL/TLFi, Wiktionnaire) | meaning, register, examples |
| **Synonym dictionaries** (CRISCO, Larousse synonymes) | alternatives; check usage |
| **Banque de dépannage linguistique (OQLF)** | quick, reliable answers on grammar, usage, typography |
| **Bescherelle/Grevisse/Académie** | grammar and conjugation reference |
| **Concordancers and corpora** | check whether a collocation is natural (Lesson 49) |
| **AI assistants** | useful to brainstorm, explain, and critique, but verify output, and keep your own writing practice; do not outsource the revision that trains you |

## 9. Common Mistakes

**Spanish speakers:**
- Long, heavily subordinated sentences copied from Spanish prose.
- Repetition of *que* chains.
- Using the gérondif (*en + -ant*) to chain sequential actions, as Spanish sometimes allows with the gerund: *Il est entré en fermant la porte* means simultaneous actions, not "he entered and then closed".
- Overuse of *muy* equivalent (*très*) rather than precise adjectives.
- Calques of Spanish verb + preposition patterns (Lesson 50).
- Wrong typography (spaces before `? !`, quotation marks).

**English speakers:**
- Over-compression and vague noun stacks.
- Using the passive too often.
- Missing connectors between sentences.

**Both:**
- Editing too early, losing the flow.
- Never reading aloud.
- Trusting the grammar checker blindly.
- Padding with empty phrases.

## 10. Four-Week Deep-Dive Plan

| Week | Focus | Activities |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Draft and diagnose** | baseline | write a 600-word text on a topic you know; leave it for a day; diagnose problems with Sections 2–4 |
| **2. Revise in layers** | macro → meso → micro | produce 3 successive revisions; keep all versions; note what each pass changed |
| **3. Voice** | pastiche and constraints | 3 short exercises from Section 6; read aloud; compare to your Week 1 text |
| **4. Publish-ready** | long-form + proofreading | write a final 800-word text (article, essay, or profile); proofread typography; get feedback |

**Revision checklist (tick each):** main point in one sentence? reverse outline logical? each paragraph one idea? connectors purposeful? balloon verbs replaced? nominalizations balanced? padding cut? sentence rhythm varied? calques checked? typography correct? read aloud?

## 11. Progressively Harder Practice

1. **Tighten (15 min):** rewrite more concisely: *À l'heure actuelle, il y a un grand nombre de personnes qui font le choix de ne plus utiliser leur voiture.*
2. **Verb upgrade:** replace *faire / mettre / avoir* in five sentences from your own drafts.
3. **Rhythm exercise:** take a paragraph of long sentences and break it into a mix of long and short; read aloud.
4. **Reverse outline:** produce one for a previous text of yours; reorder if needed.
5. **Pastiche:** write 120 words describing your morning in the style of a writer you admire, then name three features you imitated.
6. **Typography proofread:** correct the spaces and quotation marks: *Il a dit: "Viens!" et j'ai répondu : "pourquoi?"*
7. **Full cycle:** plan, draft, and revise an 800-word piece across four days; compare the first and final versions.
