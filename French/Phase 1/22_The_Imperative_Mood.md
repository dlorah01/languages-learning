# Lesson 22 — The Imperative Mood: Giving Commands and Instructions
### Phase 1: Core System (A1 → A2)

**Competency axes trained:** 🧩 Morphosyntax (primary) · 🗣️ Productive fluency
**Prerequisite:** Lesson 6 (regular conjugation), Lesson 16 (tu/vous register)
**Time investment:** One read-through — this is a short, high-payoff structural lesson

---

## 1. Why the Imperative Is Simple News for Once (Intuitive Explanation)

After several dense lessons, here's a relief: the French imperative is one of the *easiest* structures in the language, and it maps almost identically onto Spanish's command forms conceptually. It's simply the present tense with the subject pronoun dropped, plus one small spelling quirk for -ER verbs.

---

## 2. Formation: Present Tense Minus the Subject

The imperative exists in three forms — tu, nous, vous — corresponding to "you (informal)," "let's," and "you (formal/plural)":

| Infinitive | tu form | nous form | vous form |
|---|---|---|---|
| parler | Parle! | Parlons! | Parlez! |
| finir | Finis! | Finissons! | Finissez! |
| vendre | Vends! | Vendons! | Vendez! |

**The one quirk:** -ER verbs (and a few others like "aller") **drop the final -s** from the tu form: present tense "tu parles" → imperative "**Parle!**" (no -s). This exists purely for historical pronunciation reasons and is the single spelling exception to memorize.

---

## 3. Irregular Imperatives (the four pillars again — Lesson 7 payoff)

| Verb | tu | nous | vous |
|---|---|---|---|
| être | Sois! | Soyons! | Soyez! |
| avoir | Aie! | Ayons! | Ayez! |
| savoir | Sache! | Sachons! | Sachez! |
| vouloir | Veuille (rare) | — | Veuillez (formal "please") |

**High-value formal expression:** *veuillez* + infinitive is the extremely formal/written way to say "please" — you'll see it constantly in official emails, signage, and instructions:
> **Veuillez patienter.** — "Please wait." (formal/written register)

---

## 4. Negative Commands (Lesson 3 callback)

Standard ne...pas wraps around the imperative exactly as expected:
> **Ne parle pas!** — "Don't speak!"
> **N'ayez pas peur.** — "Don't be afraid." (formal)

---

## 5. Pronoun Placement Shifts! (An Exception to Lesson 3's Rule)

Here's a genuine exception worth flagging clearly: in **affirmative** commands, object pronouns move **after** the verb (attached with a hyphen) — the opposite of their normal position (Lesson 3):

> **Donne-moi le livre.** — "Give me the book." (pronoun AFTER, hyphenated)
> compare: **Tu me donnes le livre.** — "You give me the book." (pronoun BEFORE, normal statement)

But in **negative** commands, pronouns return to their normal pre-verb position:
> **Ne me donne pas le livre.** — "Don't give me the book." (pronoun BEFORE, as expected)

This mirrors a similar shift in Spanish imperative pronoun placement (dame vs. no me des) — so the *pattern* of "pronouns move for affirmative commands only" should already feel intuitive, even though the specific mechanics differ.

---

## 6. Mental Model: The Imperative as "The Sentence With Its Head Cut Off"

Picture a normal statement (Tu parles) as having a "head" (the subject pronoun) — the imperative simply **removes that head**, leaving the verb to stand alone and do the commanding. The one twist (pronouns jumping to the other side of the verb in affirmative commands) happens because, without a subject in front, French needs the sentence to still "flow" naturally — pronouns hop to the back and attach directly, almost like they're keeping the verb company since the subject is gone.

---

## 7. Common Mistakes

**Spanish speakers:** forgetting the -ER verb's dropped final -s (a French-specific quirk without a clean Spanish parallel); occasionally placing pronouns using Spanish's own specific hyphenation/accent rules rather than French's hyphen-only convention.

**English speakers:** trying to keep the subject pronoun in commands ("Tu parle!" instead of "Parle!") since English commands don't require dropping "you" as visibly as French requires dropping "tu."

---

## 8. Progressively Harder Examples

1. Écoute! — "Listen!"
2. Finissons ce travail ensemble. — "Let's finish this work together."
3. Ne sois pas triste. — "Don't be sad."
4. Donne-le-moi. — "Give it to me." (stacked pronouns, hyphenated — advanced, don't worry about mastering the stacking order yet)
5. Veuillez patienter quelques instants, s'il vous plaît. — "Please wait a few moments, please." (formal/written register, high-frequency in official contexts)
