# Lesson 25 — Phase 1 Capstone Review: Full Interleaved Assessment
### Phase 1: Core System (A1 → A2) — Final Checkpoint

**Competency axes trained:** All Phase 1 axes, fully interleaved
**Purpose:** Close out Phase 1 with a comprehensive, mixed-order retrieval test covering Lessons 6–24, plus a narrative production task that forces you to combine everything simultaneously.

---

## 1. Why This Capstone Matters

This is the last lesson of Phase 1. Everything from here forward (Phase 2: Expansion) assumes total fluency with what's below — not recognition, but instant, unconscious production. This lesson tests that directly, then closes with a full narrative task combining every system you've built.

---

## 2. Mixed Retrieval Test (attempt all before checking answers)

1. Conjugate "vendre" fully in the present tense.
2. What are the four "pillar verbs," and which two build the passé composé?
3. Give one noun where French/Spanish gender genuinely mismatches.
4. Negate: "Nous mangeons de la viande" using ne...jamais.
5. What's the difference between savoir and connaître?
6. Why can "son livre" mean both "his" and "her" book?
7. Conjugate "aller" in the passé composé for "elle," including agreement.
8. Explain, conceptually, when to use passé composé vs. imparfait.
9. Contract "à + les" and "de + le."
10. What's the irregular comparative of "bon"?
11. When should you default to "vous" with someone new?
12. What's the correct verb for "to make a decision," and why is the literal translation wrong?
13. Where does negation go in "aller + infinitive"?
14. What's the vigesimal quirk in French numbers 70–99?
15. What's the false-friend trap in "déçu"?
16. What spelling change happens to -ER verb imperatives (tu form)?
17. Where do object pronouns go in affirmative vs. negative commands?
18. What auxiliary do ALL reflexive verbs take in the passé composé?
19. What does "en" replace, and why is it hard for English/Spanish speakers to remember?
20. Give the fixed order of stacked object pronouns.

---

## 3. Answer Key

1. vends / vends / vend / vendons / vendez / vendent
2. être, avoir, faire, aller — être and avoir build the passé composé.
3. la mer (fem.) vs. Spanish el mar (masc.).
4. Nous ne mangeons jamais de viande.
5. Savoir = facts/how-to; connaître = people/places (parallels Spanish saber/conocer).
6. Because son/sa/ses agree with the gender of the object owned, not the owner.
7. Elle est allée.
8. Passé composé = completed/bounded action; imparfait = ongoing/habitual/background state.
9. aux; du.
10. meilleur.
11. Always, until a native speaker signals otherwise.
12. prendre une décision — "faire" is a literal but incorrect transfer from hacer/make.
13. Around "aller" itself (je ne vais pas partir), not around the infinitive.
14. 70–99 build on a base-20 (vigesimal) pattern inherited from Gaulish (soixante-dix, quatre-vingts...).
15. "Déçu" means "disappointed," not "deceived."
16. It drops the final -s (tu parles → Parle!).
17. Affirmative: after the verb, hyphenated. Negative: before the verb, as normal.
18. Être, always, regardless of the verb itself.
19. "En" replaces a "de + noun" or a quantity; English/Spanish don't grammatically require an equivalent placeholder.
20. me/te/se/nous/vous → le/la/les → lui/leur → y → en.

## 4. Narrative Production Task (the real test)

Narrate, out loud or in writing, **"My yesterday and my plans for tomorrow"** — roughly 8–10 sentences — deliberately including:

- At least 2 reflexive verbs (Lesson 23)
- At least one passé composé with avoir and one with être, with correct agreement (Lesson 12)
- At least one imparfait for background/habitual description (Lesson 13)
- At least one négation structure beyond simple pas (Lesson 9)
- At least one object pronoun (le/la/les, lui, y, or en) (Lesson 24)
- At least one use of "aller + infinitive" for a future plan (Lesson 19)
- Correct tu/vous register awareness if any other person is mentioned (Lesson 16)

This single exercise forces every Phase 1 system to work together simultaneously — exactly the kind of integrated production that separates "knowing grammar" from "having French."

---

## 5. Self-Assessment & Phase 1 Wrap-Up

Score the retrieval test out of 20, and honestly assess the narrative task against its 7 checklist items. Weak areas here are worth revisiting in their source lesson before Phase 2 begins — Phase 2 builds directly on top of every one of these systems without re-teaching them.

**You've now completed the entire core grammatical engine of French**: full present-tense conjugation, the four pillar verbs, gender/articles/determiners, negation and questions, two past tenses with the aspect distinction, prepositions, comparatives, register, the imperative, reflexives, and the complete object pronoun system — plus roughly 200+ words of interconnected vocabulary. This is a genuinely major milestone; most learners take considerably longer to reach unconscious command of this much structural ground.
