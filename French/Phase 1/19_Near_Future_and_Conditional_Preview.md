# Lesson 19 — The Immediate Future & Conditional Preview: Aller + Infinitive in Depth
### Phase 1: Core System (A1 → A2)

**Competency axes trained:** 🧩 Morphosyntax (primary) · 🗣️ Productive fluency
**Prerequisite:** Lesson 7 (aller conjugation)
**Time investment:** One thorough read + production drills

---

## 1. Why We Deepen This Now (Intuitive Explanation)

Lesson 7 gave you the near future (futur proche) as a quick bonus: aller + infinitive. Now that you have negation, questions, and both past tenses under control, it's time to make this future tense fully productive — and to preview its conceptual cousin, the conditional, which you'll formally learn in Phase 2 but should be able to recognize now.

---

## 2. The Near Future, Fully Activated

**Formula:** aller (present tense) + infinitive

| Person | Form |
|---|---|
| je vais | + infinitive |
| tu vas | + infinitive |
| il/elle/on va | + infinitive |
| nous allons | + infinitive |
| vous allez | + infinitive |
| ils/elles vont | + infinitive |

> **Je vais partir demain.** — "I'm going to leave tomorrow."
> **Nous allons manger bientôt.** — "We're going to eat soon."

**Negation wraps around "aller," not the infinitive** (Lesson 3's verb-cluster logic applies to the conjugated part):
> **Je ne vais pas partir.** — "I'm not going to leave." (NOT "je vais ne pas partir")

**Questions work exactly as expected:**
> **Est-ce que tu vas venir?** — "Are you going to come?"

---

## 3. Near Future vs. the "Real" Future (Futur Simple) — a preview

French has a second future tense, the futur simple (je partirai, tu partiras...), which you'll learn formally in Phase 2. For now, know this distinction conceptually — it mirrors a similar (though not identical) distinction in Spanish between "voy a + infinitivo" and "-aré/-ás" endings:

- **Aller + infinitive (near future)**: used constantly in speech, feels immediate/planned, parallels Spanish "voy a + infinitivo" almost exactly.
- **Futur simple**: more formal/written, or for more distant/uncertain future, parallels Spanish "-aré" endings.

**Practical guidance for now:** default to aller + infinitive for essentially all future expression in speech — it's correct, natural, and universally understood, exactly like relying on "voy a" in Spanish conversation. We'll build the futur simple properly in Phase 2.

---

## 4. Conditional Preview (recognition only — full lesson in Phase 2)

You'll also start encountering the conditional (je voudrais, j'aimerais, je pourrais) in everyday polite speech well before its formal grammar lesson — so let's name it now so you recognize it:

> **Je voudrais un café.** — "I would like a coffee." (polite request — extremely high frequency)
> **Pourriez-vous m'aider?** — "Could you help me?" (formal politeness)

**Structural preview:** conditional forms look like futur simple stems + imparfait endings (a pattern you'll see fully explained in Phase 2) — for now, just recognize "voudrais/pourrais/aimerais" as **polite/softened versions** of vouloir/pouvoir/aimer, directly parallel to Spanish's own conditional politeness forms (querría/podría — though Spanish more commonly uses the imperfect subjunctive "quisiera" for this function, a nuance worth noting).

---

## 5. Mental Model: Near Future as a "Committed Plan," Conditional as a "Softened Wish"

Picture the near future (aller + infinitive) as stating a **committed plan** — "this is going to happen." The conditional, by contrast, is a **softened, hypothetical, or polite** register — "this would happen" or "I would like." Even without full conditional grammar yet, you can already start using "je voudrais" instead of the blunter "je veux" ("I want") to sound more natural and polite in everyday requests — a genuinely high-value, low-effort upgrade to your spoken French starting today.

---

## 6. Common Mistakes

**Spanish speakers:** placing "ne...pas" around the infinitive instead of around "aller" (transfer error from Lesson 3's principle being under-applied here); occasionally forgetting that French politeness leans on the conditional even more heavily than Spanish's "quisiera" pattern in certain everyday contexts (ordering food, making requests).

**English speakers:** overusing "je veux" (I want) instead of "je voudrais" (I would like) in polite contexts — grammatically fine but socially blunter than native speakers typically prefer, similar to the English distinction between "I want a coffee" and the politer "I'd like a coffee."

---

## 7. Progressively Harder Examples

1. Je vais appeler ma mère ce soir. — "I'm going to call my mother tonight."
2. Nous n'allons pas sortir aujourd'hui. — "We're not going to go out today."
3. Est-ce qu'ils vont venir à la fête? — "Are they going to come to the party?"
4. Je voudrais réserver une table pour deux. — "I would like to book a table for two."
5. Qu'est-ce que tu vas faire demain? Je vais essayer de finir ce projet, et j'aimerais me reposer un peu après. — "What are you going to do tomorrow? I'm going to try to finish this project, and I'd like to rest a bit afterward."
