# Lesson 17 — Deep-Dive Verb Study: Prendre, Mettre, Donner
### Phase 1: Core System (A1 → A2)

**Competency axes trained:** 📚 Lexicon (primary) · 🧩 Morphosyntax (secondary)
**Prerequisite:** Lessons 6–7 (verb systems), Lesson 12 (passé composé)
**Time investment:** One thorough read + return to this as a reference as these verbs appear constantly

---

## 1. Why These Three Verbs (Intuitive Explanation)

Prendre, mettre, and donner are among the most versatile verbs in French — each one covers a huge semantic territory that English and Spanish often split across several different verbs. Learning them deeply now, rather than as isolated dictionary entries, gives you enormous conversational range for relatively little memorization effort.

---

## 2. Prendre — "To Take" (and much more)

**Conjugation (irregular):** je prends, tu prends, il prend, nous prenons, vous prenez, ils prennent. Past participle: **pris** (avoir).

**Core meanings beyond "take":**
- **Transportation:** prendre le bus/le train/l'avion — "to take the bus/train/plane"
- **Food/drink:** je prends un café — "I'll have a coffee" (ordering — very common in cafés/restaurants)
- **Time:** ça prend du temps — "it takes time"
- **Decisions:** prendre une décision — "to make a decision" (NOT "faire une décision" — a common learner error)

**False friend/collocation warning:** "prendre une décision," not "faire" — this is one of many fixed verb-noun pairings you simply need to memorize as chunks, since the "logical" English/Spanish translation (make/hacer) doesn't transfer.

---

## 3. Mettre — "To Put" (and much more)

**Conjugation (irregular):** je mets, tu mets, il met, nous mettons, vous mettez, ils mettent. Past participle: **mis** (avoir).

**Core meanings beyond "put":**
- **Clothing:** mettre un manteau — "to put on a coat"
- **Time investment:** j'ai mis deux heures à finir — "it took me two hours to finish" (mettre + time = "to take [duration]," a genuinely useful idiomatic use)
- **Turning on:** mettre la radio/la télé — "to turn on the radio/TV"

**Reflexive extension (preview of a later lesson):** se mettre à + infinitive = "to start [doing something]" — se mettre à pleurer ("to start crying").

---

## 4. Donner — "To Give" (more straightforward, but with key collocations)

**Conjugation:** regular -ER verb (Lesson 6 pattern) — je donne, tu donnes, il donne, nous donnons, vous donnez, ils donnent. Past participle: **donné** (avoir).

**High-value collocations:**
- **donner un coup de main** — "to lend a hand / help out" (literal: "give a hit of hand" — a vivid idiom worth memorizing as a chunk)
- **ça me donne envie de...** — "it makes me want to..." (extremely common structure for expressing desire triggered by something)
- **donner rendez-vous** — "to arrange to meet"

---

## 5. Mental Model: Verbs as Hubs With Many Spokes

Instead of picturing "prendre = take" as a single fixed translation, picture each of these verbs as a **hub** with multiple "spokes" radiating outward — each spoke a different context-specific meaning that native speakers select automatically based on the surrounding words. Your job isn't to memorize a single translation per verb, but to build recognition of which "spoke" is active based on context (transportation? food? decision-making? time?).

---

## 6. Common Mistakes

**Spanish speakers:** using "hacer" logic and saying "faire une décision" instead of "prendre une décision"; occasionally over-translating "tomar" 1:1 for every use of prendre, missing the mettre-specific "put on clothing" and "turn on device" meanings that Spanish splits across ponerse/prender differently.

**English speakers:** trying to use separate verbs for each of prendre/mettre's many meanings (English does split "take a bus" / "make a decision" / "take time" across different collocations too, so the instinct to expect one-verb-per-meaning is strong, but wrong here).

---

## 7. Progressively Harder Examples

1. Je prends le métro tous les jours. — "I take the metro every day."
2. Elle a mis son manteau avant de sortir. — "She put on her coat before going out."
3. Il m'a donné un coup de main pour déménager. — "He helped me move." (lit. "gave me a hand")
4. Ça a pris deux heures, et ça m'a donné envie de dormir. — "It took two hours, and it made me want to sleep." (both verbs, idiomatic uses, in one sentence)
5. Nous avons pris une décision difficile, mais elle a mis fin au problème. — "We made a difficult decision, but it put an end to the problem." (mettre fin à = "to put an end to")
