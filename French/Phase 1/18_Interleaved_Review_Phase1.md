# Lesson 18 — Interleaved Review: Phase 1 Consolidation (Lessons 6–17)
### Phase 1: Core System (A1 → A2) — Review Checkpoint

**Competency axes trained:** All Phase 1 axes, interleaved
**Purpose:** Spaced repetition and interleaving (Lesson 0's learning-science principles) applied deliberately — this lesson exists purely to force retrieval across everything learned so far, out of the order it was taught, which is proven to strengthen long-term retention far more than re-reading in sequence.

---

## 1. Why Interleaved Review, and Why Now

You've just completed 12 dense lessons (6–17) covering the entire core grammatical engine of French: verb conjugation, the four pillar verbs, gender/articles, negation/questions, vocabulary families, determiners, both past tenses, prepositions, comparatives, register, and three deep-dive verbs. Before moving into Lesson 19 and beyond, this checkpoint mixes everything together — deliberately out of original order — so your brain has to *retrieve* rather than just *recognize*.

## 3. Answer Key

1. finissons / finissez
2. vais / vas / va
3. Feminine (la mer) — mismatches Spanish's masculine "el mar," a genuine gender divergence from Lesson 8.
4. They elide to l' (l'ami, l'université).
5. Je ne mange jamais de viande.
6. Est-ce que tu pars?
7. toujours / jamais / parfois
8. Savoir = to know facts/how to; connaître = to know people/places (parallels Spanish saber/conocer).
9. Because son/sa/ses agree with the gender of the object owned, not the owner's gender.
10. Before a feminine noun starting with a vowel sound, "ma" becomes "mon" for pronunciation ease (e.g., mon amie).
11. Être; yes, it agrees (elle est allée).
12. faire → fait; voir → vu.
13. Il pleuvait quand je suis sorti.
14. "J'ai su" = found out (sudden realization); "je savais" = knew (ongoing state).
15. à + les = aux; de + le = du.
16. Because it means "at [person]'s place," a concept English/Spanish don't lexicalize in one word.
17. meilleur.
18. Adverb — it modifies the verb "chante," not a noun, so it doesn't agree/inflect like an adjective.
19. Vous — start formal and let the other person signal a shift to tu.
20. Because vous covers both formal singular AND all plural "you," unlike Spanish's more differentiated tú/usted/ustedes system.
21. "Faire" is wrong; correct is "prendre une décision."
22. Ça m'a donné envie de dormir.
