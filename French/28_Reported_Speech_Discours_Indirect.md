# Lesson 28 — Reported Speech: Discours Indirect
### Phase 2: Expansion (A2 → B1)

**Competency axes trained:** 🧩 Morphosyntax (primary) · 🔗 Discourse competence
**Prerequisite:** Lessons 12–13 (both past tenses), Lesson 19 (near future)
**Time investment:** One thorough read + practice converting real dialogue into reported form

---

## 1. Why Reported Speech Deserves Its Own Lesson (Intuitive Explanation)

Reporting what someone else said — "she said that she was tired," "he asked if I was coming" — requires shifting pronouns, tenses, and sometimes time expressions all at once. This is conceptually familiar from Spanish (estilo indirecto), but French has its own specific tense-shifting rules worth mapping explicitly.

---

## 2. Direct vs. Indirect Speech: The Basic Shift

> **Direct:** Elle dit: « Je suis fatiguée. » — "She says: 'I'm tired.'"
> **Indirect:** Elle dit **qu'**elle est fatiguée. — "She says that she's tired."

When the reporting verb is in the **present** (elle dit que...), the tense inside the reported clause **doesn't change** — this is the easy case.

---

## 3. The Harder Case: Reporting in the Past (Tense Backshift)

When the reporting verb is in the **past** (elle a dit que...), French — like Spanish and English — shifts the reported tense "back" one step:

| Direct speech tense | Becomes (in past-tense reported speech) |
|---|---|
| présent | imparfait |
| passé composé | plus-que-parfait (a new tense — preview below) |
| futur simple | conditionnel présent |
| futur proche (aller + inf.) | imparfait of aller + infinitive |

> **Direct:** Il a dit: « Je suis fatigué. » → **Indirect:** Il a dit qu'il **était** fatigué. (présent → imparfait)
> **Direct:** Il a dit: « J'ai fini. » → **Indirect:** Il a dit qu'il **avait fini**. (passé composé → plus-que-parfait — new!)
> **Direct:** Il a dit: « Je viendrai. » → **Indirect:** Il a dit qu'il **viendrait**. (futur → conditionnel)
> **Direct:** Il a dit: « Je vais partir. » → **Indirect:** Il a dit qu'il **allait** partir. (aller present → aller imparfait)

**This mirrors Spanish's own backshift system almost exactly** (dijo que estaba cansado, dijo que había terminado, dijo que vendría) — another high-fidelity transfer.

---

## 4. Preview: Plus-Que-Parfait ("Had Done")

You'll meet this tense formally later, but you need it now for reported speech: it's formed exactly like the passé composé (Lesson 12), but with the auxiliary in the **imparfait** instead of the present:

> **j'avais mangé** — "I had eaten" (avoir in imparfait + past participle)
> **j'étais allé(e)** — "I had gone" (être in imparfait + past participle, with the same agreement rules from Lesson 12)

---

## 5. Reported Questions

Reported yes/no questions use **si** ("if/whether"):
> Direct: « Tu viens? » → Indirect: Il m'a demandé **si** je venais.

Reported information questions keep their question word, but drop the est-ce que/inversion structure (Lesson 9 callback) and revert to normal statement word order:
> Direct: « Où habites-tu? » → Indirect: Il m'a demandé **où** j'habitais.
> Direct: « Qu'est-ce que tu fais? » → Indirect: Il m'a demandé **ce que** je faisais. (note: "qu'est-ce que" becomes "ce que" in reported form — a small but important quirk)

---

## 6. Pronoun and Possessive Shifts (obvious but easy to forget mid-sentence)

Just as in Spanish/English, pronouns and possessives shift to match the new speaker's perspective:
> Direct: Marie: « **Mon** frère vient. » → Indirect: Marie a dit que **son** frère venait. (mon → son, Lesson 11 callback)

---

## 7. Mental Model: Reported Speech as a Time Machine Adjustment

Picture the original quote as a snapshot taken at one moment in time. When you report it later, from a new vantage point, everything inside that snapshot needs to shift "one step into the past" relative to your new position — present becomes imparfait, passé composé becomes plus-que-parfait, future becomes conditional. It's the same event, just viewed through a different temporal lens, exactly like Spanish's own backshift logic already trained you to expect.

---

## 8. Common Mistakes

**Spanish speakers:** minimal conceptual risk — the backshift logic transfers cleanly. Watch for the "qu'est-ce que" → "ce que" quirk specifically, since Spanish doesn't have an equivalent surface-level change to flag.

**English speakers:** forgetting to backshift at all (colloquial English increasingly skips backshift — "she said she's tired" instead of "was tired" — a habit that doesn't transfer to French, which requires the shift more consistently).

---

## 9. Progressively Harder Examples

1. Il dit qu'il a faim. — "He says he's hungry." (present reporting verb, no shift needed)
2. Elle a dit qu'elle était contente. — "She said she was happy." (présent → imparfait)
3. Ils ont dit qu'ils avaient déjà mangé. — "They said they had already eaten." (passé composé → plus-que-parfait)
4. Je lui ai demandé s'il viendrait à la fête. — "I asked him if he would come to the party." (futur → conditionnel, plus si-question)
5. Elle m'a demandé ce que je pensais de son projet, et j'ai dit que je le trouvais excellent. — "She asked me what I thought of her project, and I said I found it excellent."
