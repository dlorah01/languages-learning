# Lesson 79 — Final Capstone: Mastery Assessment and Your Long-Term Plan
### Phase 5: Mastery & Maintenance (C1 → C2+) — Core Curriculum Finale

**Competency axes trained:** All eight axes (assessment and planning)
**Covers:** the whole program (Lessons 1–78)
**Time investment:** 4–6 hours, split across **at least three sittings** over a week.

---

## 1. What This Capstone Is (and Is Not)

This is a **diagnostic and a celebration**. It is not an official exam; it is a structured way to see where you really are across the eight competency axes and to design the next stage of your French life. Be honest: a low score in one area is information, not failure, and nobody can measure your level perfectly from a self-test.

**You will:**
1. Complete **eight short tasks**, one per axis.
2. **Score yourself** with rubrics, and ideally get an outside opinion (tutor or native speaker).
3. Compare with your earlier recordings and writings.
4. Map your results to CEFR-style descriptors.
5. Write your **long-term plan** (Section 11).

> **Reminder:** the CEFR levels (A1–C2) are broad descriptions. Real ability varies by skill and context. Official certification (DELF/DALF, TCF, TEF) gives an external check when you need one.

---

## 2. Setup

- **Materials:** a recorder, a timer, a notebook (or file) for the error journal, your mining log, and your earlier recordings (Lessons 59, 62, 67, 72).
- **Conditions:** do each task without notes first. Allow a dictionary only where indicated.
- **Record everything** you can, so you can listen back and so that future-you has a benchmark.

## 3. Task 1: Phonology and Prosody (Lessons 1, 4, 5, 59)

**Task (3 minutes):**
1. Read this passage aloud twice (second time with the best rhythm you can):
   > *Le matin, quand je me lève, je prends mon café en regardant par la fenêtre. Il y a souvent du brouillard, mais vers dix heures, le soleil réussit à percer les nuages. Mes voisins, qui travaillent tous à l'étranger, rentrent rarement avant la nuit. C'est un quartier calme, où l'on entend surtout les oiseaux.*
2. Record a 1-minute free description of your neighborhood.

**Self-check (0–4 each):**
| Criterion | 0–1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Vowels (/y/, /ø/, /œ/, nasals) | frequent substitutions | some errors | rare errors | consistently accurate |
| /ʁ/ and consonants | noticeably foreign | acceptable | clear | native-like |
| Liaison and schwa | frequent errors | partial control | mostly natural | natural |
| Rhythm and intonation | word-by-word, falling endings | uneven | mostly in groups | natural groups and continuation rises |

## 4. Task 2: Morphosyntax (Lessons 6–31, 58, 70)

**Part A (20 minutes, no notes).** Choose or conjugate correctly.

1. Hier, il (pleuvoir) quand nous (sortir). (imparfait / passé composé)
2. Si j'(avoir) plus de temps, je (voyager) davantage.
3. Il faut que tu (venir) et que nous (finir) avant midi.
4. Je cherche quelqu'un qui (savoir) coder.
5. Les photos qu'elle (prendre) étaient magnifiques. (agreement)
6. Elles se sont (parler) et (embrasser). (agreement)
7. J'espère qu'il (venir) demain.
8. Bien qu'elle (être) fatiguée, elle continue.
9. Il m'a dit qu'il (partir) le lendemain.
10. Remplacez par des pronoms : *Je donne le livre à Paul.* / *Nous parlons de ce projet.* / *Elle a vu les enfants hier.*

**Part B (5 minutes).** Correct the errors:
1. *J'ai peur de que tu partes.*
2. *Je suis allé à la librairie pour emprunter un livre.*
3. *Les lettres qu'il m'a écrit sont arrivés.*
4. *Elle est très sensible, elle prend toujours des décisions rationnelles.*

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

**Part A.** 1. *Il pleuvait quand nous sommes sortis.* 2. *Si j'avais plus de temps, je voyagerais davantage.* 3. *viennes / finissions.* 4. *sache.* 5. *prises.* 6. *parlé / embrassées.* (*se parler* = COI, no agreement; *s'embrasser* = COD, agreement.) 7. *viendra.* 8. *soit.* 9. *partirait.* 10. *Je lui donne le livre. / Nous en parlons. / Elle les a vus hier.*
**Part B.** 1. *J'ai peur que tu partes.* 2. *bibliothèque* (not *librairie*). 3. *écrites / arrivées.* 4. *raisonnable* or *sensée* instead of *sensible* (false friend).
</details>

**Score:** Part A out of 10 (+ pronouns bonus), Part B out of 4. Convert to 0–4: 90%+ = 4, 75–89% = 3, 55–74% = 2, below = 1.

## 5. Task 3: Lexicon, Idioms, and Collocations (Lessons 10, 21, 34, 48, 49, 53, 73)

**Part A (10 minutes).** Complete or translate.

1. Choose the natural verb: ___ une décision / ___ une question / ___ un examen / ___ attention / ___ rendez-vous.
2. Idioms: poser un ___ · coûter les yeux de la ___ · avoir un chat dans la ___ · mettre son grain de ___ · jeter l'___.
3. Proverbs: Chat échaudé... · Petit à petit... · Qui sème le vent...
4. Say in French: "to implement", "to rely on", "to consist in", "to take into account".
5. Explain the difference: *actuellement / en fait*, *assister à / aider*, *éventuellement / finalement*, *une conférence / une réunion*.
6. Near-synonyms: *content / heureux / ravi / comblé* (rank by intensity); *agacé / énervé / furieux / en colère* (rank by intensity).

**Part B (5 minutes).** Describe in French, without naming it: *un tournevis, le chômage, une télécommande* (circumlocution).

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. *prendre / poser / passer / faire / prendre.* 2. *lapin / tête / gorge / sel / éponge.* 3. *...craint l'eau froide. / ...l'oiseau fait son nid. / ...récolte la tempête.* 4. *mettre en œuvre; reposer sur / s'appuyer sur; consister à; tenir compte de / prendre en compte.* 5. *Actuellement* = currently / *en fait* = actually; *assister à* = attend / *aider* = assist; *éventuellement* = possibly / *finalement* = eventually; *une conférence* = lecture / *une réunion* = meeting. 6. content < heureux < ravi < comblé; agacé < énervé < en colère < furieux.
</details>

**Score:** out of about 30 points → 0–4 as above.

## 6. Task 4: Pragmatics and Register (Lessons 16, 47, 54, 65, 66)

**Task (15 minutes).**

1. Rewrite "I need that file now" in three registers (formal, neutral, casual).
2. Decode the subtext: *Ce n'est pas mal.* / *On verra.* / *Avec tout mon respect…* / *Tu parles d'une idée !* / *Bravo, champion !*
3. Write (or record) a 3-sentence polite disagreement with a colleague's proposal, using concession and a softener.
4. Role-play (2 minutes): a customer asks for a refund politely but firmly; you are the employee. Switch to the customer.
5. React naturally with backchannels (record yourself) to three short statements from a partner or podcast.

**Self-check (0–4):**
| Criterion | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Register control | appropriate *tu/vous*, vocabulary, and formulas |
| Politeness and softening | conditional, concessions, hedges |
| Subtext recognition | correctly decoding irony and understatement |
| Interactional naturalness | backchannels, turn-taking, no over-formality |

## 7. Task 5: Discourse and Writing (Lessons 33, 51, 64, 75)

**Task A (45 minutes, timed).** Write an argumentative text of **300–350 words** on:

> *« Le télétravail doit-il devenir la norme ? »*

Include: a hook, a *problématique*, a two-part plan announced in the introduction, at least **six different connectors**, one *certes… mais* concession, one subjunctive after *bien que* or *il faut que*, a *bilan* and an *ouverture*.

**Task B (15 minutes).** Write a formal email (120–150 words) to an administration requesting a document and asking for a deadline. Use at least one conditional, a closing formula, and a clear *objet*.

**Self-check (0–4):**
| Criterion | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Structure | clear plan, paragraphs with one idea each |
| Cohesion | varied and accurate connectors, transitions |
| Accuracy | agreement, tenses, mood, spelling (use the six-pass method, Lesson 70) |
| Range and style | precise verbs, nominalization, no weak verbs |

Ask a tutor or a native speaker to review at least Task A.

## 8. Task 6: Listening Decoding (Lessons 45, 56, 63, 74)

**Task (30 minutes).**

1. Choose **two** authentic clips of 3 minutes each, one from a French-France source (e.g., a *franceinfo* bulletin or a podcast) and one from a different Francophone region (Québec, Belgium, Switzerland, or Africa).
2. For each: listen once with no transcript and write a 5-sentence summary in French.
3. Listen again and list 8 specific details (names, numbers, opinions).
4. Check against the transcript or subtitles (if available) and compute your detail accuracy.
5. Identify three places where reduction, liaison, or accent hid a word.

**Self-check (0–4):**
| Criterion | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Gist | accurate summary on first listening |
| Detail | % of details caught (≥85% = 4; 70–84% = 3; 50–69% = 2; below 50% = 1) |
| Variety | comprehension of non-standard accents |
| Speed and register | following fast, informal speech |

## 9. Task 7: Productive Fluency and Thinking in French (Lessons 68, 71)

**Task (30 minutes).**

1. **Anecdote (90 seconds):** tell a story from your life using the six-move structure (hook, setting, complication, escalation, climax, resolution), including narrative present and two direct quotes.
2. **Spontaneous monologue (3 minutes, no preparation):** *« Parlez-moi de vos projets pour les cinq prochaines années. »*
3. **Interaction (10 minutes):** hold a conversation with a native speaker or tutor. Practice at least: one interruption, one change of topic, one clarification request, and one question back.
4. **Circumlocution test:** your partner names three abstract words (e.g., *la confiance, une rupture, la fierté*); you define them in French without using the words.

**Self-check (0–4):**
| Criterion | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Fluency | continuous speech, few long pauses |
| Interaction | turn-taking, backchannels, repair |
| Spontaneity | little silent translation, circumlocution when stuck |
| Accuracy under speed | tenses, agreement, prepositions in real time |

Count **switches to Spanish/English** and **pauses over 3 seconds** in the monologue and compare with Lesson 62's baseline.

## 10. Task 8: Culture, Reading, and Literary Competence (Lessons 55, 60, 69, 73, 74, 76)

**Part A: Reading (30 minutes).** Read one page (about 400 words) of an authentic text you have not seen before (a novel excerpt, an essay, or a long-form article). Then:
1. Summarize in 5 sentences.
2. Identify **three** stylistic or structural features (tense choices, connectors, figures of speech, long sentences).
3. List **five words or expressions** you learned and give a new sentence for each.
4. Rate your comprehension (% of the text understood without a dictionary).

**Part B: Cultural literacy (10 minutes).** Answer briefly in French:
1. What is *Matignon*, and what does *cohabitation* mean?
2. What is the *bac*, and what is *la rentrée*?
3. What is *un tartuffe*? What is *la Bérézina*?
4. Name two differences between French in France and in Québec (or Belgium/Switzerland).
5. Explain neutrally one point of the debate on anglicisms or inclusive writing.

**Self-check (0–4):**
| Criterion | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Reading comprehension | ≥95% = 4; 85–94% = 3; 70–84% = 2 |
| Textual analysis | noticing structure and style |
| Cultural references | recognizing allusions and institutions |
| Critical awareness | framing, register, variation |

### CEFR descriptors at a glance
| Level | Summary of what you can do |
|---|---|
| **B2** | understand complex texts on concrete and abstract topics; interact with spontaneity and fluency with native speakers; produce clear, detailed text |

## 12. Compare, Reflect, Celebrate

**Comparison exercise (30 minutes).** Listen to your recordings from:
- Lesson 59 (pronunciation baseline)
- Lesson 62 (goals talk)
- Lesson 67 and 72 (review production)
- This capstone (Task 7)

Then answer, in French, in writing or aloud:
1. Qu'est-ce qui a le plus changé dans ma façon de parler ?
2. Quelles sont mes trois erreurs les plus récurrentes aujourd'hui ?
3. Quel type de situation reste difficile pour moi (téléphone, débat rapide, humour, texte littéraire, etc.) ?
4. De quoi suis-je le plus fier(ère) dans mon parcours ?
5. Qu'est-ce que le français m'a apporté, au-delà de la langue ?

**Celebrate.** You started at A1 and studied the sound system, the grammar, the vocabulary, the culture, the registers, and the habits of a French speaker. Whatever your scores, that is a significant accomplishment. Mark it in a way that is meaningful to you: a French meal, a film, a message to a French-speaking friend, a trip.

## 13. Your Long-Term Plan

### 13.1 Choose your next stage

| Goal | What to do |
|---|---|
| **Official certification** | Prepare for **DELF B2**, **DALF C1** or **C2**; use past papers and a tutor; plan 8–12 weeks of focused practice |
| **Professional use** | Practice real tasks (meetings, presentations, emails, reports) with colleagues; shadow French-language webinars in your field |
| **Study or migration** | Check requirements (DELF/DALF or TCF/TEF scores); practice academic writing and oral presentations |
| **Literary and cultural depth** | Establish a reading plan (one novel per month), join a book club, attend films and talks |
| **Native-like intuition** | Increase volume and variety: live in French for stretches, pursue hobbies in French, converse regularly |
| **Maintenance** | Keep a light but regular routine (below) so the level does not decay |

### 13.2 A sustainable maintenance routine (about 3–4 hours/week)

| Frequency | Activity |
|---|---|
| **Daily (15–20 min)** | podcast or news bulletin; 10 pages of reading; inner monologue |
| **Weekly** | one conversation (tutor, partner, or friend); one writing piece (email, journal, review); one film or series episode without subtitles |
| **Monthly** | recorded 2-minute monologue; error journal review; one new genre (poem, essay, long-form article) |
| **Quarterly** | re-run a mini self-assessment (Tasks 3, 5, 7); adjust the plan |
| **Annually** | repeat this capstone or an official test; set new goals |

## 14. Final Mental Model: The Language as a Home You Now Live In

At the beginning, French was a foreign country you visited with a phrasebook. Now it is a **home you are moving into**: you know the rooms (grammar, vocabulary, registers), the neighbors (regional varieties, cultural references), and the house rules (politeness, rhythm, rhetoric). From here, mastery is not about finishing but about **living there**: cooking, arguing, joking, and daydreaming in French, and gradually noticing that you no longer think of it as a "foreign" language.
