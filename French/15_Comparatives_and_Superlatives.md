# Lesson 15 — Comparatives and Superlatives
### Phase 1: Core System (A1 → A2)

**Competency axes trained:** 🧩 Morphosyntax (primary) · 📚 Lexicon (secondary)

---

## 1. Why This Is a Quick Win (Intuitive Explanation)

Comparatives and superlatives in French are structurally simple and map extremely closely onto Spanish, so this lesson is deliberately compact — a chance to consolidate confidence after several dense grammar lessons in a row.

---

## 2. The Three-Way Comparative Structure

| Structure | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| plus...que | more...than | Il est plus grand que moi. |
| moins...que | less...than | Elle est moins timide que lui. |
| aussi...que | as...as | Tu es aussi intelligent que ton frère. |

Directly parallel to Spanish **más...que / menos...que / tan...como** — nearly a 1:1 structural transfer, just with different connector words (que instead of como for the "as...as" form, which is the one irregularity to note).

---

## 3. The Superlative

**Formula:** definite article (le/la/les) + plus/moins + adjective (+ de for "in/of [group]")

> **C'est le plus grand bâtiment de la ville.** — "It's the tallest building in the city."
> **Elle est la moins patiente de la famille.** — "She's the least patient in the family."

Note the article agrees with the noun (Lesson 8 logic again), and "de" (not "dans," despite English "in") introduces the comparison group — another small but important preposition-specific fact from Lesson 14.

---

## 4. Irregular Comparatives/Superlatives (memorize — very high frequency)

| Adjective | Comparative | Superlative |
|---|---|---|
| bon (good) | meilleur (better) | le meilleur (the best) |
| mauvais (bad) | pire (worse) — or plus mauvais | le pire (the worst) |

**Adverb irregular:** bien (well) → mieux (better) → le mieux (the best)

**Key distinction:** *meilleur* (adjective, agrees with noun: meilleure, meilleurs, meilleures) describes nouns, while *mieux* (adverb, invariable) describes verbs/actions:
> **un meilleur film** — "a better film" (adjective)
> **il chante mieux** — "he sings better" (adverb)

This adjective/adverb split has a direct Spanish parallel: mejor (adjective) vs. mejor (Spanish actually uses the same word for both — so this is one place French draws a distinction Spanish doesn't mark as clearly, closer to English's good/better vs. well/better split).

---

## 5. Mental Model: A Balance Scale

Picture plus/moins/aussi as a **balance scale** tipping up, down, or staying level between two things being compared, with "que" marking the second item on the scale. The superlative is simply the scale pushed to its absolute extreme, anchored by an article showing you're now singling out one item from a whole group (marked by "de").

---

## 6. Common Mistakes

**Spanish speakers:** using "como" instead of "que" for the "as...as" structure (aussi grand que, not "aussi grand comme"); occasionally confusing meilleur/mieux since Spanish "mejor" covers both roles.

**English speakers:** trying to use "-er/-est" endings instead of the plus/moins periphrastic structure (French has no equivalent of "taller/tallest" as a suffix pattern for most adjectives); using "dans" instead of "de" for superlative groups.

---

## 7. Progressively Harder Examples

1. Paris est plus grand que Bogotá? Non, c'est le contraire. — "Is Paris bigger than Bogotá? No, it's the opposite."
2. Mon français est meilleur qu'avant. — "My French is better than before."
3. C'est le meilleur restaurant de la ville. — "It's the best restaurant in the city."
4. Elle parle aussi bien que moi, mais elle écrit mieux. — "She speaks as well as I do, but she writes better."
5. C'est la pire journée de ma vie. — "It's the worst day of my life."
