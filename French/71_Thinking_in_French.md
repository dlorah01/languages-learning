# Lesson 71 — Thinking in French: Inner Monologue and Translation-Free Cognition
### Phase 5: Mastery & Maintenance (C1 → C2+)

**Competency axes trained:** 🗣️ Productive fluency (primary) · 🔗 Discourse competence · 📚 Lexicon
**Prerequisite:** Lesson 49 (collocations), Lesson 62 (mastery system), Lesson 68 (spoken storytelling)
**Time investment:** One read, then a daily 10-minute practice for at least eight weeks.

---

## 1. What "Thinking in French" Actually Means (Intuitive Explanation)

"Thinking in French" is often described as a mystical state. It is more concrete: it means that **when you need to express an idea, French words and structures arrive directly, without a Spanish or English version assembled first.** Cognitive scientists describe this as **reduced co-activation and translation mediation**: the concept connects to the French form directly.

Important facts to correct common myths:

- You **do not** need to stop thinking in Spanish or English. Bilinguals keep all languages active; they get better at selecting one.
- It is **gradual**, not a switch. You might think in French for greetings and routine tasks first, then for opinions, then for emotions, then for specialized content.
- It is **trained by use**, not by explanation. You will not read your way into it.
- Even fluent speakers sometimes translate in difficult situations (tired, stressed, unfamiliar topic). That is normal.

**The goal:** raise the percentage of your daily thinking that happens directly in French, and speed up retrieval of French chunks under pressure.

---

## 2. Why Translating Slows You Down

When you translate, you do four steps:

1. Think the idea in Spanish/English.
2. Find the equivalent words.
3. Fix the grammar and word order.
4. Speak.

Direct thinking does one step: **idea → French chunk → speech.** The path is shorter because the connection between concepts and French chunks has become automatic through repeated use, much like the way you do not translate when you read your native language.

**The key unit is the chunk, not the word.** Natives don't build sentences word by word; they retrieve ready-made phrases (*Il faut que je...*, *Ça dépend de...*, *Je n'ai pas eu le temps de...*) and fill in the blanks. Your inner French voice is built from thousands of such chunks.

---

## 3. The Five Stages of Direct Thinking

| Stage | What it feels like | Typical activities |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Labeling** | You name things in French as you see them | labels in your home, naming objects while walking |
| **2. Commenting** | Short inner comments: *Il fait froid. J'ai faim.* | narrating small actions |
| **3. Planning** | Inner lists and plans in French | to-do lists, shopping lists, daily plan |
| **4. Reasoning** | Weighing options and opinions in French | pro/con lists, debating yourself |
| **5. Feeling** | Emotions and spontaneous reactions in French | journaling, exclamations, reacting to news |

You do not need to finish a stage to start the next, but moving along the stages in order tends to feel natural.

---

## 4. The Eight-Week Training Plan

### Weeks 1–2: Labeling and commenting
- **Label your environment:** name 10 objects you see each hour in French (silently or aloud). Add gender: *le frigo, la fenêtre, un ordinateur*.
- **Commentary:** describe your actions in short sentences: *Je prépare le café. Je cherche mes clés. Je suis en retard.*
- **Phone change:** set your phone, apps, and your assistant to French (Lesson 62).

### Weeks 3–4: Planning and short monologues
- **Morning plan in French:** *Aujourd'hui, je dois..., Ensuite, je vais..., Ce soir, j'aimerais...*
- **Lists in French:** shopping, tasks, ideas.
- **Voice notes:** record a 1-minute daily summary: *Aujourd'hui, j'ai...*

### Weeks 5–6: Reasoning and opinions
- **Pro/con lists in French** for small decisions: *D'un côté..., de l'autre...*
- **Explain your reasoning aloud:** *Je choisis X parce que..., Cependant..., Au final...*
- **Mental debates:** argue both sides of a topic in your head (Lesson 64 connectors).

### Weeks 7–8: Emotion, creativity, and spontaneity
- **Evening journal (5 minutes) in French**, with emotions (Lesson 54).
- **React in French:** when something surprises you, say *Oh là là !*, *Mince !*, *Zut !* (mild), *C'est pas vrai !*
- **Creative play:** invent dialogues, tell yourself stories, daydream in French.
- **Dream log:** write short notes about dreams in French. Eventually some dreams include French.

**Weekly measure (Lesson 62):** rate yourself 0–10 on "How much of today's inner speech was in French?" Track the trend.

## 5. Specific Techniques

### 5.1 Circumlocution: Never Stop, Go Around
When a word does not come, do not switch to Spanish/English; **describe it in French** (the single most important fluency skill):

| Missing word | Circumlocution |
|---|---|
| *un tournevis* | *C'est l'outil qu'on utilise pour visser...* |
| *le chômage* | *Quand quelqu'un n'a pas de travail et reçoit une aide...* |
| *une chose* (any) | *le truc, le machin, ce... comment dire...* |

**Useful phrases:**
- *Comment dit-on... ?* / *Comment ça s'appelle ?*
- *C'est un truc qui sert à...* / *C'est comme un... mais pour...*
- *Je cherche le mot... c'est quand on...*
- *Enfin, vous voyez ce que je veux dire.*

Over time, you learn the actual word, but the habit of staying in French is what trains direct thinking.

### 5.2 Think in Chunks (Collocations, Lesson 49)
Instead of translating *"I have to leave"*, retrieve the chunk **« Il faut que je parte »**. Collect chunks by situation:
- **Starting:** *Alors, voilà...*, *Je voulais te dire que...*
- **Hesitating:** *Euh, comment dire...*, *C'est-à-dire que...*
- **Agreeing:** *C'est vrai, tu as raison.*
- **Excusing:** *Désolé, j'ai complètement oublié.*
- **Evaluating:** *Ça vaut le coup, ça n'en vaut pas la peine.*

### 5.3 Shadowing and Imitation of Thought Patterns
French has characteristic ways to **organize thought**: concession (*certes... mais*), reformulation (*autrement dit*), enumeration (*d'une part... d'autre part*), nuance (*dans une certaine mesure*). Listening to French discussion and repeating these patterns trains your *reasoning style* in French, not just vocabulary.

### 5.4 Reduce L1 Subvocalization With Speed
Set a timer for **30 seconds** and speak non-stop about a prompt (*Qu'est-ce que j'ai fait ce week-end ?*). Speed leaves no time to translate. Do not correct yourself; keep going. Review afterward.

### 5.5 Anchors: Triggers for French Mode
Associate French with specific triggers: a cup of coffee, a morning walk, a playlist, a particular chair. Cues reduce the effort of switching.

### 5.6 Use French for Real Cognitive Work
Do something **mentally demanding** in French:
- plan a trip, a budget, or a project in French notes
- watch a lecture or documentary and explain it aloud in French
- do simple mental arithmetic in French (numbers, Lesson 20), including 70–99
- read and take notes in French

When French is the language of a *task* you care about, you stop treating it as a language exercise.

## 6. Handling Interference From Spanish and English

| Interference | Example | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| **Calques** | *Je suis 25 ans* (wrong) → *J'ai 25 ans*; *Je suis chaud* → *J'ai chaud* | notice the pattern and drill the whole chunk |
| **Word order** | placing adjectives as in Spanish/English | learn common adjective positions (Lesson 8) |
| **False friends** | *éventuellement* ≠ eventually; *actuellement* ≠ actually | log and review (Lesson 70 method) |
| **Fillers** | *pues, o sea, you know, like* | replace with *ben, du coup, enfin, genre, tu vois* (Lesson 68) |
| **Code-switching** | slipping into Spanish when stuck | rehearse circumlocutions in advance |
| **Prepositions** | *penser de/à*, *dépendre de* | learn prepositions with the verb as one chunk (Lesson 50) |

**Spanish advantage:** shared Latin vocabulary and similar grammar mean your Spanish can **accelerate** learning when you use it as a tool for noticing, not as a bridge for every sentence. English advantage: abundant French content and Latin-based vocabulary in academic English.

**Colombian speaker note:** you already operate fluently in two registers of Spanish (formal *usted* and familiar *tú / vos* in some contexts). Treat the French *vous / tu* switch as the same skill.

## 7. Mental Model: Trails in a Forest

Imagine your mind as a forest. For each idea, the **Spanish trail** is a wide, well-worn path; the **French trail** begins as an overgrown, narrow track. Every time you take the French trail directly, it widens. Translating is walking down the Spanish trail and then crossing through the bushes to the French one: slow and tiring. Direct use of French *clears the French trail itself*. Eight weeks of daily walking on the French trails create a visible path.

---

## 8. Signs of Progress (and How to Measure)

| Sign | What to notice |
|---|---|
| French words arrive before Spanish/English for common objects | You say *frigo* before "refrigerator" |
| French exclamations occur spontaneously | *Zut !* when you drop something |
| You think a full sentence in French, unprompted | during walks or showers |
| Dreams include French dialogue | late-stage sign |
| You find a Spanish/English word "slow" to arrive in a French context | mild interference the other way, a good sign |
| You enjoy humor and puns in French | Lesson 65 |
| Fatigue and stress do not collapse your French | resilience |

**Weekly check:** 5 minutes of a recorded spontaneous monologue. Count the **number of switches to Spanish/English** and the **pauses longer than 3 seconds**. They should decrease over weeks.

---

## 9. Common Mistakes

**Spanish speakers:** thinking you must "forget" Spanish; relying on Spanish word order in complex sentences; feeling guilty about silent translation instead of building an alternative; translating idioms literally (*estar en las nubes → être dans les nuages* works, but *echar de menos → jeter de moins* does not: *manquer*).

**English speakers:** avoiding speaking because inner translation is slow; relying on English subtitles and apps in the French environment.

**Both:** expecting immediate results; only studying and never using French for real tasks; avoiding errors so strictly that fluency never develops; practicing for hours one day and none the next (frequency beats intensity).

## 10. Progressively Harder Practice

1. **Daily 10 minutes:** narrate your morning routine in French aloud.
2. **Circumlocution game:** pick 10 objects in your room; describe each in French without naming it, to a partner or recording.
3. **30-second sprints:** answer random prompts (*Décris ta ville. Que ferais-tu avec un million ?*) without stopping.
4. **Decision journal:** write one small decision per day in French with reasons (*pour/contre*).
5. **Silent day:** one day per week, restrict yourself to French for all inner speech, labels, lists, and notes. Debrief in French.
6. **Immersion weekend:** 48 hours of French input and output: films, cooking with a French recipe, calls with a tutor.
