# Lesson 96 — Deep Dive 16: Living, Studying, and Working in a Francophone Country
### Phase 5: Mastery & Maintenance — Optional Deep Dive

**Competency axes trained:** 🎭 Pragmatics/register (primary) · 📚 Lexicon · 🗣️ Productive fluency · 💼 Professional communication · 🌍 Cultural competence
**Prerequisites:** Lesson 32 (business French), Lesson 66 (professional mastery), Lesson 75 (administrative French), Lesson 95 (everyday life)
**Time investment:** One read, then a four-week deep-dive cycle (Lesson 80, Section 7).

> **Language, not advice.** This lesson teaches the **language and conventions** of settling in, studying, and working in French-speaking places. Immigration, tax, health-insurance, and employment rules differ by country and **change often**. Always verify requirements with official sources (consulates, government portals, universities, employers) before acting. Nothing here is legal, immigration, or financial advice.

---

## 1. The Challenge: Language Under Administrative Pressure

Settling in a new place combines three difficulties: **specialized vocabulary** (forms, contracts), **formal register** (letters, appointments), and **time pressure** (deadlines, queues). Even advanced speakers find this tiring because the situations are unfamiliar and consequences matter.

**Mental model: expat French is a toolkit of five document types and five conversation types.**

| Documents | Conversations |
|---|---|
| forms (*formulaires*) | counter appointments (*rendez-vous au guichet*) |
| formal letters/emails | phone calls |
| contracts (*contrats*) | interviews (*entretiens*) |
| bills and statements | meetings with colleagues/professors |
| certificates (*attestations*) | casual small talk with neighbours and peers |

If you can read, fill, and write these five document types and handle those five conversations, you can navigate almost anything.

## 2. Arrival and Administrative Setup (France as the Main Model)

*The details below illustrate typical steps and vocabulary. Check current rules for your country and situation.*

### 2.1 Typical steps and vocabulary
| Step | Vocabulary |
|---|---|
| **Visa / residence permit** | *un visa long séjour, un titre de séjour, la préfecture, une demande, un récépissé, un justificatif* |
| **Validation of arrival** | forms and online portals managed by the immigration office (in France, the OFII and an online platform for foreigners); *valider son visa* |
| **Address proof** | *un justificatif de domicile* (utility bill, lease, *attestation d'hébergement*) |
| **Bank account** | *ouvrir un compte bancaire, un RIB (relevé d'identité bancaire), une carte bancaire, un virement, un prélèvement, un découvert* |
| **Insurance** | *une assurance habitation (mandatory for tenants in France), une assurance responsabilité civile, une mutuelle (complementary health insurance)* |
| **Health system** | *la Sécurité sociale, l'Assurance maladie, la carte Vitale, un médecin traitant, un remboursement, le tiers payant, une ordonnance* |
| **Housing aid** | *les aides au logement (APL), la CAF (family allowance fund)* |
| **Phone / internet** | *un forfait, une box, un abonnement, une résiliation* |
| **Taxes** | *la déclaration de revenus, l'impôt sur le revenu, l'avis d'imposition, le prélèvement à la source* |

### 2.2 Phrases at the counter (*au guichet*)
| Function | Phrases |
|---|---|
| **Starting** | *Bonjour, j'ai rendez-vous à 10 heures avec… / Je viens pour une demande de…* |
| **Stating your case** | *Je suis arrivé(e) il y a deux semaines et je dois…* |
| **Asking what's needed** | *Quels documents dois-je fournir ? / Quelles pièces justificatives faut-il ?* |
| **Clarifying** | *Pourriez-vous me l'expliquer plus lentement ? / Est-ce que je peux le faire en ligne ?* |
| **Checking deadlines** | *Dans quel délai recevrai-je une réponse ?* |
| **Confirming** | *Si je comprends bien, je dois… / Pouvez-vous me le confirmer par écrit ?* |
| **Problems** | *Il y a une erreur sur mon dossier. / Mon dossier est incomplet ? Que manque-t-il ?* |
| **Thanks and exit** | *Je vous remercie pour votre aide. Bonne journée.* |

### 2.3 Strategy: the "dossier"
Prepare a folder (paper + digital) with: passport, visa/permit, photos, proof of address, bank details, insurance certificates, birth certificate (with a sworn translation if required), school/work documents. Keep **copies**. Administrative French loves *pièces justificatives*.

**Tip:** write your personal "pitch" (one minute in French describing who you are, why you're there, and what you need). It saves you every time.

## 3. Studying: University and School Vocabulary

### 3.1 The system (France as model)
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **la licence (L1–L3), le master (M1–M2), le doctorat** | bachelor's (3 years), master's (2 years), doctorate |
| **l'université, la faculté, l'UFR** | university, faculty, teaching unit |
| **une grande école** | selective higher education institution (engineering, business, etc.) |
| **les classes préparatoires (prépa)** | intensive 2-year preparation for grandes écoles |
| **un cours magistral (CM), des travaux dirigés (TD), des travaux pratiques (TP)** | lecture, tutorial, lab/practical |
| **un amphi(théâtre)** | lecture hall |
| **une unité d'enseignement (UE)** | course module |
| **les crédits ECTS** | European credits |
| **le contrôle continu, un partiel, les examens, le rattrapage** | continuous assessment, mid/final exam, resit |
| **un mémoire, une thèse** | master's dissertation, doctoral thesis |
| **un stage, un stagiaire** | internship, intern |
| **l'inscription administrative / pédagogique** | administrative / course enrollment |
| **la bibliothèque universitaire (BU)** | university library |
| **une bourse** | scholarship/grant |
| **le CROUS** | student services (housing, restaurants, aid) in France |
| **un/une enseignant(e)-chercheur(-euse), un directeur / une directrice de thèse** | academic, doctoral supervisor |
| **l'emploi du temps** | timetable |

*In Québec:* *le cégep* (pre-university/technical college), *le baccalauréat* (a 3–4 year university bachelor's degree, not a school-leaving exam), *la maîtrise*, *le doctorat*, *un cours* with credits. In Belgium and Switzerland, vocabulary also differs (e.g., *candidature* and *licence* in Belgium; *haute école* in both), so check each institution.

### 3.2 Academic communication
| Situation | Phrases |
|---|---|
| **Emailing a professor** | *Madame la Professeure / Monsieur le Professeur, … Je me permets de vous écrire au sujet de… Je vous remercie par avance. Bien cordialement.* |
| **Asking for an appointment** | *Seriez-vous disponible pour un rendez-vous la semaine prochaine ?* |
| **Asking for clarification in class** | *Pardon, pourriez-vous répéter le dernier point ? / Est-ce que vous pouvez donner un exemple ?* |
| **Negotiating deadlines** | *Serait-il possible d'obtenir un délai supplémentaire pour… ?* |
| **Group work** | *Qui s'occupe de quelle partie ? / On se répartit le travail.* |
| **Presenting (exposé)** | *Je vais vous présenter… / Mon exposé se compose de trois parties.* (Lesson 89) |
| **Feedback** | *Quels sont les points à améliorer ?* |

**Cultural notes:** French academic culture values **structured argument** (Lesson 94), **written expression**, and **reasoned critique**. Professor-student relations may feel more formal than in some countries; *vous* is the norm with professors. Students use *tu* among themselves.

## 4. Working: Contracts, Culture, and Communication

### 4.1 Employment vocabulary
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **un CDI / un CDD** | permanent contract / fixed-term contract (France) |
| **un contrat de travail** | employment contract |
| **la période d'essai** | probationary period |
| **le salaire brut / net** | gross / net salary |
| **la fiche de paie / le bulletin de salaire** | payslip |
| **les cotisations sociales** | social contributions |
| **un cadre / non-cadre** | managerial status / non-managerial |
| **la convention collective** | sector-wide agreement |
| **les congés payés, les RTT** | paid leave, working-time-reduction days |
| **les tickets-restaurant, la mutuelle d'entreprise** | meal vouchers, employer health plan |
| **le préavis, la démission, le licenciement, la rupture conventionnelle** | notice period, resignation, dismissal, negotiated termination |
| **le télétravail** | remote work |
| **les ressources humaines (RH)** | HR |
| **le droit à la déconnexion** | right to disconnect (recognized in French law) |
| **un entretien annuel** | annual review |
| **un poste, une offre d'emploi, un recruteur** | position, job ad, recruiter |

Check the contract line by line: *clauses*, *durée*, *rémunération*, *lieu de travail*, *horaires*, *période d'essai*, *préavis*, *clause de non-concurrence*, *clause de mobilité*. Ask HR or a legal professional when unsure.

### 4.2 Job search
| Document / event | Notes |
|---|---|
| **CV** | one or two pages; chronological or skill-based; photo optional; avoid unnecessary personal data; check local norms |
| **Lettre de motivation** | cover letter, often expected; structure: why this company → why me → how I can contribute → request for an interview (Lesson 51) |
| **Entretien d'embauche** | job interview; see phrases below |
| **Réseautage (networking)** | LinkedIn, professional associations, alumni events, *afterwork* |

**Interview phrases**
| Function | Phrases |
|---|---|
| **Introduce yourself** | *Je me présente : …, j'ai un parcours en… et je travaille depuis cinq ans dans…* |
| **Strengths** | *Je suis rigoureux(se), autonome, et j'aime travailler en équipe.* |
| **Weakness (honest, constructive)** | *J'ai tendance à vouloir tout vérifier, mais j'apprends à déléguer.* |
| **Motivation** | *Ce poste m'intéresse parce que…* |
| **Experience** | *Dans mon poste précédent, j'étais chargé(e) de… J'ai géré… J'ai mis en place…* |
| **Salary** | *Quelles sont les perspectives de rémunération ?* / *Mes prétentions salariales se situent entre… et…* |
| **Questions** | *Comment est organisée l'équipe ? Quelles sont les perspectives d'évolution ?* |
| **Closing** | *Je vous remercie pour cet échange. Je reste à votre disposition.* |

**Note on your French:** at an interview, a few errors are less damaging than long hesitation. Prepare answers to the 10 standard questions aloud.

### 4.3 Workplace culture (tendencies, not rules)
| Aspect | Typical features |
|---|---|
| **Formality** | *vous* often used with superiors and new colleagues; *tu* spreads quickly in some sectors (start-ups, tech, creative), less in banking, law, public administration |
| **Greetings** | greet colleagues individually in the morning (*bonjour*, handshake or *bise* depending on the workplace) |
| **Lunch** | lunch is often a shared social time; skipping it can seem unusual |
| **Meetings** | structured, with debate; people may disagree openly on ideas (Lesson 89) |
| **Hierarchy** | decisions can be more centralized in some organizations; respect titles and procedures |
| **Emails** | begin with *Bonjour [Name],* end with *Cordialement*; subject lines clear (Lesson 51) |
| **Work-life boundary** | emails outside hours are discouraged in many places; *droit à la déconnexion* exists |
| **Humor** | present but contextual (Lesson 87) |
| **Small talk** | weekend, holidays, food, family (lightly), local news |

Work cultures differ widely by company, region, and sector; observe, ask colleagues, and adapt.

## 5. Social Life: Building Your Network

French-speaking environments can feel reserved at first. Friendships often form **slowly** through repeated contact in structured settings.

| Setting | Language and tips |
|---|---|
| **Associations (clubs, sports, choirs, volunteering)** | Search for *une association*; join a class; the same group meets regularly: ideal for friendships. |
| **Neighbours** | greet in stairwells; *Bonjour, je suis votre nouveau voisin / nouvelle voisine.* The annual neighbours' day (*Fête des voisins*, usually late spring) can help. |
| **Language exchanges and meetups** | *Je cherche un partenaire d'échange linguistique.* |
| **Colleagues** | accept *un pot* (drink at work), *un afterwork*, coffee breaks |
| **Flatmates** | *une colocation / un colocataire*: agree on house rules (*règles de vie*) |
| **Dating and relationships** | direct communication is valued; learn expressions of interest and boundaries (Lesson 54) |

**Phrases for making friends**
*Qu'est-ce que tu fais dans la vie ? / Tu viens d'où ? / Ça fait longtemps que tu habites ici ? / Tu connais un bon endroit pour… ? / On pourrait se voir un de ces jours ? / Je te donne mon numéro.*

**Distinguishing people:** *une connaissance* (acquaintance), *un(e) collègue*, *un(e) copain/copine* (friend/pal; also boyfriend/girlfriend depending on context), *un(e) ami(e)* (closer friend), *un(e) pote* (informal), *mon/ma meilleur(e) ami(e)*, *mon compagnon / ma compagne*.

*Note:* *copain/copine* can mean "boyfriend/girlfriend"; if you mean friend in general, *ami(e)* or *pote* may be clearer.

## 6. Health and Emergencies (Short Reference)

Review Lessons 37 and 82. Essentials:

| Situation | Phrases / numbers |
|---|---|
| **Emergency (EU)** | **112** works across the European Union; in France also **15** (medical emergency, SAMU), **17** (police), **18** (fire) — confirm the numbers for the country you live in |
| **Pharmacy** | *pharmacie* (green cross); pharmacists advise on minor ailments: *Qu'est-ce que vous me conseillez pour… ?* |
| **Doctor appointment** | *Je voudrais prendre rendez-vous avec le docteur… / C'est urgent ?* |
| **Describing symptoms** | *J'ai mal à…, J'ai de la fièvre depuis deux jours, Je suis allergique à…* |
| **Insurance** | *Je suis couvert(e) par… / Ma carte Vitale n'est pas encore arrivée.* |

## 7. Cultural Adjustment

Living abroad in a second language involves **language fatigue** and **culture shock**. Some phases are common: initial excitement, then frustration, then gradual adjustment.

**Strategies**
- Allow yourself **recovery time** in Spanish or English.
- Keep a **daily log** of small wins in French.
- Set **weekly goals** (one new contact, one new place).
- Learn the **unwritten rules** by asking: *Comment ça se passe d'habitude ?*
- Use **humor and self-deprecation** (Lesson 87).
- Ask for feedback: *Est-ce que c'est poli de dire ça comme ça ?*
- Maintain Colombian connections and traditions; sharing your culture (food, music) is a good social bridge.

## 8. Common Mistakes

**Spanish speakers:**
- Using *tu* with officials, professors, or superiors.
- Using *aplicar* → *appliquer* for job applications: say *postuler* / *poser sa candidature*.
- Translating *carrera* (university degree programme) as *carrière* (career path): use *études* or *cursus*.
- Confusing *passer un examen* (to take an exam) with *réussir un examen* (to pass it); Spanish *pasar* can mean either.
- Calling a professor *docteur* automatically; use *professeur* or *madame/monsieur*.
- Forgetting that dates are day/month/year.
- Handling contracts casually; read carefully.

**English speakers:**
- *Actually* → *actuellement*, *to graduate* → *obtenir son diplôme*, not *graduer*.
- Over-directness in emails without formulas.

**Both:**
- Not greeting at counters.
- Underestimating paperwork lead times.
- Assuming procedures are identical across countries and regions.
- Avoiding phone calls because of listening anxiety (prepare scripts; ask people to repeat).

| Week | Focus | Activities |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Paperwork language** | forms and counters | read three real forms; fill them in French (practice versions); write a one-minute self-introduction for the counter; role-play 3 counter scenarios |

**Resources:** official government portals (e.g., service-public sites), university international offices, employer onboarding guides, newspapers' career pages, forums for expatriates (verify claims against official sources).

## 10. Progressively Harder Practice

1. **Counter script (10 min):** write and say a one-minute introduction for a prefecture or municipal office.
2. **Form vocabulary:** list 20 words you meet on a French administrative form (*nom de naissance, nom d'usage, date et lieu de naissance, situation familiale, justificatif*, etc.) and learn them.
3. **Email to a professor:** ask for an appointment about your thesis topic (100 words).
4. **Interview rehearsal:** answer in French: *Parlez-moi de votre parcours. Pourquoi voulez-vous travailler chez nous ? Quelles sont vos qualités et vos défauts ?*
5. **Contract reading:** read a sample employment contract and summarize its five most important clauses in simple French.
6. **Phone call:** role-play a call to a landlord or a bank (with a partner or recorded script).
7. **Reflection:** write 200 words describing what would be hardest for you in settling in a French-speaking place and your plan to manage it.
