# Lesson 6 — The Present Tense System: Regular Verbs and the Logic Behind French Conjugation
### Phase 1: Core System (A1 → A2)

**Competency axes trained:** 🧩 Morphosyntax (primary) · 📚 Lexicon (secondary)
**Prerequisite:** Phase 0 complete (sound system, sentence structure)
**Time investment:** One thorough read + daily conjugation drills this week

---

## 1. Why We Teach Conjugation as a System, Not a List

Most courses hand you an "-ER verb" ending table and say "memorize it." Instead, we're going to show you **why** those endings exist and how they connect to what you already know from Spanish, so you're recognizing a pattern instead of memorizing arbitrary strings.

French verbs fall into three "families" by infinitive ending: **-ER** (largest group, ~90% of verbs, all new verbs entering French use this pattern), **-IR**, and **-RE**. This three-way split is a direct descendant of Latin's four conjugation classes — Spanish kept a very similar three-way split (-AR, -ER, -IR), so your instinct that "the ending predicts the pattern" transfers directly.

---

## 2. The -ER Family (regular, largest group)

Take **parler** ("to speak"), remove **-er**, add:

| Person | Ending | Form | IPA |
|---|---|---|---|
| je | -e | parle | [paʁl] |
| tu | -es | parles | [paʁl] |
| il/elle/on | -e | parle | [paʁl] |
| nous | -ons | parlons | [paʁ.lɔ̃] |
| vous | -ez | parlez | [paʁ.le] |
| ils/elles | -ent | parlent | [paʁl] |

**Critical insight for you:** notice that **je, tu, il/elle/on, ils/elles all sound identical** — [paʁl]. This is completely different from Spanish, where every person has a distinct sound (hablo/hablas/habla/hablamos/habláis/hablan). In French, **the subject pronoun carries most of the grammatical information; the verb ending is often silent.** This is a huge conceptual shift: French relies on pronouns far more than Spanish does, because Spanish's rich verb endings let you drop the pronoun (*hablo* = "I speak," no "yo" needed) while French almost never drops it (*je parle*, never just *parle*).

---

## 3. The -IR Family (two sub-patterns — this is where Spanish intuition needs adjusting)

**Pattern A (like finir — "to finish"):**

| Person | Form |
|---|---|
| je | finis |
| tu | finis |
| il/elle/on | finit |
| nous | finissons |
| vous | finissez |
| ils/elles | finissent |

Notice the **-iss-** insertion in the plural forms — this has no direct Spanish parallel and simply needs to become automatic through repetition.

**Pattern B (like partir — "to leave," dormir, sortir):**

| Person | Form |
|---|---|
| je | pars |
| tu | pars |
| il/elle/on | part |
| nous | partons |
| vous | partez |
| ils/elles | partent |

The consonant before the ending (**t** in partir) drops in the singular — pattern recognition, not memorization: singular forms are "shorter/stripped," plural forms restore the full stem.

| nous | vendons |
| vous | vendez |
| ils/elles | vendent |

## 5. Mental Model: Two Layers — the Stem (Meaning) and the Ending (Grammar)

Every regular French verb splits cleanly into a **stem** (carries meaning, usually stable) and an **ending** (carries grammar: who's doing it). This is identical in principle to Spanish (habl-o, habl-as) — what's different is that French endings are **mostly silent in speech**, so the *spelling* carries more grammatical information than the *sound* does. This is why French relies so heavily on the subject pronoun as the audible carrier of "who."

---

## 6. Common Mistakes

**Spanish speakers:** dropping subject pronouns out of habit (*"Parle français"* instead of *"Je parle français"*) — grammatically wrong in French, unlike Spanish where it's normal and even preferred.

**English speakers:** trying to add "do/does" for questions/negation with these verbs (no equivalent in French — covered in Lesson 3).

## 7. Progressively Harder Examples

1. Je parle. — "I speak."
2. Tu finis tes devoirs. — "You finish your homework."
3. Nous vendons la maison. — "We're selling the house."
4. Ils ne parlent pas français. — "They don't speak French."
5. Est-ce que vous finissez bientôt? — "Are you finishing soon?"
