# Lesson 52 — Phase 4 Interleaved Review #1: Register, Idioms, Collocations, Prepositions, Writing
### Phase 4: Native Naturalness (B2 → C1) — Review Checkpoint

**Competency axes trained:** All Phase 4 axes so far, interleaved
**Purpose:** Retrieval practice on Lessons 47–51, mixed out of order, plus spaced review of earlier-phase material that these lessons depend on.

---

## 1. Why This Checkpoint

Lessons 47–51 are about **naturalness**: matching register, using idioms and collocations as chunks, choosing prepositions by contrast, and writing with the right conventions. These are "feel" skills, so retrieval practice matters even more than usual: you want the right choice to come out automatically, without consulting a rule.

**How to use this lesson:** attempt every item **before** reading the answer key. Say answers aloud, or write them. Then score yourself honestly and note which numbered items you missed.

### E. Writing (Lesson 51)

## 3. Answer Key

1. Very formal: *Je ne sais pas s'il viendra.* / Neutral: *Je ne sais pas s'il va venir.* / Casual: *Je sais pas s'il va venir.* / Very casual: *Chais pas s'y va venir.*
2. A change in **word choice** by register: *voiture* (neutral) vs. *bagnole* (casual); *se restaurer* (formal) vs. *manger* (neutral) vs. *bouffer* (casual).
3. To stand someone up (Spanish: *dejar plantado*).
4. *Mettre la main à la pâte.*
5. It is an English idiom translated word for word. The French image is *il pleut des cordes*.
6. To feel down or gloomy (literally "to have the cockroach").
7. *Prendre une décision*, *poser une question*, *respecter un délai*, *mettre en œuvre* (or *mettre en place*).
8. A verb with little meaning of its own that combines with a noun to form the predicate: *faire*, *prendre*, *avoir*, *mettre*, *donner* (any three).
9. *s'habituer **à***, *penser **à***, *rêver **de***.
10. *J'habite ici **depuis** trois ans.* / *J'ai habité là **pendant** trois ans.*
11. *Dans deux heures* = two hours from now (a point in the future); *en deux heures* = it takes two hours to complete.
12. *À vélo* (you ride on it), *en train* (you sit inside it).
13. *Un cadeau **pour** toi* / *écrit **par** Marie.*
14. A space **before** the double marks ? ! : ; (and spaces inside « guillemets »).
15. Formal: *Je vous prie d'agréer, Madame, Monsieur, l'expression de mes salutations distinguées.* Neutral: *Cordialement,* (or *Bien cordialement,*).
16. No. Days, months, and languages are lowercase (*lundi*, *mars*, *français*).
17. The subject line of an email, placed at the top (*Objet : ...*).
18. *que j'aie*, *que nous ayons*.
19. *voyagerais*.
20. *J'en parle.* / *J'y vais.*
21. *On parle français ici.*
22. *Chais pas* (the *ne* drops, then *je sais* contracts).

## 4. Integrated Production Task

Write a **formal email** (8–10 lines), then **rewrite the same message** as a short text to a friend. Requirements:

1. Formal version: *objet*, opening, purpose in the first sentence, one polite conditional request (Lesson 29/32), correct closing formula, French punctuation spacing.
2. Include **at least three collocations** from Lesson 49 (for example *prendre une décision*, *respecter un délai*, *poser une question*).
3. Include **at least two time prepositions** from Lesson 50 (*depuis*, *pendant*, *dans*, *il y a*, *en*).
4. Casual version: use one Tier 2 idiom (Lesson 48), dropped *ne*, and one filler or discourse marker (Lesson 41).
5. Read both aloud and notice how register changes **grammar, vocabulary, and rhythm** together, not only the greeting.
