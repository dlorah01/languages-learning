# Lesson 100 — Centennial Lesson: Your Living Curriculum
### Phase 5: Mastery & Maintenance — Milestone

**Competency axes trained:** All eight axes, synthesized · 🧠 Metacognition
**Prerequisites:** the whole program, especially Lessons 62, 79, 80, 92, and 99
**Time investment:** One reflective read (about 45 minutes), then permanent use as a reference.

---

## 1. One Hundred Lessons: What You Have Built

You started with the sounds of French and an IPA chart. You now have a **map of the language** that connects pronunciation, grammar, vocabulary, register, discourse, listening, speaking, culture, and style, and a **set of habits** to keep growing. This lesson does not teach a new rule. It gives you the whole picture, a toolkit, and a way to keep generating your own curriculum.

**Mental model: from course to operating system.** A course ends. A **living curriculum** is a system you run: it takes your goals and your data (errors, recordings, interests) and produces the next piece of learning.

## 3. What "Native-Like Intuition" Looks Like (Honest Indicators)

You set the goal of native-like intuition. It is useful to define it by observable signs rather than a mystical state. Signs that your French is becoming intuitive:

| Sign | What it feels like |
|---|---|
| **Gender and agreement feel automatic** | "Wrong" forms sound wrong before you can explain why |
| **Collocations arrive ready-made** | you reach for *prendre une décision*, *poser une question* without thinking |
| **Register shifts happen without effort** | you switch between standard, informal, and formal almost unconsciously |
| **You catch irony and understatement** | jokes land in real time (Lesson 87) |
| **You think in French in short bursts** | inner commentary appears in French (Lesson 71) |
| **You predict while listening** | you finish sentences in your head |
| **You recognize regional accents** | you place speakers roughly (Lesson 63) |
| **Writing has a voice** | your texts sound like you (Lesson 90) |
| **You repair on the fly** | you rephrase when stuck without panic (Lesson 89) |
| **You get cultural allusions** | references from history, literature, and media connect (Lessons 69, 93) |

Be realistic: even highly proficient adults retain a recognizable accent or occasional errors, and native speakers themselves differ widely. The aim is **functional and expressive ease**, not erasing your origins. Your Colombian identity and Spanish are part of what makes your French yours.

## 4. The Reusable Toolkit

### 4.1 The lesson template (to design your own deep dives)
Every lesson in this program followed one structure. You can reuse it for any topic:

1. **Purpose and mental model:** why this matters; one image that organizes it.
2. **Core content in tables:** vocabulary, rules, patterns.
3. **Spanish ↔ English ↔ French comparison:** transfer and traps.
4. **Common mistakes** for Spanish and English speakers.
5. **Four-week deep-dive plan:** weekly foci and activities.
6. **Progressively harder practice:** 7 tasks from easy to demanding.
7. **Mini quiz with hidden answers** (active recall).
8. **Spaced-repetition flags:** days 2, 7, and 21.
9. **Next step.**

### 4.2 The prompt to generate a new deep dive
You can ask a tutor, a teacher, or an AI assistant with a request like this (adapt as needed):

> *Je voudrais un cours approfondi sur [thème], niveau C1–C2, pour un hispanophone qui parle aussi anglais. Structure : modèle mental, vocabulaire et structures en tableaux, comparaison avec l'espagnol et l'anglais, erreurs fréquentes, plan de quatre semaines, exercices progressifs, mini-quiz avec réponses, rappel espacé. Ne reproduis pas de textes protégés ; donne des exemples originaux. Signale les points à vérifier auprès de sources officielles.*

**Always verify** factual claims (legal, medical, administrative, cultural) with primary sources, and use native speakers or teachers to check naturalness.

### 4.3 The error log (the most valuable document you own)
| Date | Where | What I said/wrote | Correct form | Rule or reason | Type | Retested? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
Review it weekly; turn recurring items into drills (Lessons 70, 85, 90).

### 4.4 The spaced-repetition schedule
New item → review at **day 2**, **day 7**, **day 21**, **day 60**, then on demand. Use cards that force **recall and production** (prompt in Spanish/English or a cloze in French), not mere recognition. Suspend cards you answer instantly.

### 4.5 The weekly rhythm (adaptable)
| Day | Focus |
|---|---|
| Mon | intensive listening (Lesson 88) |
| Tue | reading + journal (Lesson 93) |
| Wed | speaking practice or conversation (Lesson 89) |
| Thu | writing/revision (Lesson 90) |
| Fri | culture/media: series, radio, music (Lessons 56, 97) |
| Sat | project work (Lesson 92) |
| Sun | 20-minute review + planning (Lesson 99) |

### 4.6 The reference shelf
- **Dictionaries:** Larousse, Robert, CNRTL/TLFi, Wiktionnaire, a bilingual French–Spanish dictionary for contrast.
- **Grammar:** Bescherelle, Grevisse's *Le Bon Usage*, Académie française's language pages, the OQLF's *Banque de dépannage linguistique*.
- **Terminology:** FranceTerme, the OQLF's *Grand dictionnaire terminologique*.
- **Corpora and concordancers:** spoken and written corpora (Lessons 49, 88).
- **Libraries:** Gallica, Wikisource, public libraries, Alliance française mediatheques.
- **Media:** public radio, TV, podcasts, newspapers (Lesson 74).

## 7. A Closing Reflection Exercise

Write in French (300–500 words), by hand or on screen, a text titled **« Mon parcours en français »** answering:

1. Qu'est-ce qui m'a le plus surpris(e) dans la langue française ?
2. Quelles ressemblances et différences avec l'espagnol m'ont aidé(e) ou gêné(e) ?
3. Quelle méthode a le mieux fonctionné pour moi ?
4. Quelle est ma plus grande fierté dans mon apprentissage ?
5. Quel est mon plus grand défi pour les douze prochains mois ?
6. Comment est-ce que je veux utiliser le français dans ma vie ?

Record yourself reading it aloud. Keep the text and recording in your portfolio; read and listen to them again a year from now (Lesson 99).
