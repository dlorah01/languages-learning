# Lesson 43 — Phase 3 Interleaved Review: Nuance, Fillers, Sentence Architecture
### Phase 3: Fluency Build (B1 → B2) — Review Checkpoint

**Competency axes trained:** All Phase 3 axes so far, interleaved
**Purpose:** Consolidate Lessons 39–42 before continuing deeper into Phase 3's remaining lessons on humor, literature entry points, and further nuance work.

---

## 1. Why This Checkpoint

Lessons 39–42 shifted from pure grammar toward fluency multipliers: prefixed verb families, near-synonym nuance, natural discourse markers, and compressed sentence architecture. These are exactly the skills that make speech sound native rather than merely correct — worth consolidating before adding more.

---

## 2. Mixed Retrieval Practice

1. What does "supporter" mean in French, and why is it a trap?
2. Distinguish apporter and emporter.
3. What's the difference between "petit" and "faible" as translations of "small"?
4. When would you choose "mignon" over "beau"?
5. Why can filler-free French sound *more* foreign, not less?
6. Give one casual filler and one formal academic connector for contrast.
7. How is the French gerund formed?
8. Why can't the French gerund form a progressive tense like English "-ing"?

---

## 3. Answer Key

1. "To tolerate/put up with" — NOT "to support" (emotionally/financially), a common false-friend trap especially for English speakers.
2. Apporter = to bring toward (the speaker); emporter = to take away (à emporter = "to go").
3. Petit = small in physical size; faible = weak/low in intensity (une faible chance = a slim chance), not physical size.
4. For small/cute things (babies, animals, small objects) — not for grand or majestic beauty.
5. Because native speech naturally includes fillers/hedges; total absence sounds overly clipped and unnaturally formal.
6. Casual: "euh," "ben," "voilà." Formal: "cependant," "par conséquent," "en conclusion."
7. Nous-form of present tense, minus -ons, plus -ant (parlons → parlant).
8. Because French has no gerund-based progressive tense; ongoing action uses simple present or "être en train de + infinitive."

---

## 4. Combined Production Task

Narrate a short story (6–8 sentences) about something that happened to you recently, deliberately including:
- At least one gerund ("en + present participle")
- At least one carefully chosen near-synonym (justify your choice mentally — why this word over its close alternative)
- At least 2 natural fillers/discourse markers appropriate to casual spoken register
- At least one participial clause (bare past participle opening a clause)
