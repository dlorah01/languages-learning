# Lesson 46 — Phase 3 Capstone Review: Full Assessment Before Phase 4
### Phase 3: Fluency Build (B1 → B2) — Final Checkpoint

**Competency axes trained:** All Phase 3 axes, fully interleaved
**Purpose:** Close out Phase 3 (Lessons 39–45) with comprehensive review before Phase 4 (Native Naturalness, B2→C1) begins.

---

## 1. Why This Capstone Matters

Phase 3 shifted decisively from "correct French" toward "natural, fluent French": prefixed verb families, nuanced word choice, natural discourse markers, compressed sentence architecture, cultural humor literacy, and native-speed listening decoding. Phase 4 builds directly on all of this to work on register mastery, idiom density, and literary entry points.

---

## 2. Mixed Retrieval Test

1. Name two members of the "tenir" prefix family and their meanings.
2. What's the false-friend trap with "supporter"?
3. Distinguish "petit" and "faible."
4. Give one casual filler and explain why its absence can sound unnatural.
5. How is the French gerund formed, and what can't it be used for?
6. Name one of the four humor categories from Lesson 44.
7. What happens to "ne" in casual negation, and give an example.
8. What's the difference between top-down and bottom-up listening processing?

---

## 3. Answer Key

1. Obtenir (to obtain), retenir (to retain/remember), maintenir (to maintain) — any two.
2. It means "to tolerate," not "to support" (emotionally/financially).
3. Petit = small in physical size; faible = weak/low in intensity.
4. E.g., "ben," "voilà," "euh" — absence sounds overly clipped/formal, unlike natural spontaneous speech.
5. Nous-form minus -ons, plus -ant; cannot form a progressive tense (no French equivalent of "-ing" as a tense marker).
6. Wordplay/jeux de mots, irony/understatement, self-deprecation/social commentary, or l'humour noir — any one.
7. It's frequently dropped entirely: "je ne sais pas" → "je sais pas" (or further reduced "chais pas").
8. Bottom-up = parsing every sound sequentially (breaks down at native speed); top-down = using context/expectation to predict and fill gaps (how fluent listeners actually process speech).

## 4. Integrated Production Task

Record yourself (or write, reading it aloud) narrating a short anecdote (8–10 sentences) about a recent event, deliberately combining:

- At least one prefixed verb from the tenir/porter families (Lesson 39)
- At least one deliberately chosen near-synonym, justified mentally (Lesson 40)
- At least 2 natural spoken fillers/discourse markers (Lesson 41)
- At least one gerund or participial clause (Lesson 42)
- Casual-register negation (dropped "ne") if the register fits (Lesson 45)

Then, separately, listen to 2 minutes of real native French audio (podcast, news, or video) and try to catch at least 3 of the reduction patterns from Lesson 45 in the wild.
