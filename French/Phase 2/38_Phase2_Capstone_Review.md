# Lesson 38 — Phase 2 Capstone Review: Full Assessment Before Phase 3
### Phase 2: Expansion (A2 → B1) — Final Checkpoint

**Competency axes trained:** All Phase 2 axes, fully interleaved
**Purpose:** Close out Phase 2 (Lessons 26–37) with comprehensive mixed retrieval plus an integrated narrative/argumentative production task before Phase 3 (Fluency Build) begins.

---

## 1. Why This Capstone Matters

Phase 2 added enormous range on top of Phase 1's core system: the subjunctive, relative clauses, reported speech, the conditional, the passive voice (and its natural alternatives), professional and academic register, idioms, four more deep-dive verbs, and health vocabulary. Phase 3 will assume fluent, unconscious command of all of it.

---

## 2. Mixed Retrieval Test

1. Give the subjunctive of "avoir" for "je" and "nous."
2. Choose qui/que: "La femme ___ je connais habite ici." / "Le chien ___ aboie est à mon voisin."
3. Convert to past reported speech: Elle a dit: « Je viendrai demain. »
4. Complete: "Si tu ___ (avoir) le temps, tu ___ (pouvoir) m'aider."
5. Rewrite naturally (avoid the passive): "Le français est parlé dans plusieurs pays."
6. Give one formal professional email closing.
7. What's the difference between "cependant" and "pourtant"?
8. What does "avoir la flemme" mean?
9. Distinguish partir/quitter/laisser with an example each.
10. What's the formula for "avoir mal à," with one contraction example?

---

## 3. Answer Key

1. que j'aie; que nous ayons
2. La femme que je connais... / Le chien qui aboie...
3. Elle a dit qu'elle viendrait demain. (futur → conditionnel)
4. avais; pourrais
5. On parle français dans plusieurs pays.
6. Cordialement, / Je vous prie d'agréer, Madame, Monsieur, l'expression de mes salutations distinguées.
7. Cependant = formal/written; pourtant = spoken/informal-leaning.
8. "To not feel like doing something."
9. Partir (to depart, intransitive: je pars demain), quitter (to leave a place/person, transitive: je quitte la ville), laisser (to leave an object: j'ai laissé mon sac ici).
10. avoir mal à + le/la/les + body part; à + le → au (j'ai mal au dos).

---

## 4. Integrated Production Task: A Short Argumentative Narrative

Write or say aloud a short passage (10–12 sentences) titled **"Pourquoi j'apprends le français"** ("Why I'm learning French"), deliberately including:

- At least one subjunctive trigger (Lesson 26)
- At least one relative clause with qui, que, or dont (Lesson 27)
- At least one reported statement — something someone told you about learning languages (Lesson 28)
- At least one conditional/hypothetical si-clause (Lesson 29)
- At least 2 academic/argumentative connectors (tout d'abord, cependant, par conséquent, en conclusion — Lesson 33)
- At least one Tier 1 idiom (Lesson 34)
- Correct tu/vous register awareness if addressing anyone directly (Lesson 16)

This task forces every Phase 2 system to combine — the true test of whether these are now productive tools rather than recognized rules.

---

## 5. Self-Assessment & Phase 2 Wrap-Up

Score the retrieval test out of 10, and evaluate the narrative task against its 7 checklist items. Any consistently weak area is worth a full re-read of its source lesson before Phase 3 begins — Phase 3 (Fluency Build, B1→B2) will use these systems constantly and will not re-teach them.

**You've now completed Phase 2.** Combined with Phase 1, you have functional command of essentially the entire grammatical architecture of French — moods (indicative, subjunctive, conditional, imperative), all core tenses, the full pronoun system, relative and reported clauses, and a solid, register-aware vocabulary spanning daily life, work, academia, health, and idiomatic speech. Phase 3 shifts emphasis toward fluency, nuance, and authentic native material.
