# Lesson 39 — The Remaining High-Frequency Verbs: Tenir, Porter, and Their Prefixed Families
### Phase 3: Fluency Build (B1 → B2)

**Competency axes trained:** 📚 Lexicon (primary) · 🧩 Morphosyntax (secondary)
**Prerequisite:** Lessons 17, 36 (verb deep-dive method), Lesson 12 (passé composé)
**Time investment:** One thorough read + this lesson introduces a genuinely powerful vocabulary-expansion technique

---

## 1. Why This Opens Phase 3: A New Kind of Leverage (Intuitive Explanation)

Phase 1 and 2 built your grammatical engine and core vocabulary. Phase 3 shifts toward **fluency multipliers** — techniques that generate large vocabulary gains from small effort. This lesson introduces one of the most powerful: French (like Spanish and English, but even more systematically) builds huge verb families by attaching prefixes to a small set of base verbs. Master "tenir" and "porter" deeply, and you unlock a dozen more verbs almost for free.

---

## 2. Tenir — "To Hold" (and its prefixed family)

**Conjugation (irregular, same pattern as "venir"):** je tiens, tu tiens, il tient, nous tenons, vous tenez, ils tiennent. Past participle: **tenu** (avoir).

**Core meaning:** to hold, to keep.

**The prefixed family — each adds a prefix that shifts the meaning predictably:**

| Verb | Prefix meaning | Resulting meaning |
|---|---|---|
| obtenir | ob- (toward) | to obtain |
| retenir | re- (back) | to retain / hold back / remember |
| maintenir | main- (hand, historically) | to maintain |
| contenir | con- (with/together) | to contain |
| appartenir | à + | to belong (à) |
| soutenir | sous- (under) | to support/sustain |

**Conjugation note:** all of these follow "tenir's" exact irregular pattern (je retiens, je maintiens, etc.) — you're not learning six new conjugation patterns, just six new prefixed meanings on one pattern you already have.

---

## 3. Porter — "To Carry / To Wear" (regular -ER, and its prefixed family)

**Conjugation:** regular -ER verb (Lesson 6). Past participle: **porté** (avoir).

**Core meaning:** to carry, to wear (clothing).

**The prefixed family:**

| Verb | Prefix meaning | Resulting meaning |
|---|---|---|
| apporter | ap- (toward) | to bring (an object toward someone) |
| emporter | em-/en- (away) | to take away / carry off |
| rapporter | re- + ap- (back + toward) | to bring back / report |
| supporter | sup- (under/bear) | to bear/tolerate (NOT "to support" — false friend!) |
| comporter | com- (with) | to comprise/include; se comporter = to behave |

**Critical false-friend flag:** **"supporter"** in French means "to tolerate/put up with," NOT "to support" (emotionally/financially) — a genuinely dangerous false friend for both English and Spanish speakers ("soportar" in Spanish also means "to tolerate," so this one transfers correctly from Spanish, but is a trap coming from English "to support").

---

## 4. Apporter vs. Emporter — A High-Value Everyday Distinction

> **Apporter** = to bring **toward** the speaker/a fixed point: **Apporte-moi le livre.** — "Bring me the book."
> **Emporter** = to take **away**: **à emporter** — "to take away/to go" (the phrase you'll see on every French café menu for takeout orders!)

This "toward vs. away" distinction, encoded directly in the prefix (ap- vs. em-), is a genuinely elegant piece of French morphology worth internalizing as a pattern, not just two separate vocabulary items.

---

## 5. Mental Model: Prefixes as Modular Attachments on a Base Verb

Picture "tenir" and "porter" as base units, with prefixes functioning like modular attachments that shift meaning in predictable directions: re- (back/again), ob-/ap- (toward), em-/ex- (away/out), sou-/sup- (under), con-/com- (with/together). Once you internalize what a handful of these prefixes mean, encountering a new prefixed verb becomes a puzzle you can often solve rather than a new item to memorize cold — a genuine fluency multiplier, and a technique that will keep paying off as your vocabulary grows into the thousands.

---

## 6. Common Mistakes

**Spanish speakers:** genuinely strong position here, since Spanish has a very similar Latin-derived prefix system (obtener, retener, mantener, contener, sostener — nearly identical to the tenir family!) — lean into this parallel heavily. Watch specifically for "supporter" ≠ "soportar's" full range (French "supporter" is narrower, purely "to tolerate," while Spanish "soportar" can occasionally stretch toward bearing weight/structure too).

**English speakers:** the "supporter" false friend is a serious, high-frequency trap — English speakers very commonly misuse "supporter" to mean emotional/financial support, producing an incorrect or confusing sentence in French.

## 7. Progressively Harder Examples

1. Ce livre m'appartient. — "This book belongs to me."
2. Je ne peux plus supporter ce bruit. — "I can't stand this noise anymore." (NOT "support")
3. C'est pour ici ou à emporter? — "Is that for here or to go?" (classic café phrase)
4. Elle m'a apporté des fleurs pour mon anniversaire. — "She brought me flowers for my birthday."
5. Ce projet contient plusieurs erreurs, mais je vais essayer de le soutenir devant le comité. — "This project contains several errors, but I'm going to try to support it in front of the committee." (soutenir, correctly used for genuine support/backing, contrasted with supporter's narrower "tolerate" meaning)
