## 1. Continuing the Verb-Hub Method (Intuitive Explanation)

Following Lesson 17's method, this lesson deep-dives four more high-frequency verbs from your original curriculum verb list, each with surprising range beyond their core dictionary meaning.

---

## 2. Voir — "To See"

**Conjugation (irregular):** je vois, tu vois, il voit, nous voyons, vous voyez, ils voient. Past participle: **vu** (avoir).

**Beyond literal sight:**
- **Understanding:** je vois ce que tu veux dire — "I see what you mean"
- **Visiting/meeting:** on se voit demain? — "shall we see each other tomorrow?" (reflexive, Lesson 23 callback)
- **Checking:** voyons voir — "let's see" (a doubled, idiomatic filler phrase)

---

## 3. Croire — "To Believe"

**Conjugation (irregular):** je crois, tu crois, il croit, nous croyons, vous croyez, ils croient. Past participle: **cru** (avoir).

**Key structures:**
- **croire que** + indicative (affirmative): je crois qu'il vient — "I believe he's coming"
- **ne pas croire que** + subjunctive (Lesson 26 callback, same pattern as "penser"): je ne crois pas qu'il vienne — "I don't believe he's coming"
- **croire à/en** — "to believe in": croire en Dieu (in God), croire à un projet (in a project's viability) — note the preposition split, a genuine nuance worth learning as fixed collocations rather than a single rule.

---

## 4. Laisser — "To Let / To Leave (something)"

**Conjugation:** regular -ER verb (Lesson 6). Past participle: **laissé** (avoir).

**Core meanings:**
- **To let/allow + infinitive:** laisse-moi t'expliquer — "let me explain to you" (imperative, Lesson 22 callback, with pronoun placement)
- **To leave (an object) behind:** j'ai laissé mon sac au bureau — "I left my bag at the office" (contrast with "partir/quitter" for leaving a place/person — a common confusion point)
- **Laisser tomber** — "to drop / give up" (Lesson 34 idiom callback)

**Critical distinction (three French verbs for English "to leave"):** partir (to leave/depart, intransitive), quitter (to leave a place/person, transitive), laisser (to leave an object behind, transitive) — English collapses all three into "leave," but French requires choosing the right one based on what's being left.

---

## 5. Rendre — "To Give Back / To Render"

**Conjugation:** regular -RE verb (Lesson 6). Past participle: **rendu** (avoir).

**Core meanings:**
- **To return/give back:** je te rends ton livre — "I'm giving you back your book"
- **To make (+ adjective) — a genuinely useful causative structure:** ça me rend heureux — "that makes me happy" (rendre + adjective, an alternative to simpler "faire" constructions, often preferred for emotional states)
- **Se rendre (reflexive, Lesson 23 callback):** se rendre à + place — "to go to [a place]" (a more formal alternative to aller); se rendre compte de/que — "to realize" (extremely high-frequency idiomatic reflexive)

---

## 6. Mental Model: Four More Hubs, Same Method as Lesson 17

Apply Lesson 17's "hub with spokes" model here too: voir's spokes range from literal sight to understanding to social meeting; croire's spokes split by preposition (à/en) and by mood (indicative/subjunctive depending on polarity); laisser sits in a three-way contrast with partir/quitter; rendre extends from simple "giving back" to a full causative construction. None of these are separate verbs to memorize in isolation — they're each one hub, multiple contextual spokes.

---

## 7. Common Mistakes

**Spanish speakers:** confusing "dejar" (which covers much of laisser's range) with the French three-way partir/quitter/laisser split, since Spanish "dejar" alone covers more ground than any single French equivalent; occasionally using "faire" where "rendre + adjective" would sound more natural for emotional causation.

**English speakers:** using a single verb "to leave" and needing to consciously choose among partir/quitter/laisser each time, since English provides no structural cue for which is needed.

---

## 8. Progressively Harder Examples

1. Je vois ce que tu veux dire. — "I see what you mean."
2. Je ne crois pas qu'il vienne ce soir. — "I don't think he's coming tonight." (subjunctive after negative croire)
3. J'ai laissé mon parapluie chez toi. — "I left my umbrella at your place."
4. Ça me rend triste de penser à ça. — "That makes me sad to think about that."
5. Je me suis rendu compte que j'avais laissé mon sac au bureau avant de partir. — "I realized I had left my bag at the office before leaving." (rendre compte + laisser + partir, three verbs from this lesson's contrast set in one sentence)
