# Lesson 7 — Être, Avoir, Faire, Aller: The Four Pillar Verbs
### Phase 1: Core System (A1 → A2)

**Competency axes trained:** 🧩 Morphosyntax (primary) · 📚 Lexicon (secondary) · 🗣️ Productive fluency
**Prerequisite:** Lesson 6 (regular verb system)
**Time investment:** One thorough read + daily drills — these four verbs deserve more repetition than any other vocabulary in this program

---

## 1. Why These Four Verbs First (Intuitive Explanation)

**Être** ("to be"), **avoir** ("to have"), **faire** ("to do/make"), and **aller** ("to go") are irregular — they don't follow Lesson 6's patterns — but they are so extraordinarily high-frequency that they function as **grammatical infrastructure**, not ordinary vocabulary. Être and avoir alone build every compound tense in French (you'll see this soon with passé composé). Aller builds the entire near-future tense. Faire appears in hundreds of fixed expressions where English/Spanish would use a completely different verb. Master these four irregularly, deliberately, now — everything downstream depends on them.

---

## 2. Être — "To Be"

| Person | Form | IPA |
|---|---|---|
| je | suis | [sɥi] |
| tu | es | [ɛ] |
| il/elle/on | est | [ɛ] |
| nous | sommes | [sɔm] |
| vous | êtes | [ɛt] |
| ils/elles | sont | [sɔ̃] |

**Spanish false-friend warning:** French has only **one** verb for "to be" where Spanish has two (*ser/estar*). French être covers both permanent characteristics (*je suis grand* — "I am tall") and temporary states (*je suis fatigué* — "I am tired"). Don't hunt for a French "estar" — it doesn't exist as a separate verb; context does the work Spanish splits across two verbs.

**Key uses:** identity (*je suis Colombien*), location in some fixed expressions, all adjective-linking, and as the auxiliary for certain compound tenses (more in the passé composé lesson).

---

## 3. Avoir — "To Have"

| Person | Form | IPA |
|---|---|---|
| j'ai | ai | [e] |
| tu as | as | [a] |
| il/elle/on a | a | [a] |
| nous avons | avons | [a.vɔ̃] |
| vous avez | avez | [a.ve] |
| ils/elles ont | ont | [ɔ̃] |

**Critical idiom cluster — avoir for states English/Spanish express with "to be":**

| French (avoir) | Literal | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| avoir faim | to have hunger | to be hungry |
| avoir soif | to have thirst | to be thirsty |
| avoir chaud/froid | to have hot/cold | to be hot/cold |
| avoir peur | to have fear | to be afraid |
| avoir raison/tort | to have reason/wrong | to be right/wrong |
| avoir ans | to have years | to be [age] years old |

This is a genuinely important structural difference from both Spanish (*tener hambre* — actually Spanish agrees with French here!) and English (*to be hungry* — English is the outlier). Your Spanish intuition transfers perfectly on this whole cluster.

---

## 4. Faire — "To Do / To Make"

| Person | Form |
|---|---|
| je fais | fais |
| tu fais | fais |
| il/elle/on fait | fait |
| nous faisons | faisons [fə.zɔ̃] |
| vous faites | faites [fɛt] |
| ils/elles font | font |

**Faire appears in weather, activities, and countless fixed expressions:**
- *il fait beau/chaud/froid* — "it's nice/hot/cold out"
- *faire du sport* — "to play sports"
- *faire les courses* — "to go grocery shopping"
- *faire attention* — "to pay attention"
- *faire la cuisine* — "to cook"

---

## 5. Aller — "To Go"

| Person | Form |
|---|---|
| je vais | vais |
| tu vas | vas |
| il/elle/on va | va |
| nous allons | allons |
| vous allez | allez |
| ils/elles vont | vont |

**Highest-value use: the near future (futur proche)** — aller + infinitive, exactly parallel to Spanish "ir a + infinitive":
> **Je vais manger.** = **Voy a comer.** = "I'm going to eat."

This gives you an entire future tense for free, using a verb you're learning today.

---

## 6. Mental Model: The Four Pillars Holding Up the Rest of the Language

Picture French grammar as a building: être and avoir are the **foundation columns** (every compound tense stands on one of these two), faire is the **utility verb** covering dozens of everyday actions English/Spanish split across many different verbs, and aller is your **bridge to the future tense**, no new conjugation pattern needed. Learning these four deeply now means every future lesson has something solid to attach to.

---

## 7. Common Mistakes

**Spanish speakers:** searching for a French ser/estar split that doesn't exist; occasionally using avoir where French requires être (this becomes relevant in the passé composé lesson).

**English speakers:** translating avoir-idioms literally ("I have hunger" sounds odd in English, so learners under-use the correct avoir pattern); forgetting aller + infinitive and defaulting to a more complex future tense unnecessarily.

---

## 8. Progressively Harder Examples

1. Je suis fatigué. — "I'm tired."
2. J'ai vingt-cinq ans. — "I'm 25 years old."
3. Il fait du sport tous les jours. — "He plays sports every day."
4. Nous allons manger bientôt. — "We're going to eat soon."
5. Est-ce que vous avez faim? Moi, j'ai très faim et je vais faire à manger. — "Are you hungry? I'm very hungry and I'm going to make food."
