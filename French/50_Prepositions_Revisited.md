# Lesson 50 — Prepositions Revisited: Why Natives Choose One Over Another
### Phase 4: Native Naturalness (B2 → C1)

**Competency axes trained:** 🧩 Morphosyntax (primary) · 📚 Lexicon · 🗣️ Productive fluency
**Prerequisite:** Lesson 14 (conceptual map of à/de/en/dans/chez), Lesson 49 (verb + preposition collocations)
**Time investment:** One thorough read, then notice these choices in everything you read and hear.

---

## 1. From Map to Real Choices (Intuitive Explanation)

Lesson 14 gave you the conceptual core of the main prepositions. At B2/C1 the questions become sharper: why *en* two heures but *dans* deux heures? Why *pendant* but *depuis* but *il y a*? Why *par* but *pour*? These pairs are where educated learners still make mistakes, because English and Spanish each carve the territory differently. This lesson works by **contrast pairs**: for each pair, one question decides the choice.

---

## 2. Time Prepositions: Five Words, Five Questions

| Preposition | The question it answers | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **il y a** + duration | How long **ago**? (point in the past) | Je suis arrivé **il y a** deux ans. — "I arrived two years ago." |
| **depuis** + duration/date | Since when / for how long, **still true now**? | J'habite ici **depuis** deux ans. — "I've lived here for two years (and still do)." |
| **pendant** + duration | For how long, **a completed period**? | J'ai habité là **pendant** deux ans. — "I lived there for two years (finished)." |
| **dans** + duration | In how long **from now**? (future) | Je pars **dans** deux heures. — "I'm leaving in two hours." |
| **en** + duration | **How long it takes** to complete something | Je l'ai fait **en** deux heures. — "I did it in two hours." |

**The decisive contrast: dans vs. en.**
- *Je finirai dans deux heures* = starting from now, I'll be done two hours from now (a deadline in the future).
- *Je finirai en deux heures* = the task itself will take two hours (duration of the work).

**Depuis and tense (recall Lesson 37):** French uses the **present** with *depuis* for something still ongoing (*j'habite ici depuis 2020*), where English needs "have lived" and Spanish uses *vivo aquí desde* (parallel to French, an easy transfer for you).

---

## 3. À vs. En vs. Dans (Place and Time)

| Preposition | Core idea | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **à** | point/destination, city, institution as a "location of function" | à Paris, à l'école, à la maison, au travail |
| **en** | no article; inside a country (feminine), a region, a season, a means of transport you sit **inside** | en France, en été, en voiture, en train, en avion |
| **dans** | inside a **bounded, concrete space** (with article), or a future time span | dans la boîte, dans le salon, dans trois jours |

**Transport rule:** *en* for enclosed vehicles you sit inside (*en voiture, en train, en avion, en bus*), *à* for things you straddle or stand on (*à vélo, à pied, à moto, à cheval*). Memory image: if you can be **inside** it, use *en*; if you ride **on** it, use *à*.

**Spanish bridge:** *en* (Spanish) covers all three French prepositions. You must now **choose** between à, en, and dans in French, a good reason to learn them through the contrast above and not through translation.

---

## 4. Par vs. Pour

| Preposition | Core idea | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **pour** | purpose, recipient, destination, exchange | un cadeau **pour** toi, je pars **pour** Lyon, **pour** réussir, merci **pour** ton aide |
| **par** | agent/means/path (the way **through**) | écrit **par** Marie, **par** la fenêtre, **par** hasard, deux fois **par** jour |

**Quick test:** if you can say "**for the sake of / in order to**," use *pour*. If you can say "**by means of / through**," use *par*.

> Il est parti **pour** Paris **par** le train de nuit. — "He left for Paris by (via) the night train."

---

## 5. De vs. Du/De la (Partitive Reminder)

Quantity and negation trigger **de** alone (no article):
- *Je bois du café* → *Je ne bois pas **de** café.*
- *beaucoup **de** gens*, *un peu **de** temps*, *trop **de** travail*, *assez **d'**argent*

**Exceptions:** *être* + noun keeps the article in negation (*ce n'est pas un problème*), and "la plupart" takes *des* (*la plupart des gens*).

---

## 6. Avec, Sans, Chez, Entre, Parmi, Selon, Malgré

| Preposition | Meaning | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **avec** | with | *avec plaisir* (gladly) |
| **sans** | without | no article with abstract nouns: *sans doute*, *sans aucun doute*, *sans problème* |
| **chez** | at the place of | (Lesson 14) |
| **entre** | between (two) | *entre nous* |
| **parmi** | among (many) | contrast with *entre* |
| **selon / d'après** | according to | *selon moi*, *d'après lui* |
| **malgré** | despite | *malgré la pluie* |
| **grâce à / à cause de** | thanks to / because of | positive vs. negative cause |

---

## 7. Mental Model: Prepositions as a Decision Tree

Instead of memorizing 40 words, ask two questions in order:

1. **Is it time or space?**
2. **If time: is it past, ongoing, completed, future, or duration?** → *il y a* / *depuis* / *pendant* / *dans* / *en*
   **If space: is it a point/city (*à*), a country/transport inside (*en*), a bounded interior (*dans*), a person's place (*chez*)?**

For abstract uses (*pour* vs. *par*), ask: **purpose or path?** Once these questions become automatic, the "right" preposition stops feeling arbitrary.

---

## 8. Common Mistakes

**Spanish speakers:** using *en* for everything (*en* two heures vs. *dans* two heures); saying *je suis ici pour deux ans* (wrong: use *depuis* or *pendant*); using *pour* where French needs *par* for agents.

**English speakers:** "for two years" → *pendant* vs. *depuis* vs. *pour* (the English "for" collapses three French words); "in two hours" → *dans* vs. *en*; "by train" → *en train*, not *par train*.

**Both:** the same mistake in a different form: transferring the preposition from the native language instead of choosing from the French contrast.

## 9. Progressively Harder Examples

1. J'étudie le français depuis un an. — "I've been studying French for a year."
2. J'ai habité à Bogotá pendant dix ans, mais j'ai déménagé il y a cinq ans. — "I lived in Bogotá for ten years, but I moved five years ago."
3. Je termine ce projet dans une semaine; si tout va bien, je le finirai en quatre jours de travail. — "I'm finishing this project in a week; if all goes well, I'll finish it in four working days."
4. Elle voyage en train, mais son frère préfère aller à vélo au bureau. — "She travels by train, but her brother prefers to cycle to the office."
5. Selon le rapport, malgré le succès commercial et grâce à des efforts constants, la plupart des employés travaillent sans pause ni gratitude. — "According to the report, despite commercial success and thanks to constant effort, most employees work without break or gratitude."

## 10. Speaking Practice

1. **Contrast drill:** say five sentences about yourself, one for each time preposition (il y a / depuis / pendant / dans / en), using real facts.
2. **Transport quiz:** say "I go to work by ___" with five different transports, choosing *en* or *à*.
3. **Decision-tree narration:** describe your last trip in 6 sentences using at least 4 different prepositions from this lesson.

---

## 11. Mini Quiz (active recall)

1. Say "I've lived here for three years" (still true) and "I lived there for three years" (finished).
2. What is the difference between *dans deux heures* and *en deux heures*?
3. *En voiture* or *à voiture*? *En vélo* or *à vélo*?
4. Pour or par: "a gift ___ you" / "written ___ Marie."
5. Which preposition expresses "despite"?
