# Lesson 74 — Journalistic and Media French: Reading the News Critically
### Phase 5: Mastery & Maintenance (C1 → C2+)

**Competency axes trained:** 📖 Reading · 👂 Listening decoding · 📚 Lexicon · 🌍 Cultural competence
**Prerequisite:** Lesson 31 (passive voice), Lesson 55 (reading ladder), Lesson 56 (immersion ladder), Lesson 69 (cultural literacy)
**Time investment:** One read, then 15 minutes of news per day for four weeks.

---

## 1. Why Media French Is Its Own Dialect (Intuitive Explanation)

The news is the most abundant, free, and current source of high-level French you will ever find, but it has **its own grammar of conventions**: headlines without verbs, the present tense for past events, a special conditional for unconfirmed facts, and verbs of attribution that quietly signal the journalist's attitude. If you do not know these conventions, you will misread what the text claims to be true.

Learning them gives you three benefits:
1. **Comprehension:** you decode headlines and radio bulletins faster.
2. **Critical reading:** you notice *how* a claim is framed, not only what it says.
3. **Vocabulary:** news is a steady stream of high-frequency, formal vocabulary and collocations (Lesson 49).

## 3. Sections and Genres of a Newspaper

### Sections (*rubriques*)
*À la une* (front page), *Politique*, *International*, *Société*, *Économie*, *Planète / Environnement*, *Sciences*, *Culture*, *Sport*, *Idées / Débats*, *Faits divers* (crime and minor news), *Météo*.

### Genres (*genres journalistiques*)
| Genre | What it is | Recognizable by |
|---|---|---|
| **Brève** | very short item | 2–4 sentences |
| **Dépêche** | agency wire story | factual, neutral, dated |
| **Article d'information** | standard news report | who/what/where/when, quotes |
| **Reportage** | on-the-scene description | present tense, vivid detail |
| **Enquête** | investigation | sources, documents, long form |
| **Interview / entretien** | Q&A | questions in bold |
| **Portrait** | profile of a person | narrative, adjectives |
| **Analyse / décryptage** | explanation of events | *comment, pourquoi, enjeux* |
| **Éditorial** | the newspaper's stated position | unsigned or signed by editor, argumentative |
| **Tribune** | opinion piece by outside contributor | *Je, nous*, strong claims |
| **Chronique / billet** | regular column | personal voice, humor |

**Critical habit:** always identify the genre before reading. Treating an *éditorial* or *tribune* as a neutral report is a classic misreading.

### Parts of an article
*Le titre* (headline), *le surtitre*, *le chapeau* (lead paragraph/standfirst), *l'intertitre* (subheading), *la légende* (photo caption), *le corps du texte*, *la signature / le byline*, *la source*.

## 4. The Grammar of Headlines

| Convention | Example | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Present tense for past events** | *Le gouvernement annonce une réforme.* | the *présent de narration*; the event just happened |
| **Nominal headlines** (no verb) | *Hausse des prix de l'énergie* | very frequent, high density |
| **Past participle only** | *Un accord trouvé à Bruxelles* | passive without *être* |
| **Colon structure** | *Réforme des retraites : le Sénat adopte le texte* | topic : development |
| **Quotes as headlines** | *« Nous ne céderons pas »* | words of a source, in guillemets |
| **Question headline** | *Faut-il interdire les écrans aux enfants ?* | opens debate, often analysis |
| **Future with *va*** | *Le président va s'exprimer ce soir* | near future (Lesson 19) |
| **Puns and wordplay** | *Le prix de la rentrée* | frequent in magazines and culture sections (Lesson 65) |
| **Abbreviations** | *PS, LR, RN, CGT, SNCF, PME, CAC 40, PIB, UE, OMS* | learn the frequent ones |

**Example:** *Inflation : les prix à la consommation stables en octobre, selon l'Insee* = *Inflation: consumer prices stable in October, according to Insee.*

## 5. The Journalistic Conditional (Le Conditionnel de l'Information Incertaine)

A hallmark of French news: the **conditional marks information that is not confirmed.** It does not mean "would," it means "reportedly / allegedly / according to unconfirmed sources."

| Sentence | Reading |
|---|---|
| Le ministre **démissionnerait** demain. | The minister is reportedly going to resign; unconfirmed. |
| Deux personnes **auraient été blessées**. | Two people were reportedly injured. |
| Le suspect **serait** en fuite. | The suspect is said to be on the run. |
| Selon nos informations, le projet **serait** abandonné. | According to our information, the project is reportedly being dropped. |

**Contrast:** *Le ministre démissionne demain.* (confirmed) vs. *Le ministre démissionnerait demain.* (rumor).

**Why it matters:** if you read the conditional as a hypothetical, you may think a story is a possibility rather than a rumor, and the reverse. This is one of the most important keys to understanding French news.

## 6. Verbs of Attribution: Reading the Journalist's Attitude

How a source's words are reported reveals the framing.

| Verb | Tone | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **déclarer, dire, annoncer, indiquer, préciser** | neutral | *Le porte-parole a déclaré que...* |
| **affirmer, soutenir, assurer, garantir** | the source claims with confidence | *Il affirme que le budget est respecté.* |
| **prétendre** | skeptical (the journalist doubts) | *Elle prétend ne rien savoir.* |
| **admettre, reconnaître, avouer, concéder** | the source concedes something | *Il reconnaît des erreurs.* |
| **dénoncer, accuser, reprocher** | criticism | *L'opposition dénonce une manœuvre.* |
| **souligner, insister, rappeler** | emphasis | *Elle souligne que...* |
| **nier, démentir, contester** | denial | *Le ministère dément ces informations.* |
| **révéler, indiquer, faire savoir** | disclosure | *Le journal révèle que...* |
| **s'inquiéter, déplorer, regretter** | negative evaluation | *Les syndicats déplorent un manque de dialogue.* |
| **se féliciter, saluer, se réjouir** | positive evaluation | *Le gouvernement se félicite de ce résultat.* |

**Practice:** in any article, list the attribution verbs for each speaker. Do the verbs treat all sources the same way?

### Attribution phrases
*selon, d'après, de source proche de..., de source officielle, comme l'indique..., à en croire..., aux dires de..., si l'on en croit...*

## 8. Listening to the News: Radio and TV Conventions

| Format | What it is | Learner tips |
|---|---|---|
| **Le journal** (e.g., 8 heures) | 10–15 minute bulletin | structured, formal, fast |
| **Flash info** | 1–2 minute update | very dense |
| **La revue de presse** | summary of the press | many quotes, sources |
| **L'invité du matin** | interview with a public figure | tests your comprehension of debate |
| **L'édito** | commentary | argumentative, rhetorical (Lesson 65) |
| **Le débat** | multiple speakers | overlap, speed, politics |
| **Le reportage** | field report | background noise, natural speech |

**Tips:**
- Start with RFI *Journal en français facile* (slow) and then *franceinfo* flashes.
- Listen to the same story in text and audio.
- Use the **five-pass technique** (Lesson 56) on a 3-minute bulletin.
- Note the newsreader's rhythm: groups of 4–7 syllables with a rising continuation pitch, falling at the end (Lesson 59).

## 9. Critical Reading: A Checklist

Ask these questions of any article:

1. **Genre:** news report, analysis, editorial, or opinion?
2. **Source:** who is speaking, and how do we know (*selon qui ?*)? Is there a named source or only *une source proche du dossier* (anonymous)?
3. **Verbs of attribution:** neutral or loaded?
4. **Conditional:** what is confirmed and what is only reported?
5. **Date and updates:** when was it published (*publié le*) and modified (*mis à jour le*)?
6. **Omissions:** what perspective is missing?
7. **Vocabulary:** are the adjectives and nouns neutral (*manifestants*) or charged (*émeutiers*)? Compare to another outlet.
8. **Verification:** does a fact-checking service cover the claim?

### Fact-checking resources (French-language)
*Les Décodeurs* (Le Monde), *CheckNews* (Libération), *AFP Factuel*, *Les Surligneurs* (legal fact-checking), plus the vocabulary: *une infox / une fausse information* (fake news), *une désinformation*, *une rumeur*, *une théorie du complot*, *démentir*, *vérifier*, *recouper les sources* (cross-check).

**Important:** being critical does not mean being cynical. Cross-checking and noticing framing are ordinary habits of an informed reader in any language.

## 10. Mental Model: Reading Through Three Lenses

Read every news item through **three lenses**:

1. **Facts lens:** what happened, according to whom, and how certain is it (indicative vs. conditional)?
2. **Frame lens:** which words, verbs, and order of information shape the impression?
3. **Context lens:** what French institutions, history, and references (Lesson 69) are assumed?

At first you will mostly use lens 1. Over months, lenses 2 and 3 switch on automatically, and your reading becomes both faster and sharper.

## 11. Common Mistakes

**Spanish speakers:** the Spanish press also uses a conditional of rumor (*habría*, *sería*), so you have a head start; but watch false friends (*une actualité* = a current event, *actuellement* = currently; *un titulaire* ≠ *titular*; *une conférence* = a lecture; *la presse* vs. *la prensa* is fine); and don't assume Spanish headline order.

**English speakers:** reading the conditional as hypothetical; missing nominal headlines; translating *Il a été annoncé que...* as "it was announced that" without noticing the impersonal passive; confusing *actuel(lement)* with "actual(ly)".

**Both:** assuming every outlet is neutral or that every claim in the text is verified; skipping the byline and the genre; not looking up institutional nicknames (*Bercy, Matignon, Quai d'Orsay*, Lesson 69); losing the thread in long sentences with embedded relative clauses (Lesson 42).

## 12. Progressively Harder Practice

1. **Headline decoding (10 min/day):** read 10 headlines. For each, rewrite it as a full sentence (restoring the verb and tense).
2. **Conditional hunt:** find five uses of the journalistic conditional in articles. Mark what is confirmed and what is reported.
3. **Attribution verbs:** in one article, list every attribution verb and classify (neutral, claim, concession, criticism, denial).
4. **Same-story comparison:** read one story in two outlets. Write 5 sentences in French on how the framing differs (verbs, adjectives, quotes selected).
5. **Listen and summarize:** listen to a 5-minute *franceinfo* bulletin, then summarize four stories in 8–10 sentences aloud.
6. **Write a news brief:** write a *brève* (60–80 words) on a real event, with a headline in nominal form, a chapeau, and one quote with a correct attribution verb. Then rewrite it as an *analyse* (150 words).
7. **Weekly habit:** one daily podcast or bulletin, one weekly longer article, one monthly *enquête* or *reportage*.
