# Lesson 80 — Maintenance Mode: Monthly Review Cycles and Optional Deep Dives
### Phase 5: Mastery & Maintenance (C1 → C2+)

**Competency axes trained:** All eight axes (long-term maintenance and growth)
**Prerequisite:** Lesson 62 (mastery system), Lesson 79 (final capstone and long-term plan)
**Time investment:** One read, then a 30–60 minute monthly review ritual.

---

## 1. Where You Are Now (Intuitive Explanation)

The core curriculum is complete. From here, your French will either **grow** or **fade**, depending on how you use it. Research on language attrition shows that skills do not vanish quickly at an advanced level, but **active skills (speaking, writing, quick vocabulary retrieval) decay first** if unused, while **receptive skills (reading, listening) are more stable**. The risk is not forgetting everything; it is slowly losing speed, precision, and ease.

Maintenance mode has two goals:
1. **Prevent decay:** a small, regular dose of all skills.
2. **Keep growing:** targeted deep dives into areas that matter to you.

You no longer need a curriculum to tell you what to do; you need a **calendar, a feedback loop, and a menu of interesting directions**. This lesson provides all three.

---

## 2. The Minimum Effective Dose

Even in busy periods, a tiny routine protects your level.

| Level | Time | What it protects |
|---|---|---|
| **Survival (busy weeks)** | 10 min/day | one podcast segment or 5 pages; keeps the ear and reading habit |
| **Maintenance (normal)** | 3–4 hours/week | all skills at current level (Lesson 79 routine) |
| **Growth** | 5–7 hours/week | measurable progress toward C2 or a specific goal |

**Rule from Lesson 62:** never miss twice. If you skip a day, do the survival version the next day.

**The four non-negotiables for maintenance:**
1. **Hear** French every day (podcast, radio, series).
2. **Read** French several times a week (news, novel, essay).
3. **Speak** French at least weekly with a real person (tutor, partner, friend).
4. **Write** French at least weekly (journal, message, email, review).

## 3. The Monthly Review Ritual (30–60 Minutes)

Once a month, on a fixed day (for example, the first Sunday), run this cycle.

### Step 1: Record (5 minutes)
Record a **2-minute spontaneous monologue** on a rotating prompt (see Section 4). Save it with the date in the filename.

### Step 2: Listen and Diagnose (10 minutes)
Listen once and note:
- Number of long pauses (over 3 seconds)
- Number of switches to Spanish/English
- Three errors (grammar, vocabulary, pronunciation)
- One thing you did well

### Step 3: Compare (5 minutes)
Compare with last month's recording: is fluency up, errors down, vocabulary richer?

### Step 4: Error journal update (10 minutes)
Add your three errors to the journal with the correct form and the rule (Lesson 62). Count recurrences: the top five errors of the quarter become your targets.

### Step 5: Mining log review (10 minutes)
Review items from the last 30 days by recall (cover the French, produce it from a cue). Items you cannot produce return to spaced repetition (days 2, 7, 21).

### Step 6: Plan next month (10 minutes)
Choose:
- One **deep dive** (Section 6)
- One **new genre or source** (a podcast, author, or creator)
- One **stretch goal** (e.g., read a full novel; hold a 30-minute conversation without English)

### Step 7: Log it (5 minutes)
Fill in a one-line entry in your tracker:

```
DATE: ___   MONOLOGUE: ___ min   PAUSES: ___   SWITCHES: ___
TOP ERRORS: ___   WIN: ___   DEEP DIVE: ___   STRETCH: ___
```

Rotate through these (2 minutes each), so that you practice different registers and functions.

| Month | Prompt (in French) | Skill emphasis |

| 2 | *Expliquez comment fonctionne un outil ou un processus de votre métier.* | technical explanation (Lesson 53) |
| 3 | *Donnez votre opinion sur un sujet d'actualité.* | argumentation, subjunctive |

## 5. Quarterly and Annual Checkpoints

### Quarterly (every 3 months, about 2 hours)
1. Redo **Tasks 3, 5, and 7** from the Final Capstone (Lesson 79) in short form.
2. Update your skill profile (0–4 per axis) and compare with the last quarter.
3. Review your **top five recurring errors**: are they shrinking?
4. Adjust your weekly plan: raise the dose in your weakest axis.

### Annual (once a year, 4–6 hours)
1. Redo the **whole Final Capstone** or take an official test (**DELF/DALF**, **TCF**, **TEF**).
2. Re-read your first recordings and writings: celebrate progress.
3. Set new 12-month goals.
4. Replace or add learning resources: new podcasts, authors, tutors.

## 6. Menu of Optional Deep Dives

Choose according to your life, career, and curiosity. You can do them in any order, one per month, or ask for a lesson on any of them.

### A. Specialized registers and professions
| Deep dive | What you would study |
|---|---|
| **Legal and contractual French** | contracts, clauses, legal vocabulary, reading terms and conditions |
| **Medical and health French** | consultations, symptoms, health system navigation, patient–doctor dialogue |
| **Scientific writing and conference French** | abstracts, methods, results, presentations, peer-review language |
| **Business and finance French** | reports, financial statements, markets, negotiation, marketing |
| **Technology and engineering French** | documentation, specifications, code review language, incident reports |
| **Teaching and academic French** | lectures, seminars, supervision, grant applications |

### B. Language-focused deep dives
| Deep dive | What you would study |
|---|---|
| **Test preparation (DELF B2 / DALF C1 / C2 / TCF)** | exam formats, scoring, strategies, mock tests |
| **Advanced grammar clinic** | rare tenses, advanced subordination, stylistic inversion, historical present |
| **Phonetics clinic** | accent reduction, intonation of specific genres, regional accents |
| **Etymology and word families** | Latin and Greek roots, historical changes, links to Spanish and English |
| **Slang, verlan, and informal registers** | youth language, social media, music, regional slang |
| **Dialects and Francophone varieties** | deeper study of Québécois, Belgian, Swiss, West African, Maghrebi, Antillean French |

### C. Literature, arts, and culture
| Deep dive | What you would study |
|---|---|
| **Classic literature reading club** | Maupassant, Camus, Colette, Duras, Ernaux, Yourcenar |
| **Poetry** | meter, rhyme, major poets, reading and reciting aloud |
| **Theatre** | Molière, Racine, modern drama; reading and acting scenes |
| **Cinema** | film vocabulary, Nouvelle Vague, contemporary directors, analyzing dialogue |
| **Music and songwriting** | chanson française, rap, lyrics analysis, rhyme and wordplay |
| **Philosophy and ideas** | key French thinkers, argument analysis, reading philosophical texts |
| **History and politics** | focused modules on specific periods, institutions, and current debates |
| **Gastronomy and regional cultures** | food vocabulary, regional specialties, wine, cooking in French |

### D. Skills and applications
| Deep dive | What you would study |
|---|---|
| **Translation and interpreting** | advanced translation, consecutive interpreting, terminology work |
| **Creative writing** | short stories, dialogue, style, revision |
| **Public speaking and debate** | speeches, rhetoric, debate formats, Q&A |
| **Podcasting / YouTube in French** | scripting, recording, vocabulary of media production |
| **Teaching French or tutoring Spanish speakers** | explaining grammar, designing exercises |

**How to choose:** pick the deep dive that (a) connects to a real use case in the next three months and (b) gives you at least some enjoyment. Motivation outweighs optimization.

## 7. How to Run a Deep Dive (A Four-Week Template)

| Week | Focus | Activities |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Map** | scope and vocabulary | read 2–3 authentic sources; build a 30-item lexicon (word families and collocations) |
| **2. Absorb** | input at volume | read/listen to 4–5 hours of material in the topic; mine 5 items per session |
| **3. Produce** | output | write a 300-word piece and give a 3-minute talk on the topic; get feedback |
| **4. Consolidate** | review and test | retrieval quiz on the lexicon; redo the talk; log what you will keep using |

At the end, write a **one-page summary in French** of what you learned, and add the best phrases to your mining log.

## 8. Troubleshooting: Common Maintenance Problems

| Problem | Likely cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| **"I feel stuck."** | plateau, comfortable repetition | change genre or format; add a stretch goal; get external feedback |
| **"I'm losing words."** | low active use | do retrieval (flashcards from cues), write more, speak weekly |
| **"I understand but can't speak."** | not enough output | schedule a regular speaking partner; record monologues |
| **"My errors never go away."** | fossilization | log recurring errors; drill 10 targeted sentences per error across days; ask for corrections |
| **"I have no time."** | routine too heavy | use the survival version (10 min/day); attach to existing habits |
| **"I'm bored."** | content is not engaging | choose topics you love; try a new medium (comics, games, sports commentary) |
| **"I'm intimidated by native speed."** | limited exposure to fast speech | shadowing, 1.25× playback, sitcom dialogue with subtitles |
| **"I don't have anyone to speak with."** | limited network | tutors, exchange apps, local Alliance Française events, online communities, book clubs |
| **"I stopped for weeks."** | life events | restart with the survival dose; do a 2-day immersion reboot; re-record a monologue to see what you actually lost (usually less than you fear) |

### The "reboot weekend" protocol
After a long break:
1. **Friday:** 1 hour of listening (podcast or series).
2. **Saturday:** 1 hour of reading, 30 minutes of writing, 30 minutes of speaking (voice notes or a tutor).
3. **Sunday:** record a 2-minute monologue, review errors, and plan the week.
You will likely find that your passive skills are intact and your active skills return within days.

## 9. Staying Connected to the Language

French becomes durable when it is **part of your life**, not a task.

- **People:** a regular conversation partner, a French-speaking friend or group, a tutor, a language exchange.
- **Places:** French-speaking communities, cultural centers (*Alliance Française*), film festivals, book clubs, travel to French-speaking regions.
- **Media:** a podcast habit, a favorite series, a newsletter, a YouTube creator, a French playlist.
- **Projects:** a blog or journal in French, a translation project, a hobby done in French (cooking, football, photography, gaming).
- **Identity:** think of yourself as a Spanish–English–French speaker. This changes how you notice and use the language.

---

## 10. Mental Model: A Sourdough Starter

A sourdough starter stays alive and improves with **small, regular feedings**, not with occasional big batches. French at your level is similar: ten minutes a day, a weekly conversation, and a monthly check-up keep it alive and strengthening. Skipping a feeding or two doesn't kill it; ignoring it for months makes it sluggish, but a good feeding revives it. Feed it often, vary the flour, and bake something with it regularly (use it for real purposes).
