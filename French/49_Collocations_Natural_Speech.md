# Lesson 49 — Collocations: Why Native Speech Sounds Natural
### Phase 4: Native Naturalness (B2 → C1)

**Competency axes trained:** 📚 Lexicon (primary) · 🗣️ Productive fluency · 🎭 Pragmatics/Register
**Prerequisite:** Lesson 17 (prendre une décision), Lesson 34 and 48 (idioms and fixed chunks)
**Time investment:** Reference + drill lesson. Collect collocations daily from everything you read and hear.

---

## 1. The Hidden Skeleton of Fluency (Intuitive Explanation)

A **collocation** is a pair or group of words that native speakers habitually use together, where a synonym would sound odd even though it is grammatically and logically correct. You met your first one in Lesson 17: *prendre une décision*, never *faire une décision*. Nothing in the grammar forbids "faire une décision." Speakers simply never say it.

This is the single biggest difference between "correct" French and "native-sounding" French. Learners build sentences word by word, while natives retrieve **prefabricated chunks**. When your instinct about what "sounds natural" improves (one of your stated goals), it is largely your collocation database growing.

---

## 2. Why Collocations Are Arbitrary but Systematic

Collocations are not logical, which is why translation fails:

| Concept | French | Literal (wrong) transfer |
|---|---|---|
| to make a mistake | **faire une erreur** | ~~*commettre*~~ (formal only) |
| to take a decision | **prendre une décision** | ~~faire une décision~~ |
| to pay attention | **faire attention** | ~~payer attention~~ |
| to have a look | **jeter un coup d'œil** | ~~avoir un regard~~ |
| to catch a cold | **attraper un rhume** | ~~prendre un froid~~ (this one exists, but means "to get chilled") |
| heavy rain | **une pluie battante / de fortes pluies** | ~~pluie lourde~~ |
| to lose time | **perdre du temps** | (matches English and Spanish) |
| strong coffee | **un café serré / fort** | ~~café puissant~~ |

**Systematic part:** the same verbs recur as collocation hubs (Lessons 17, 36, 39). *Faire*, *prendre*, *mettre*, *donner*, *avoir*, and *porter* "light verbs" carry little meaning on their own and combine with nouns to form the real predicate.

---

## 3. The Big Light-Verb Hubs

### Faire (to do/make), actions and activities
- faire attention, faire un effort, faire une erreur, faire une pause
- faire des études, faire du sport, faire les courses, faire la cuisine
- faire un voyage, faire une promenade, faire la queue, faire la connaissance de quelqu'un
- faire plaisir à quelqu'un, faire peur, faire mal

### Prendre (to take), decisions, time, means
- prendre une décision, prendre un risque, prendre une douche, prendre des notes
- prendre le temps de, prendre rendez-vous, prendre un verre, prendre soin de
- prendre congé, prendre la parole, prendre en compte

### Avoir (to have), states and feelings (Lesson 7)
- avoir besoin de, avoir envie de, avoir l'air + adjective, avoir lieu
- avoir raison / tort, avoir confiance en, avoir de la chance
- avoir du mal à + infinitive, avoir le temps de

### Mettre (to put), placing, starting, effort
- mettre du temps à, mettre la table, mettre fin à, mettre en place
- mettre en valeur, mettre au point, mettre en évidence

### Donner (to give)
- donner un coup de main, donner son avis, donner rendez-vous
- donner naissance à, donner lieu à, donner envie de

## 4. Adjective + Noun Collocations (Intensity)

French strongly prefers specific intensifiers with specific nouns:

| Noun | Natural intensifier | Not natural |
|---|---|---|
| une pluie | battante, torrentielle | ~~forte~~ (acceptable but less vivid) |
| un échec | cuisant, retentissant | ~~grand~~ |
| un succès | franc, retentissant | ~~gros~~ |
| une erreur | grossière, grave | ~~énorme~~ (colloquial) |
| un silence | pesant, total | ~~lourd~~ |
| un malentendu | total, regrettable | ~~gros~~ |
| une faim | de loup | ~~grande~~ |

## 5. Verb + Preposition Collocations

Many verbs are tied to a specific preposition with no logic (Lesson 14's "concept" approach reaches its limit here, and you simply store the pair):

| Verb | Collocation | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| penser | **à** quelque chose | to think about |
| rêver | **de** | to dream of/about |
| dépendre | **de** | to depend on |
| s'intéresser | **à** | to be interested in |
| s'habituer | **à** | to get used to |
| compter | **sur** | to count on |
| réussir | **à** + infinitive | to succeed in |
| tenir | **à** | to care about, be attached to |

**Spanish bridge:** *pensar en*, *soñar con*, *depender de*, *interesarse por*, *acostumbrarse a*, *contar con*. Spanish and French prepositions diverge frequently here, so make the French pairing automatic from the start.

## 7. Mental Model: Words Have Friends

Picture each word as a person at a party. Some pairs stand together all night (*prendre* and *décision*), others never speak to each other (*faire* and *décision*), even though introducing them would make perfect sense. Fluency means knowing **who stands next to whom**. This is why the program teaches vocabulary in chunks and not isolated words: you store *prendre une décision* as one unit, and the "wrong" version simply feels strange.

**Memory technique:** keep a collocation notebook with the **noun in the center** and its common verbs and adjectives around it ("word spider"). For *décision*: prendre, annoncer, reporter, remettre en question; difficile, lourde, unanime, collective.

---

## 8. Common Mistakes

**Spanish speakers:** transferring Spanish collocations directly, since they often differ even when the words match: *hacer una pregunta* → **poser** une question (not "faire"); *tomar un café* → **prendre** un café (matches); *hacer una decisión* → **prendre** une décision; *dar un paseo* → **faire** une promenade.

**English speakers:** "make a decision" → *prendre*, "take a picture" → *prendre une photo* (matches), "do a favor" → *rendre un service* (not "faire"), "make a mistake" → *faire une erreur* (matches). Also avoid "make" → "faire" as a blanket rule.

**Both groups:** using a "logical" synonym for the right collocation (*exécuter* a decision instead of *prendre*). The error is subtle, grammatical, and exactly the kind of thing that marks a speaker as non-native.

---

## 9. Progressively Harder Examples

1. J'ai pris une décision difficile hier soir. — "I made a difficult decision last night."
2. Il faut respecter les délais et tenir nos engagements. — "We have to meet deadlines and keep our commitments."
3. Après avoir essuyé un échec cuisant, l'équipe a mis un projet en place pour relever le défi. — "After suffering a bitter failure, the team set up a project to take up the challenge."
4. Elle a eu du mal à s'habituer à l'idée, mais elle a fini par faire confiance à son équipe. — "She had trouble getting used to the idea, but she ended up trusting her team."
5. En mettant en évidence ces erreurs, le rapport soulève plusieurs questions et invite à tirer des conclusions prudentes. — "By highlighting these errors, the report raises several questions and invites cautious conclusions."

## 10. Speaking Practice

1. **Chunk shadowing:** read the hub lists in Section 3 aloud, one hub per day, with a natural rhythm (chunks should flow as single units, as in Lesson 5's connected speech).
2. **Noun-first drill:** choose a noun from your own work or field. List three verbs and two adjectives that "belong" with it in French (use a monolingual dictionary or search "*[nom]* collocations").
3. **Spontaneous production:** describe your last week in 6 sentences using at least four light-verb collocations.
