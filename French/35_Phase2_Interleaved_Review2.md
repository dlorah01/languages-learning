# Lesson 35 — Phase 2 Interleaved Review #2: Passive Voice, Register, Idioms
### Phase 2: Expansion (A2 → B1) — Review Checkpoint

**Competency axes trained:** All Phase 2 axes so far, interleaved
**Purpose:** Consolidate Lessons 31–34 (passive voice, business register, academic discourse, Tier 1 idioms) before continuing into the remaining verb and vocabulary depth of Phase 2.

---

## 1. Why This Second Checkpoint

Lessons 31–34 shifted focus from pure grammar (Lessons 26–29) toward register, professional/academic contexts, and natural idiomatic speech. This is exactly the kind of "pragmatic layer" that separates textbook French from natural French — worth consolidating deliberately before moving forward.

---

## 2. Mixed Retrieval Practice

1. What's the more natural spoken alternative to "Le français est parlé ici" (a passive construction)?
2. Give one formal email closing phrase from Lesson 32.
3. What's the difference between "cependant" and "pourtant"?
4. What does "j'ai la flemme" mean, and why can't you translate it word-for-word?
5. Name one subjunctive-triggering phrase used in academic argument (from Lesson 33).
6. What's the idiom for "little by little"?
7. Why is agreement mandatory in the être-passive?
8. Give one hedging phrase used to soften professional disagreement.

---

## 3. Answer Key

1. On parle français ici.
2. Je vous prie d'agréer, Madame, Monsieur, l'expression de mes salutations distinguées. (or the simpler "Cordialement,")
3. Cependant = more formal/written; pourtant = more spoken/informal-leaning, both roughly "however."
4. "To not feel like doing something" — literal translation ("to have the laziness") doesn't convey the idiomatic meaning.
5. Il se peut que... / Il n'est pas certain que...
6. petit à petit
7. Because être is always the auxiliary in the passive, and être-verbs always trigger subject agreement (Lesson 12's rule).
8. Il me semble que... / Ce serait peut-être plus efficace de...

---

## 4. Combined Production Task

Write or say a short paragraph (4–5 sentences) that:
- Uses "on" instead of a formal passive at least once
- Includes one professional/polite conditional request
- Uses at least one academic connector (cependant, par conséquent, etc.)
- Includes at least one Tier 1 idiom naturally
