---

## 2. Formation: Futur Simple Stem + Imparfait Endings

The conditional is built from two pieces you already know: the **futur simple stem** (usually the infinitive, or an irregular stem) + the **imparfait endings** from Lesson 13.

| Person | Ending |
|---|---|
| je | -ais |
| tu | -ais |
| il/elle/on | -ait |
| nous | -ions |
| vous | -iez |
| ils/elles | -aient |

**Example — parler:** parler- (infinitive stem) + ais/ais/ait/ions/iez/aient → je parlerais, tu parlerais, il parlerait, nous parlerions, vous parleriez, ils parleraient

**Irregular stems (same stems used for futur simple, worth learning together):**

| Verb | Conditional stem |
|---|---|
| être | ser- (je serais) |
| avoir | aur- (j'aurais) |
| aller | ir- (j'irais) |
| faire | fer- (je ferais) |
| pouvoir | pourr- (je pourrais) |
| vouloir | voudr- (je voudrais) |
| devoir | devr- (je devrais) |
| venir | viendr- (je viendrais) |

## 3. Use Case 1: Politeness (Lesson 19 formalized)

> **Je voudrais un café.** — "I would like a coffee."
> **Pourriez-vous m'aider?** — "Could you help me?"
> **Auriez-vous l'heure?** — "Would you have the time?"

---

## 4. Use Case 2: Hypothetical/Speculation

> **À ta place, je partirais tout de suite.** — "In your place, I would leave right away."
> **Ça serait bien de voyager plus.** — "It would be nice to travel more."

---

## 5. Use Case 3: The First Si Clause — Hypothetical Present/Future (Type 2)

French has three "si" (if) clause patterns; this lesson covers the most common one for speculative/hypothetical situations:

**Formula: Si + imparfait, ... conditionnel**

> **Si j'avais plus de temps, je voyagerais plus.** — "If I had more time, I would travel more."
> **Si tu me le demandais, je t'aiderais.** — "If you asked me, I'd help you."

**Direct Spanish parallel:** si + imperfecto de subjuntivo, condicional (si tuviera más tiempo, viajaría más) — the *structure* (si + a past-flavored form, then conditional) is genuinely parallel, even though French uses plain imparfait here rather than a subjunctive form — a small but important divergence worth flagging: **French's si-clause never uses the subjunctive**, unlike Spanish, which does. This is a specific exception to remember.

---

## 6. Mental Model: The Conditional as "The Future, Softened by the Past"

Recall the conditional's formation: future stem + imparfait (past) endings. This isn't a coincidence — conceptually, the conditional describes a **future-oriented outcome, viewed through a hypothetical or softened lens** (the "past-flavored" endings signal that distance/unreality). It's literally "the future" grammatically infused with "pastness" to mark it as unreal, polite, or contingent — a nice payoff of understanding both source tenses deeply (Lesson 13's imparfait, and futur simple which you now have the stems for).

---

## 7. Common Mistakes

**Spanish speakers:** trying to use a subjunctive-flavored form in the "si" clause (transferring Spanish's "si tuviera" logic too literally) — remember, French's si-clause always uses plain imparfait, never subjunctive.

**English speakers:** confusing conditional with future simple since both can translate loosely as "would/will" depending on context; forgetting the irregular stems (voudr-, pourr-, etc.) and trying to regularize them.

## 8. Progressively Harder Examples

1. Je voudrais visiter la France un jour. — "I would like to visit France one day."
2. Si j'étais toi, je ne dirais rien. — "If I were you, I wouldn't say anything."
3. Nous pourrions partir plus tôt si tu voulais. — "We could leave earlier if you wanted."
4. Si elle avait de l'argent, elle achèterait une maison. — "If she had money, she would buy a house."
5. Que ferais-tu si tu gagnais à la loterie? — "What would you do if you won the lottery?"
