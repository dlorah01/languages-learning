# Lesson 63 — Turn-Taking, Interrupting Politely, and Signaling You're Listening
### Phase 6: Natural, Spoken English | Module 5 of 5 (Phase capstone)

---

## 0. Where this fits
This is the last lesson of Phase 6. You now have softeners (59), connecting words (60), agreement and disagreement (61), and small-talk rituals (62). One thing remains: **the machinery that lets people share the floor.**

Who speaks when? How do you enter a conversation that is already moving? How do you stop someone without insulting them? How do you show you are listening when you cannot nod, for example on a phone or video call? These questions are rarely taught, yet they decide whether a conversation feels **smooth, respectful, and balanced** or **tense, rude, or one-sided**.

For Spanish speakers this lesson is especially valuable, because the *default style* in many Spanish-speaking settings is **high involvement**: fast pace, overlapping talk, interruptions that show enthusiasm, finishing each other's sentences. In many English-speaking professional settings, the default is closer to **high considerateness**: fewer overlaps, short gaps, interrupting with a signal and a softener. Neither is "better." But the same behavior can be read as warm in one and aggressive in the other.

Connections: Lesson 59 (softeners: *Could I just…?*), Lesson 60 (*well, so, anyway, I mean* hold and shift turns; backchannels), Lesson 61 (disagreeing without damage), Lesson 62 (conversation openings and exits), Phase 7 and 8 (intonation, listening, speaking).

> **Core idea:** conversation is a shared floor. You **signal** before you enter, **wait** for a gap, **enter** with a short softened bridge, make a **short** point, and **yield** the floor clearly. While others speak, you **show** you're listening.

---

## 1. Intuitive Explanation
Think of conversation as a road with traffic lights. Nobody tells you the rules out loud, but everyone watches the lights:

- 🟢 **Green (go ahead):** the speaker's voice drops and stops, they look at you, they ask a question, or they say something like *What do you think?*
- 🟡 **Amber (careful):** the speaker pauses with *um…*, uses a flat rising tone, or says *and…*; they are thinking, not finished.
- 🔴 **Red (stop):** the speaker speeds up, raises volume, or says *Hold on, let me finish.*

Native speakers read these signals automatically. If you enter on red, you are "running a light." If you wait for a perfect green that never comes, you never speak. The art is to **spot the early amber-to-green moments** and enter with a polite phrase.

At the same time, **listeners have a job**: they send small signals (*mm-hm, right, I see, really?*, nods) that say, *"Keep going, I'm with you."* When these signals are missing, speakers become anxious: *Are you bored? Do you understand? Are you angry?*

So turn-taking has two parts:
1. **Speaker's job:** signal when you're done or not done, and share the floor.
2. **Listener's job:** show attention, wait for the right moment, and enter politely.

## 2. The Linguistic Explanation

### 2.1 How turns work (basic mechanics)
Conversation analysts (Sacks, Schegloff, and Jefferson) describe turns as units of speech ending at points where a change of speaker becomes possible. These points are called **transition relevance places (TRPs)**. Typical features of a TRP:
- the sentence is **grammatically complete**;
- the pitch **falls** (statement) or **rises** (yes/no question);
- the speaker **slows down** or stops;
- the speaker **looks at** the next speaker (face-to-face);
- a **question** or **tag** is added (*…don't you think?*);
- the speaker uses a **closing phrase** (*…and that's it, really. / So, yeah.*).

The typical gap between turns in conversation is very short, often a fraction of a second. English-speaking professional settings tend to accept **short gaps** and **little overlap**. A silence of two or three seconds begins to feel awkward.

### 2.2 Four kinds of turn signals

| Function | What it does | Verbal tools | Non-verbal / prosodic tools |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Yielding** (give the floor) | "Your turn" | *What do you think? / Over to you. / Any thoughts? / Anything to add?* | falling pitch, pause, eye contact, open palm, slowing down |
| **Holding** (keep the floor) | "I'm not finished" | *and… / so… / the thing is… / first of all… / there are three points…* / filled pauses *um* | level or rising mid-sentence pitch, no pause at sentence end, quicker speed, lifted hand, continuing gaze away |
| **Claiming** (take the floor) | "I'd like to speak" | *Can I come in here? / Sorry, can I just add something? / If I could just say… / May I?* | inbreath, leaning forward, raising a finger/hand, starting a sound (*Well… / So…*) |
| **Passing** (give it to someone specific) | "You next" | *Ana, what's your view? / I'd like to hear from Luis.* | gesturing, eye contact, name |

### 2.3 Entering a conversation (joining a group)
**Observe** first: listen for the topic and the mood. Then:
- *Mind if I join you? / Is this seat taken? / Hi, I hope I'm not interrupting.*
- Enter with a **relevant, positive** comment: *Sorry to jump in, but I couldn't help hearing you mention Cartagena. I'm from there!*
- Don't change the topic abruptly; link: *That reminds me…*
- Introduce yourself if you're new: *Hi, I'm Camila.*

### 2.4 Interrupting politely: a ladder

Not all interruptions are bad. Sometimes you need to ask a clarification, correct something urgent, or add important information. **Strength of interruption should match urgency and the relationship.**

| Level | Situation | Phrases |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Waiting for a gap** | normal | *(wait for a TRP, then:)* *Sorry, can I add something? / If I could just add…* |
| **2. Small interjection** | clarification | *Sorry, just to check, did you say Tuesday? / Can I just ask a quick question?* |
| **3. Gentle interruption** | something to add | *Sorry to interrupt, but… / Can I jump in here? / Sorry, may I make a quick point?* |
| **4. Firm but polite** | time/urgent | *Sorry, I'm going to have to stop you there because we're short on time. / If I can just stop you there for a moment…* |
| **5. Urgent** | safety/error | *Wait, sorry, we have a problem. / Hold on, that figure isn't right.* |

Useful frames:
- ***Sorry to interrupt, but…*** (most common)
- ***Can I just jump in here?*** (informal, US/UK)
- ***Can I just add something?***
- ***If I could just come in here…***
- ***Sorry, just a quick point…***
- ***Hang on a second… / Hold on a moment…*** (informal; stronger)
- ***Before you go on, …***
- ***Sorry, can I stop you there?***
- ***Just to build on that…*** (supportive interruption)
- ***May I? / If I may…*** (formal)

**Add a reason or promise brevity:** *Sorry to interrupt, but I have to leave at four, so I wanted to say something quickly.* This shows respect.

**After interrupting:** return the floor if appropriate: *Sorry, go on. / Sorry, please continue.*

### 2.5 Supportive vs. hostile overlap
Not all overlap is rude.

| Type | Example | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| **Backchannel overlap** | *Mm-hm. Right. Yeah.* during a speaker's turn | support (good) |
| **Collaborative completion** | A: *We went to the…* B: *…beach, yeah!* | closeness (friends); at work can feel presumptuous |
| **Enthusiastic overlap** | *Oh my God, me too!* | warmth (informal) |
| **Hostile interruption** | cutting someone off to disagree: *No, no, that's wrong…* | aggressive |
| **Topic hijack** | interrupting to switch the subject | rude or selfish |
| **Talking over** | continuing to speak while the other does | domination |

In English-speaking professional settings, **supportive overlaps** (*yeah, right*) are welcome; **content overlaps** (long competing speech) tend to be read as rude.

### 2.6 When you're interrupted: how to respond
| Goal | Phrases |
|---|---|
| **Hold the floor politely** | *Just a second, let me finish this point. / If I could just finish…* |
| **Hold the floor firmly** | *Sorry, may I finish? / Please let me finish. / Hold on, I'm almost done.* |
| **Promise to return** | *I'll come to that in a moment. / Good point, let me just finish and I'll come back to it.* |
| **Yield gracefully** | *Sure, go ahead. / Of course. / Please, go on.* |
| **Resume** | *As I was saying… / To get back to what I was saying… / Where was I?* |
| **Defuse** | *No problem. Let's hear your point first.* |
| **Name the pattern kindly (to a repeat interrupter)** | *I notice I haven't been able to finish a thought. Could I finish and then hear your view?* |

**Intonation matters:** *Sorry, may I finish?* with a calm, low-pitched voice and a smile is firm; with high pitch and a sharp tone, it's an attack.

### 2.7 Keeping the floor without being rude: "headlining" and numbering
Because English speakers dislike being cut off, they **announce structure** to hold the floor:
- *I have three quick points. First…, second…, and third…*
- *There are two reasons for that. One is…, and the other is…*
- *Let me explain in a few steps.*
- *Just to give a bit of background first…*
- *The short answer is yes. The longer answer is…*

When you announce structure, listeners accept longer turns because they know when you will finish.

**Avoid rambling:** use *To cut a long story short, … / The bottom line is… / In a nutshell…* and then stop. Hand over: *That's it from me. What do you think?*

### 2.8 Dominating vs. sharing the floor
Signs you might be dominating:
- you speak much longer than others;
- others say less and less;
- you don't ask questions;
- you ignore signals (looks away, short answers).

Strategies:
- *Sorry, I've been talking a lot. What about you?*
- *I'd love to hear your thoughts.*
- *Does anyone else have something to add?*
- Aim for **turns of 20–40 seconds** in normal conversation; in meetings, aim for one clear point per turn.

If you tend to be **quiet** (maybe worried about English), strategies:
- Signal early: *I have a point on that.* (put your hand up, lean forward)
- Prepare one question and one comment before meetings.
- Use "building" phrases: *Just to build on what Ana said…*
- Remember: a short, simple contribution is better than a perfect one that never arrives.

### 2.9 Chairing and facilitating (running turns for others)
Useful for meetings, classes, and presentations:
- *Let's hear from Luis first. / Ana, you had your hand up?*
- *One at a time, please.*
- *Thanks, Sam. I'll come back to you in a moment.*
- *Let's keep our comments brief so everyone has a chance.*
- *We're running short on time, so let's take two more comments.*
- *Can we park that for now? / Let's take that offline.*
- *To keep us on track, …*
- *Thanks, everyone. That's a good place to stop.*
- Online: *Please use the raise-hand function. / You're on mute. / Could you unmute? / I think we lost you for a second.*

### 2.10 Signaling you're listening (active listening)
**A. Verbal backchannels (continuers)** (Lesson 60 recap)
*mm-hm, uh-huh, yeah, right, okay, I see, sure, got it, interesting, really?, wow, oh no, no way*

**B. Non-verbal**
- eye contact (comfortable, not staring);
- nodding;
- leaning slightly toward the speaker;
- open posture;
- facial expressions matching content;
- no phone checking.

**C. Paraphrasing / reflecting back**
- *So what you're saying is…*
- *If I understand correctly, …*
- *Let me make sure I've got this right: …*
- *It sounds like…*
- *In other words, …*
- *So you mean…*

**D. Asking follow-up questions**
- *And then what happened? / How did that go? / What did you do next? / What was that like? / Tell me more. / Can you give me an example?*

**E. Acknowledging feelings**
- *That sounds frustrating. / I can imagine. / That must have been tough. / I'm so sorry to hear that. / That's great news!*

**F. Encouraging continuation**
- *Go on. / Please, continue. / I'm listening. / Take your time.*

**G. Summarizing**
- *So to sum up, …* / *Let me summarize.*

**H. Remote communication**
- On the phone, your face is invisible: increase verbal signals.
- On video, mute when not speaking, nod visibly, and use chat for brief supportive comments.
- Delays cause overlap: use *You go ahead. / Sorry, you first. / After you.*

### 2.11 Pauses and silence
- A pause of 1–2 seconds is normal; a longer silence may be read as hesitation, disagreement, or discomfort.
- To fill a silence after a question: *Good question. Let me think for a second… / Hmm, let me see…* (Lesson 60).
- It is acceptable to **take a moment** in important or emotional conversations: *Give me a second.*
- After you speak, **tolerate a short silence** while the other person thinks; don't fill it with a second question immediately (a common habit that can feel like pressure).
- Silence is also used for emphasis in presentations: *pause before the key figure.*

### 2.12 Meetings and calls: practical turn-taking
**Before**
- Prepare 1–2 points and one question.
- Know the agenda.
- Arrive early; small talk helps you start in the flow.

**During**
- Signal early: raise a hand or say *Can I come in on that?*
- Be brief: **point + reason + proposal** in 20–40 seconds.
- Refer to others: *Building on Ana's point…*
- Check understanding: *Does that make sense?*
- Don't repeat what has been said; add something new or agree briefly: *I agree with Luis.*
- If you didn't understand: *Sorry, could you repeat that? / Could you clarify what you mean by X?* (Never pretend.)

**After**
- Follow up in writing: *Thanks for the discussion. To recap…*

**Online meetings**
| Situation | Phrase |
|---|---|
| Want to speak | *May I come in? / I'd like to add something. / (use raise hand)* |
| Speak at the same time | *Sorry, you go ahead. / No, please, after you.* |
| Connection issue | *You're breaking up. / Sorry, you froze. / Can you hear me? / You're on mute.* |
| Share screen | *Can everyone see my screen? / Let me share my screen.* |
| End | *Thanks, everyone. I'll drop off now.* |

### 2.13 Politeness formulas (summary)
- **Start:** *Sorry, …, Excuse me, …, Can I …?*
- **Reason:** *… I have a quick question. / … I have to leave soon.*
- **Content:** short.
- **Return:** *Sorry, go on.*

## 3. Why Native Speakers Do This
1. **Face and autonomy.** Being cut off threatens a speaker's sense of respect and control. Politeness formulas reduce the threat.
2. **Efficiency.** Clear turn signals prevent chaos in groups, meetings, and phone calls.
3. **Equality.** In many English-speaking workplaces, every participant is expected to have a chance to speak; hogging the floor is frowned upon.
4. **Cognitive load.** Overlapping speech is hard to process; clean turns allow clarity.
5. **Cultural norms about conflict.** Overlap that feels like warmth in one culture can feel like competition in another.
6. **Clarity in a second language.** For non-native speakers (including English-speaking people from other countries), clear turn management helps comprehension.
7. **Listening as relationship-building.** Backchannels, paraphrases, and follow-up questions show care and build trust.

It's also worth remembering: **English-speaking cultures differ**. New Yorkers, for example, often have a faster, more overlapping style than people in the UK Midlands or the Midwest US. Read your room.

## 4. When to Use / When Not To

**Interrupt when**
- you need a clarification (otherwise the rest will be lost);
- there is a safety, ethical, or urgent matter;
- a clear error will cause problems;
- time is running out and you chair the meeting;
- you are supporting the speaker (*Exactly!*).

**Don't interrupt when**
- the speaker is telling an emotional story;
- they're mid-sentence and you simply want to disagree (wait, then use the ramp from Lesson 61);
- you're the junior person and the interruption is not essential;
- you're about to complete their sentence (unless you know them well, in friendly settings);
- you're about to change the topic abruptly.

**Hold the floor when**
- you're explaining something with steps;
- you've been interrupted early and the point is important.
Do it politely: *Just a second, let me finish.*

**Yield the floor when**
- you've made your point;
- others are waiting;
- you've spoken longer than 30–60 seconds;
- someone signals they want to speak.

**Avoid**
- *Wait, wait, wait…* (commanding);
- *No, no, no…* (dismissive);
- *Listen!* (command);
- talking over someone repeatedly;
- fake listening (nodding while on your phone).

## 5. Frequency & Register

**Priority order**
1. *Sorry, can I just add something? / Sorry to interrupt, but…*
2. *Mm-hm. / Right. / Really? / I see. / Go on.*
3. *What do you think? / Over to you.*
4. *Just a second, let me finish.*
5. *So what you're saying is… / If I understand correctly…*
6. *Sorry, could you say that again?*
7. *Can I come in here? / May I?* (formal)
8. *As I was saying… / Where was I?*

**Register**
| Level | Interrupting | Holding |
|---|---|---|
| **Informal** | *Hang on! / Wait, wait. / Sorry, quick thing—* | *Let me finish. / Hold on, I'm almost done.* |
| **Neutral** | *Sorry to interrupt, but… / Can I just add…?* | *Just a second, let me finish. / If I could just finish…* |
| **Formal** | *If I may… / May I interject? / Excuse me, I'd like to add…* | *Allow me to finish. / If you'll permit me…* |
| **Chairing** | *Thank you. I'll have to stop you there.* | *Let's hear everyone's view, please.* |

## 6. American vs. British Notes

| Feature | US | UK |
|---|---|---|
| Interrupting | more direct but friendly: *Can I jump in?* | more apologetic: *Sorry to interrupt, but… / If I could just stop you there…* |
| Style in meetings | fast, assertive, enthusiastic | more reserved, indirect, more *sorry* |
| Overlap tolerance | varies (New York high, Midwest low) | generally low in professional contexts |
| Holding the floor | *Let me finish. Hear me out.* | *If I could just finish… / Do let me finish.* |
| Agreement signals | *Exactly! Totally! Right!* | *Quite. Indeed. Absolutely.* |
| Inviting others | *What do you think? What's your take?* | *Do you have any thoughts? Would you like to add anything?* |
| Thanks for the comment | *Great point.* | *That's a good point. Thank you.* |
| Mild interruption | *Quick question…* | *Sorry, just a quick question…* |
| Waiting | *Go ahead.* | *After you. Please, go on.* |
| Take it later | *Let's circle back / Let's table that.* | *Let's park that. Let's come back to that.* |
| *Table* a topic | US: postpone (**table**) | UK: **table** a motion = bring it forward for discussion (opposite!) |
| "Brief" | *quick thought* | *a quick word* |

**Important false pair:** *Let's table this* means "postpone" in the US and "put forward for discussion" in the UK. In international meetings, say *Let's postpone this* or *Let's discuss it now* to avoid confusion.

**Practical tip:** with unknown listeners, use the neutral British-style apology (*Sorry to interrupt, but…*): it works everywhere.

## 7. Common Mistakes Made by Spanish Speakers

### 7.1 Interrupting too abruptly
| ❌ Error | ✅ More natural |
|---|---|
| *Wait, wait, wait.* | *Sorry, can I just add something?* |
| *No, no, no, listen.* | *Sorry, may I make a quick point?* |
| *Yes, but… (immediately overlapping)* | *Sorry to interrupt, but…* |
| *I want to say something.* | *Can I just say something?* |
| *Let me tell you something.* | *If I could just add something…* |
| *Pay attention.* | *Just to be sure we're on the same page…* |
| *You're wrong, stop.* | *Sorry, can I stop you there? I think there's a mistake.* |
| *Hey!* | *Sorry, excuse me.* |
| *Wait a moment.* (command) | *Just a moment, please. / Hang on a second.* |
| *I interrupt you.* | *Sorry to interrupt.* |
| *Excuse me, excuse me, excuse me!* (repeated) | *Excuse me. (pause) Sorry, may I…?* |
| *Let me speak.* | *Sorry, could I finish?* |
| *Don't interrupt me.* (accusing) | *Sorry, may I finish my point? Then I'd love to hear yours.* |

### 7.2 Finishing others' sentences and overlapping
- In Spanish, finishing the other's sentence shows closeness. In English at work, it can sound as if you think you know better.
- If you do it, keep it light and friendly with people you know well.
- Leave a **short gap** (about a second) after someone finishes before you start: this is longer than your Spanish habit.
- Don't start your answer while the other person is still finishing their last word; wait for the falling pitch.

### 7.3 Staying silent
- Waiting to be invited can mean you never speak in a fast meeting.
- Not giving **any** backchannel makes you seem uninterested.
- Giving only *yes, yes, yes* (rapid) can sound impatient or dismissive.
- Silence after a question may be read as refusal or confusion; use *Let me think…*

### 7.4 Language errors in the topic
| ❌ Error | ✅ Correct |
|---|---|
| *I'm listening you.* | *I'm listening to you.* |
| *I don't hear you.* (audio issue) | *I can't hear you.* |
| *Say me.* | *Tell me.* |
| *Explain me.* | *Explain it to me.* |
| *Continue.* | *Go on. / Please continue.* |
| *Follow, please.* | *Go ahead. / Go on.* |
| *You understand me?* | *Does that make sense? / Do you follow?* |
| *Understood?* (sounds like a command) | *Is that clear? / Does that make sense?* |
| *Listen to me.* (command) | *Hear me out. / Let me explain.* |
| *Pay attention to me.* | *Can I have your attention for a moment?* |
| *Can you repeat me?* | *Can you repeat that?* |
| *Another time please.* | *Could you say that again, please?* |
| *What did you say?* (blunt) | *Sorry, what was that? / Sorry? / Sorry, could you say that again?* |
| *I didn't understand nothing.* | *I didn't understand anything.* |
| *Tell it slowly.* | *Could you speak a bit slower?* |
| *The word is...?* | *What's the word for…?* |
| *It's my turn to talk.* | *Could I have a turn?* |
| *I have the word.* | *I have the floor.* (formal) |
| *I ask the word.* (pedir la palabra) | *I'd like to speak. / May I say something?* |
| *Give me the word.* (dar la palabra) | *Could I have the floor? / May I speak?* |

### 7.5 Cultural misreads
- **Interpreting a pause as a signal to jump in.** In English professional talk, a pause may be thinking; give it a moment.
- **Believing that quick overlap = friendliness.** It may be read as competition.
- **Believing that an English speaker who says "Sorry, can I just…" lacks confidence.** It's a courtesy, not weakness.
- **Reading rapid-fire "mm-hm" as agreement.** It means "I follow," not "I agree."
- **Expecting an invitation to speak.** In many English meetings, you're expected to claim your turn; the chair may not call on you.
- **Assuming silence is consent.** If someone doesn't speak, it doesn't mean they agree. Ask: *Do you have any thoughts?*
- **Finishing the speaker's thought to help them find a word.** Better: wait, then offer: *Do you mean…?*
- **Interrupting to translate or explain for others.** Offer politely: *Can I help clarify?*

## 8. Common Misconceptions
- ❌ **"Interrupting is always rude."** — Polite, brief, well-timed interruption is normal and sometimes necessary.
- ❌ **"Overlap means the speakers are fighting."** — Supportive overlap (*Yeah! Exactly!*) is warm and normal.
- ❌ **"Quiet people have nothing to say."** — They may be waiting for a gap. Invite them.
- ❌ **"Good speakers never pause."** — They pause often. Pauses help listeners process.
- ❌ **"English speakers are cold because they don't interrupt."** — They express warmth differently: through backchannels, follow-up questions, and humor.
- ❌ **"If I say *Sorry*, I'm admitting guilt."** — In English, *Sorry* is often a courtesy marker (*Sorry, can I…?*).
- ❌ **"I must wait for a perfect sentence before speaking."** — A short, simple contribution is better than silence.
- ❌ **"*Listen to me* is a polite way to ask for attention."** — It's a command. Use *Can I explain? / Hear me out.*
- ❌ **"People who talk more are more competent."** — Not necessarily; clarity and relevance matter.
- ❌ **"In a video call, I don't need to show I'm listening."** — You need to do it more explicitly.
- ❌ **"Active listening is just staying quiet."** — It involves signals, questions, and summaries.
- ❌ **"Chairing a meeting means controlling it."** — It means enabling fair turns.

## 9. Pronunciation Note

### 9.1 Intonation is the turn signal
| Pattern | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Falling pitch** at the end (*…and that's why we left.* ↘) | I'm done; your turn |
| **Rising pitch** at the end of a yes/no question (*Do you agree?* ↗) | I'm waiting for your answer |
| **Level or slightly rising pitch** mid-sentence (*We went to Spain, → and then France, → and then…*) | I'm continuing |
| **List intonation** (rise, rise, fall: *apples ↗, bananas ↗, and oranges ↘*) | the list continues until the fall |
| **Drawn-out filler** (*sooo…, and…, uhmm…*) | holding the floor |
| **Fast speech with no pauses** | holding the floor, perhaps anxious or excited |
| **Sudden drop in volume and speed** | winding down |
| **Fall-rise** (*I suppose so…* ↘↗) | uncertain, may add more |

If you want to hold the floor, **avoid a falling pitch** at the end of a clause (it signals completion). Use level pitch and continue. If you want to yield, finish with a clear fall and look at the next speaker.

### 9.3 Softening with tone
- *Sorry to interrupt, but…* should have a **higher pitch** at *Sorry*, lower on *interrupt*, and a smile.
- *Hold on, let me finish* said **slowly** with a **falling, calm tone** sounds firm and polite. Said fast with a high pitch, it sounds irritated.
- Speak a bit **slower** when you interrupt; fast speech sounds rushed or aggressive.
- Use **lower volume** rather than louder to enter; loud entry grabs attention but invites conflict.

### 9.4 Spanish-speaker trouble spots
- **/ɪ/ in *interrupt, listen, think, in*:** short, relaxed vowel; not Spanish /i/.
- **/ʌ/ in *jump, just, hang (æ), but, huh*:** neutral central vowel.
- **Final consonants:** *interrupt /pt/, jump /mp/, point /nt/, finish /ʃ/.*
- **Stress in long words:** *inter-RUPT, con-ver-SA-tion, a-TEN-tion.*
- **Silent t in *listen, often (optional)*:** /ˈlɪsən/.
- **/h/ in *hold, hang, huh, hear, how*:** breath, not /x/.
- **Intonation transfer.** Spanish speakers often use a falling pitch at the end of every phrase, so listeners think they have finished and start speaking. Practice level pitch to hold the floor.
- **Speed.** Spanish rhythm is syllable-timed; English is stress-timed. Do not rush; allow content words to carry stress.

## 10. Memory Technique

### 10.1 The S-W-E-Y routine
**S**ignal → **W**ait → **E**nter → **Y**ield.
1. **Signal:** lean in, inbreath, raise a finger, say *Sorry…*
2. **Wait:** for a pause or falling pitch.
3. **Enter:** with a softener and short point: *Sorry, can I add something?*
4. **Yield:** *That's all from me. What do you think?*

### 10.2 The traffic-light check
Before speaking, quickly ask: *Is the light green, amber, or red?*
- Green: falling pitch + pause.
- Amber: filler or level pitch.
- Red: speaker is speeding up or says *let me finish.*

### 10.3 The 3-second rule
After the other person finishes, count one (in your head) before starting. This simple pause helps avoid overlapping, and it also makes you sound thoughtful.

### 10.4 One point per turn
Plan to make **one clear point** per turn. If you have three, number them: *I have three quick points.* This protects you from rambling.

### 10.5 Active listening checklist
During a conversation, do at least one of these every 20–30 seconds: backchannel, paraphrase, follow-up question, emotional acknowledgement. Count them after a conversation to build awareness.

### 10.6 Observe and mark transcripts
Watch a panel discussion or a meeting scene. Mark: where turns change, how speakers hold the floor, what interrupting phrases appear, and how listeners respond. Collect 5 phrases.

### 10.7 Role-play with signals
With a partner, practice a 3-minute discussion where each person:
1. must wait for a falling pitch before speaking;
2. must use a verbal backchannel at least every 20 seconds;
3. must paraphrase once.

### 10.8 Spaced retrieval cards
- Front: *Politely interrupt* → **Sorry to interrupt, but… / Can I just add something?**
- Front: *Keep the floor* → **Just a second, let me finish.**
- Front: *Return the floor* → **Sorry, go on.**
- Front: *Show you're listening* → **Mm-hm. / I see. / So what you're saying is…**
- Front: *Invite a quiet person* → **What do you think? / Would you like to add anything?**
- Front: *Park a topic* → **Let's come back to that later.**
Review on days 1, 3, 7, 14, 30.

## 11. Patterns (Not Isolated Rules)

> **Pattern 1: Interrupt = apology + purpose + brevity.** *Sorry to interrupt, but I have a quick question.*

> **Pattern 2: Claim a turn = softener + verb of adding.** *Can I just add / say / come in?*

> **Pattern 3: Hold the floor = acknowledge + promise + continue.** *Good point, I'll come to that. Let me just finish this.*

> **Pattern 4: Yield = finish + invite.** *That's my view. What do you think?*

> **Pattern 5: Number your points.** *Two things: first…, second…*

> **Pattern 6: Return the floor after interrupting.** *Sorry, please go on.*

> **Pattern 7: Paraphrase = So + you + verb + summary.** *So you're saying the launch should move to May.*

> **Pattern 8: Clarify = Could you + verb + noun?** *Could you clarify what you mean by "phase two"?*

> **Pattern 9: Show emotion = That sounds + adjective.** *That sounds exhausting. / That sounds amazing.*

> **Pattern 10: Backchannel timing.** At the end of idea units, not in the middle of words; vary tokens.

> **Pattern 11: Build rather than repeat.** *Just to build on Ana's point, …*

> **Pattern 12: Resume = as I was saying + point.** *As I was saying, the budget is tight.*

> **Connections:** Lesson 59 (softeners: *just, could, would you mind*), Lesson 60 (backchannels, *anyway, well, so* for shifting), Lesson 61 (disagreeing after waiting for your turn), Lesson 62 (opening and closing conversations), Phase 7 (intonation drives turn signals), Phase 8 (Listening and Speaking).

## 12. Real-Life Examples (Progressively Harder)

**Level 1 — Basic**
- *Sorry, can I ask something?*
- *Go ahead.*
- *Mm-hm.*
- *What do you think?*
- *Sorry, could you repeat that?*

**Level 2 — Everyday**
- *Sorry to interrupt, but what time does it start?*
- *Just a second, let me finish.*
- *Go on, I'm listening.*
- *After you!*
- *Sorry, you were saying?*

**Level 3 — Intermediate**
- *Can I just add something to that?*
- *Sorry to cut you off, but we're running out of time.*
- *So what you're saying is that we need more time?*
- *If I could just finish this point, then I'd love to hear your view.*
- *That's a good point. I'll come back to it in a moment.*

**Level 4 — Advanced**
- *If I may jump in here, I think we're mixing up two separate issues.*
- *Sorry, let me make sure I've understood. You're proposing that we pilot it in two cities first?*
- *I don't want to interrupt, but we have only five minutes left and haven't covered item four.*
- *Building on what Luis said, I'd add that cost is only part of the picture.*
- *Can I stop you there for a second? I want to make sure everyone's following.*

**Level 5 — Native-like**
- "Sorry, I'm going to have to stop you there. We've got a hard stop at four, and I want to hear from Ana."
- "Hang on, let me just get this out and then it's all yours."
- "No, no, go ahead. I've been talking forever."
- "I hear you, and I don't want to cut you off, but I think we're going round in circles."
- "That's helpful, thanks. Just so I'm clear, are you saying that the delay is on our side or the supplier's?"

### 12.1 Mini-dialogues

**1. Polite interruption in a meeting**
> **Ana:** …and then we move to phase two, which will take about eight weeks, and after that we…
> **Camila:** Sorry to interrupt, Ana. Could I ask a quick question about phase two?
> **Ana:** Of course.
> **Camila:** Is that eight weeks including testing?
> **Ana:** Good question. Yes, it includes testing. Anyway, as I was saying…

**2. Holding the floor**
> **Luis:** I don't think that makes sense because…
> **Camila:** Sorry, Luis, may I just finish this point? Then I'd really like to hear your view.
> **Luis:** Sure, go ahead.
> **Camila:** Thanks. So the main issue is the deadline. Then I'm happy to take questions.

**3. Joining a conversation**
> **Camila:** Hi, sorry to jump in. I couldn't help hearing you talk about Medellín. I'm from there!
> **Sam:** Oh, really? We were just saying how much we loved it.
> **Camila:** It's a great city. Have you been to the Comuna 13 tour?

**4. Showing you're listening**
> **A:** I had a terrible week. My project was canceled, and then my laptop broke.
> **B:** Oh no. That sounds awful.
> **A:** Yeah, and on top of that I got a cold.
> **B:** So it's been one thing after another. What are you going to do about the project?

**5. Chairing a meeting**
> **Chair:** Thanks, Sam. Let's hear from Ana next. Ana?
> **Ana:** Thanks. I'd like to add that…
> **Chair:** Great point. We have five minutes left, so let's take two more comments.

**6. Video call overlap**
> **A:** Sorry, you go ahead.
> **B:** No, no, after you.
> **A:** Okay, thanks. I was just going to say that the demo is ready.
> **B:** Great news!

**7. Being interrupted**
> **A:** That's wrong, the figure is…
> **B:** Just a second, let me finish. I'll come to the numbers in a moment.
> **A:** Sorry, go on.

**8. Paraphrasing**
> **A:** We can't ship before the 15th because the supplier is late, and we haven't finalized the packaging.
> **B:** So there are two blockers: the supplier and the packaging. Is that right?
> **A:** Exactly.

### 12.2 Email/chat equivalents
- *Sorry to jump in on this thread, but I wanted to add one thing…*
- *Quick follow-up on Ana's point…*
- *Just to confirm I understood, you want us to move the launch to May?*
- *Thanks for letting me finish. Happy to discuss further.*

### 12.3 Before and after
> ❌ *"Wait, wait, wait. No, no, no. That is wrong. Listen to me. I am talking."*
> ✅ *"Sorry to interrupt, but I think there might be a mistake in that figure. Could I check something?"*

> ❌ *(Silence for ten minutes in a meeting, then nodding "yes, yes, yes.")*
> ✅ *Mm-hm. Right. / Sorry, can I add something? / So you're saying… Is that right?*

> ❌ *"Understand? You understand me?"*
> ✅ *"Does that make sense?"*

**Contrast groups**
| Items | Difference |
|---|---|
| *interrupt / butt in / cut off / talk over / chime in / jump in* | *interrupt* neutral; *butt in* rude; *cut off* stops someone abruptly; *talk over* speak at the same time; *chime in / jump in* add (often welcome) |
| *listen / hear / pay attention / overhear* | *hear* = perceive sound; *listen* = deliberate; *pay attention* = focus; *overhear* = hear by accident |
| *say / tell / speak / talk* | *say* words; *tell* someone something; *speak* language/formal; *talk* conversation |
| *ask / question / inquire* | *ask* general; *question* challenge; *inquire* formal |
| *clarify / explain / elaborate / rephrase* | clear confusion / make understandable / add details / say differently |
| *paraphrase / summarize / recap* | restate in other words / short version / quick review |
| *pause / silence / gap / lull* | brief stop / no sound / short break between turns / quiet period in conversation |
| *floor / turn / word* | *have the floor* = formal right to speak; *take a turn* = speak in order; *have a word* = a brief talk |
| *table (US) / table (UK)* | postpone / bring to discussion |

## 17. Speaking Practice

**Drill 1 — Interrupting ladder.** Practice five levels of interrupting phrases, from *Sorry, can I add something?* to *Sorry, I'm going to have to stop you there.* Vary the tone.

**Drill 2 — Hold and yield.** Speak for 30 seconds on a topic, using level pitch and fillers to hold the floor, then finish with a clear falling tone and *What do you think?*

**Drill 3 — Shadowing meetings.** Shadow 2 minutes from a recorded meeting or panel. Copy the interrupting phrases and the intonation of yielding.

**Drill 4 — Backchannel training.** Play a 3-minute story. Produce a backchannel every 10–15 seconds, varying them (mm-hm, really?, I see, wow).

**Drill 5 — Paraphrase.** A partner explains a short situation; you respond with *So what you're saying is… Is that right?* Do ten rounds.

**Drill 6 — The 3-second pause.** Practice leaving a short gap before responding. Record and compare with your usual timing.

**Drill 7 — Role-play (video call).** Practice overlapping and recovering: *Sorry, you go ahead. / No, after you. / You're on mute.*

**Drill 8 — Chairing.** Chair a 5-minute discussion with a partner or imaginary group, using all four chairing functions (give, limit, park, close).

**Drill 9 — Reverse roles.** Practice being the interrupted person: use *Just a second, let me finish,* then *Go ahead* with a calm tone.
