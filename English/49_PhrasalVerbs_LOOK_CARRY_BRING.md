# Lesson 49 — Phrasal Verbs with LOOK, CARRY and BRING
### Phase 4: Phrasal Verbs | Module 6 of 7

---

## 0. Where this fits
You now have the full toolkit from Lessons 44–48: the transparency spectrum, separability, three-part verbs, and "choose the meaning from the object." This lesson adds three verbs that organize a different part of life:

- **LOOK** = *perception and attention* (seeing, searching, caring, investigating, expecting)
- **CARRY** = *continuation and execution* (keep going, perform, transfer)
- **BRING** = *causation and introduction* (make something arrive, cause it, raise it, produce it)

Lesson 35 covered BRING and RUN as core verbs. Here they become the engine of professional English: *look into, carry out, bring about, bring up* appear in nearly every meeting, report, and interview. Lesson 50 will gather the business and academic vocabulary built from all the verbs of Phase 4.

---

## 1. Intuitive Explanation

**LOOK** = your eyes (or attention) pointed somewhere. The particle tells you what you do with that attention.
- look **at** → direct your eyes
- look **for** → search
- look **after** → take care of
- look **up** → search in a reference; improve
- look **into** → investigate
- look **forward to** → anticipate with pleasure
- look **out** → be careful
- look **down on / up to** → judge lower / admire

**CARRY** = you have a load and you keep moving with it.
- carry **on** → continue
- carry **out** → perform, execute
- carry **over** → transfer to the next period
- carry **off** → succeed in something difficult
- carry **through** → complete
- carry **forward** → move to next period (accounting)
- be carried **away** → get too excited

**BRING** = you move something toward the reference point (here, the listener, the topic).
- bring **up** → raise (a topic, a child)
- bring **about** → cause
- bring **in** → introduce, earn, attract
- bring **down** → lower, topple
- bring **back** → return, revive
- bring **out** → release, reveal
- bring **together** → unite
- bring **forward** → move earlier, propose
- bring **on** → cause (an illness, a change)
- bring **off** → achieve against odds

Picture an **eye**, a **bag**, and a **hand extending toward you**. The eye focuses, the bag keeps moving, the hand delivers.

## 2. The Linguistic Explanation

### 2.1 Families

| Verb | Family | Phrasal verbs |
|---|---|---|
| LOOK | **Direct attention** | look **at**, look **out**, look **over**, look **through**, look **around** |
| LOOK | **Search / investigate** | look **for**, look **up**, look **into**, look **after** (a missing thing, UK), look **back** |
| LOOK | **Care / attitude** | look **after**, look **down on**, look **up to**, look **forward to** |
| CARRY | **Continue** | carry **on**, carry **forward**, carry **over**, carry **through** |
| CARRY | **Perform** | carry **out**, carry **off** |
| CARRY | **Emotion** | be carried **away**, carry **on** (make a fuss) |
| BRING | **Raise / introduce** | bring **up**, bring **in**, bring **out**, bring **forward**, bring **to** |
| BRING | **Cause** | bring **about**, bring **on**, bring **down**, bring **together** |
| BRING | **Return / reverse** | bring **back**, bring **round** (UK: revive/persuade) |

### 2.2 LOOK — meanings and examples

- **look at** — direct your eyes or consider: *Look at this chart.* / *We need to look at all the options.*
- **look for** — search: *I'm looking for my keys.* / *She's looking for a job.*
- **look after** — take care of: *Can you look after the kids tonight?*
- **look up** — (1) search in a reference: *Look up the word in a dictionary.* (2) improve: *Things are looking up.* (3) visit (UK): *Look me up when you're in town.*
- **look into** — investigate: *The police are looking into the case.*
- **look forward to** (three-part, + noun / -ing): *I'm looking forward to meeting you.*
- **look out** (for) — be careful or watch for: *Look out! There's a car.* / *Look out for typos.*
- **look over** — check quickly: *Could you look over my draft?*
- **look through** — scan or examine in detail: *I looked through the files.*
- **look around** — explore: *We looked around the city.*
- **look back** (on) — remember: *Looking back, I would do it differently.*
- **look down on** (three-part) — consider inferior: *He looks down on people who didn't go to college.*
- **look up to** (three-part) — admire: *I really look up to my mentor.*
- **look like** — resemble: *She looks like her mother.* (Not phrasal but crucial; see Section 7.)
- **look out for** (three-part) — keep watch for / protect: *Look out for each other.*

### 2.3 CARRY — meanings and examples

- **carry on** — (1) continue: *Carry on with your work.* (2) make a fuss: *Stop carrying on about it.* (3) have an affair (carry on with): informal.
- **carry out** — perform: *We carried out a survey.* / *The plan was carried out successfully.*
- **carry over** — transfer: *You can carry over five vacation days.*
- **carry off** — succeed with difficulty: *She carried off the presentation brilliantly.*
- **carry through** — complete despite difficulty: *He carried through his promise.*
- **carry forward** — transfer to next period (accounting): *The loss was carried forward.*
- **be carried away** — get overexcited: *I got carried away and spent too much.*
- **carry weight** — have influence: *Her opinion carries a lot of weight.*

### 2.4 BRING — meanings and examples

- **bring up** — (1) raise a topic: *I'll bring it up at the meeting.* (2) raise a child: *She was brought up in Bogotá.* (3) vomit (informal).
- **bring about** — cause: *The reforms brought about major change.*
- **bring in** — (1) earn: *The shop brings in $5,000 a month.* (2) introduce a law or system: *They brought in new rules.* (3) hire: *We brought in a consultant.*
- **bring down** — lower or topple: *The scandal brought down the government.* / *Bring down the prices.*
- **bring back** — return or revive: *Bring back my book!* / *The song brought back memories.*
- **bring out** — release or reveal: *The company brought out a new model.* / *Stress brings out the worst in him.*
- **bring together** — unite: *The tragedy brought the community together.*
- **bring forward** — move earlier, propose: *The meeting was brought forward to Tuesday.*
- **bring on** — cause (often negative): *Stress brought on a headache.*
- **bring off** — achieve: *Nobody thought she could bring it off.*
- **bring round** (UK) — persuade or revive: *We brought him round to our point of view.*
- **bring to** — revive (*bring someone to*); less common.

### 2.5 Separability

| Phrasal verb | Type | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| look at | **Inseparable** | *look at it* |
| look for | **Inseparable** | *look for it* |
| look after | **Inseparable** | *look after them* |
| look into | **Inseparable** | *look into it* |
| look up (a word) | Separable | *look it up / look up the word* |
| look up (improve) | Intransitive | *Things are looking up.* |
| look over | Separable | *look it over* |
| look through | **Inseparable** | *look through them* |
| look forward to | **Inseparable** | *look forward to it* |
| look down on / up to | **Inseparable** | *look up to her* |
| carry on | Intransitive / inseparable | *carry on with it* |
| carry out | Separable | *carry it out* |
| carry over | Separable | *carry it over* |
| carry off | Separable | *carry it off* |
| be carried away | Passive only | |
| bring up | Separable | *bring it up* |
| bring about | Separable | *bring it about* |
| bring in | Separable | *bring it in* |
| bring down | Separable | *bring them down* |
| bring back | Separable | *bring it back* |
| bring out | Separable | *bring it out* |
| bring together | Separable | *bring them together* |

| bring on | Separable | *bring it on* |

**Rule of thumb:** LOOK verbs describe attention directed *at* things, so they are mostly **inseparable** (except *look up* and *look over*, where you "handle" a reference). CARRY and BRING move things, so they are mostly **separable**.

## 3. Why Native Speakers Use Them

| Phrasal (natural) | Formal alternative |
|---|---|
| look into | investigate, examine |
| look after | care for, tend |
| look up (a word) | consult |
| look forward to | anticipate |
| look down on | disdain, despise |
| look up to | admire, respect |
| look over | review, inspect |
| carry out | conduct, perform, implement |
| carry on | continue, proceed |
| carry over | transfer |
| carry off | accomplish |
| bring up | raise, mention |
| bring about | cause, effect |
| bring in | introduce, earn |
| bring down | reduce, topple |
| bring out | release, reveal |
| bring together | unite, assemble |
| bring forward | advance |

Notice that in professional writing, formal verbs like *investigate, conduct, implement, introduce, raise* are appropriate, and in speech the phrasal verbs are the default. A strong user knows **both** and switches by register. A formal report: *"The committee will investigate the claims."* A meeting: *"We'll look into it."*

## 4. When to Use / When Not To
- **Use** *look into* when promising to investigate (*I'll look into it*): it is polite, non-committal, and extremely common in customer service and business.
- **Use** *carry out* for studies, surveys, experiments, orders, plans, and threats: *carry out research*, *carry out instructions*.
- **Use** *bring up* for topics in conversation and for raising children (*brought up* = raised; *grew up* = became an adult).
- **Use** *bring about* for formal change and consequences (news, history, policy).
- **Don't** confuse *look at* with *see* or *watch*. *Look at* directs your eyes; *see* is the result; *watch* tracks movement over time.
- **Don't** use *carry* for "wear" or "bring along." You *bring* a document; you *carry* a heavy bag.
- **Don't** say *bring up* for "educate" in the academic sense. *Bring up* a child = raise; *educate* = teach.
- **Don't** use *bring* when the movement is away from the speaker; use *take* (see Lessons 30 and 35 for bring/take direction).

## 6. American vs. British Notes
- **bring forward** (move a meeting earlier): UK/business norm. US: *move up*. Careful: *push back* = delay in both varieties.
- **look in on** (visit briefly): UK and US both, but more UK.
- **look up** someone (visit): UK; US uses *look someone up* for finding contact info.
- **look after** (UK) vs. **take care of** (US more common; both understood).
- **bring round** (UK) vs. **bring around** (US): persuade or revive.
- **bring up** a child: both; **be brought up** (UK) vs. **be raised** (US).
- **carry on** is more British as an everyday "continue": *Carry on, please.* US prefers *go on* / *keep going*.
- **carry-on** (noun, US/UK): hand luggage on a plane: *carry-on bag*.
- **look out!** (both: warning). **Watch out!** is more common in US for warnings.
- **bring a plate** (Australia/UK) = bring food to share. Not US.

## 7. Common Mistakes Made by Spanish Speakers

1. **Look at / look for / look like confusion**
   - Spanish uses *mirar*, *buscar*, *parecer*.
   - ❌ *I look my keys.* / *I'm looking my keys.*
   - ✅ *I'm looking for my keys.*
   - ❌ *She looks her mother.* → ✅ *She looks like her mother.*
   - ❌ *Look this!* → ✅ *Look at this!*

2. **Leaving out *forward to* or using an infinitive after it**
   - ❌ *I look forward to see you.*
   - ✅ *I look forward to seeing you.*
   - *Why:* *to* is a preposition here, so the verb must be a gerund (-ing). Compare *used to* (Lesson 43).
   - ❌ *I'm looking forward to your answer to me.* (unnatural) → ✅ *I look forward to your reply.*

3. **Pronoun placement in separable verbs**
   - ❌ *Look up it.* / *Bring up it.* / *Carry out it.*
   - ✅ *Look it up.* / *Bring it up.* / *Carry it out.*

4. **Splitting inseparable verbs**
   - ❌ *Look the problem into.* / *Look the kids after.*
   - ✅ *Look into the problem.* / *Look after the kids.*

5. **Using *take care* for *look after***
   - ❌ *Who takes the baby?* (meaning looks after) → ✅ *Who is looking after the baby?*

6. **Confusing *bring* and *take***
   - ❌ *Bring this to your sister* (when moving away from the speaker) → ✅ *Take this to your sister.*
   - ✅ *Bring me a glass of water.* (toward the speaker)

7. **Using *carry* for *llevar* (to wear) or *traer***
   - ❌ *She carries a red dress.* → ✅ *She is wearing a red dress.*
   - ❌ *Carry me the book.* → ✅ *Bring me the book.*

8. **Raising vs. educating vs. bringing up**
   - ❌ *My parents educated me in Medellín.* (when meaning raised) → ✅ *I was brought up in Medellín.*
   - ❌ *She was grown up by her grandmother.* → ✅ *She was brought up by her grandmother.* / *She grew up with her grandmother.*

9. **Misusing *carry out* and *take out***
   - ❌ *We took out a study.* → ✅ *We carried out a study.*
   - ✅ *We took out a loan.* (obtained)

10. **Mispronouncing *bring* and *look***
    - ❌ /briŋ/ ("breeng") or /liŋk/ ("link")
    - ✅ *Bring* /brɪŋ/ with a short /ɪ/; *look* /lʊk/ with a short /ʊ/ like *put*, *good*.

11. **Literal translation of *ya verás***
    - ❌ *We'll see you after* → ✅ *We'll see.* / *You'll see.* (look is not used)

12. **Overusing *bring about* in speech**
    - ❌ *The rain brought about my headache.* (too formal) → ✅ *The rain gave me a headache.* / *brought on a headache.*

## 8. Common Misconceptions
- ❌ "*Look for* and *look at* are interchangeable." — *At* = direct your eyes. *For* = search. Different goals.
- ❌ "*Bring up* only means raise a topic." — It also means raise a child, and (informally) vomit.
- ❌ "*Carry out* is just a fancy *do*." — It implies a *plan, order, or procedure* being executed (*carry out a survey, carry out orders*), not casual actions.
- ❌ "*Look forward to* takes an infinitive because of *to*." — No: *to* is a preposition, so use a noun or gerund.
- ❌ "All phrasal verbs are separable." — Most LOOK verbs are inseparable.
- ❌ "*Bring* and *take* mean the same." — Direction: toward the speaker/listener (bring) vs. away (take).
- ❌ "*Look up to* and *look up* are the same." — The first is admiration (three-part, inseparable), the second is searching.

## 9. Pronunciation Note
1. **LOOK /lʊk/** — short /ʊ/ as in *book, good, put*. Spanish speakers often lengthen it to /luːk/ ("luke"). Keep it short.
2. **BRING /brɪŋ/** — short /ɪ/ followed by /ŋ/. Don't pronounce a final /g/. Same for *-ing* endings: *looking /ˈlʊkɪŋ/, bringing /ˈbrɪŋɪŋ/*. Practice the double -ng in *bringing*.
3. **CARRY /ˈkæri/** — the first vowel is /æ/ (as in *cat*), not /ɑ/ or /e/. Spanish speakers often say /ˈkari/ or /ˈkɛri/. Open the mouth wider and drop the jaw.
4. **Particle stress:** *look FOR, look AFTER, carry ON, bring UP*. The particle receives the stress. In three-part verbs: *look FORWARD to* (stress on FORWARD), *look UP to*, *look DOWN on*.
5. **Noun stress shift:** verb = particle stress; noun = first-syllable stress.

| Verb | Noun |
|---|---|
| to carry ON | a CARRYon (carry-on bag, also *carry-on* = fuss) |
| to look OUT | a LOOKout |
| to bring UP | upbringing /ˈʌpbrɪŋɪŋ/ (note spelling) |
| to look OVER | an OVERlook (rare) |

6. **Linking:** *look it up* → /lʊk ɪ ˈtʌp/ ("loo-ki-tup"). *Bring it up* → /brɪŋ ɪ ˈtʌp/ ("brin-gi-tup"): in fast speech the /ŋ/ links to the vowel with a light /g/ sound. *Carry on* → /ˈkæri ɒn/ with a glide /j/ between: "carry-yon".
7. **Reduction:** *looking forward to* often becomes /ˈlʊkɪŋ ˈfɔːrwərd tə/ with a weak *to*. In emails you write it in full; in speech it contracts.
8. **Minimal pairs for LOOK/LUCK/LAKE:** /lʊk/, /lʌk/, /leɪk/. Practice the three.

## 10. Memory Technique

### Three pictures
1. **LOOK = an eye with a beam.** The beam goes *at* something (at), *searches* (for), *protects* (after), *rises* (up), *goes inside* (into), *reaches ahead* (forward to).
2. **CARRY = a backpack on a road.** *On* = keep walking, *out* = unpack and do the work, *over* = hand the bag to the next day, *off* = reach the summit.
3. **BRING = a conveyor belt toward you.** *Up* = lift to the surface (a topic), *about* = cause the event, *in* = bring through the door (money, rules), *down* = lower, *back* = return, *out* = reveal.

### Two-question rule for LOOK
1. Am I **searching**? → *for*. 2. Am I **caring**? → *after*. 3. Am I **investigating**? → *into*. 4. Am I **admiring/despising**? → *up to / down on*. 5. Am I **anticipating**? → *forward to*.

### The "-ing after to" test for *look forward to*
Ask: *Can I put "the" or "it" after "to"?* If yes, *to* is a preposition. *I look forward to the meeting.* ✅ *I look forward to it.* ✅ Therefore *I look forward to meeting you.* (gerund). Use this test for *be used to, object to, committed to, in addition to, with a view to*.

### Business story chain (memorize as a story)
*"We **looked into** the problem, **carried out** a survey, **brought up** the results at the meeting, and the changes **brought about** a recovery; now we **look forward to** the next quarter."*

### Opposites cement meaning
| Verb | Opposite |
|---|---|
| bring up (a topic) | drop (a topic) |
| bring down | bring up / raise |
| look up to | look down on |
| carry on | give up / stop |
| bring in (earn) | take out (spend) |
| bring about | prevent / head off |
| bring forward | put back / push back |

## 11. Patterns (Not Isolated Rules)

> **Pattern 1:** **look forward to + noun / -ing.** *I look forward to hearing from you.* The standard closing of professional emails. Never an infinitive.

> **Pattern 2:** **look into + problem/complaint/case/matter.** *We'll look into it.* One of the most useful chunks in customer-facing English.

> **Pattern 3:** **carry out + study/survey/test/order/plan/task.** A collocation chain (see Section 13). If it is a procedure, you *carry it out*.

> **Pattern 4:** **bring up + topic/issue/point/subject** and **be brought up + place/by + person.** One verb, two patterns.

> **Pattern 5:** **bring about + change/result/collapse/improvement.** The object is usually abstract. If it's a concrete thing, use *bring*.

> **Pattern 6:** **look up to / look down on** are a matched pair of attitude verbs; use them for people, not things.

> **Pattern 7:** **get carried away + by/with** (excitement): *I got carried away with the decorations.* The passive-like *get* form is the usual one in speech.

> **Pattern 8:** **bring + object + to + noun/gerund** for result: *This brings me to my next point.* A discourse chunk that links ideas (Lesson 28).

> **Connection to Lesson 33 (come/go) and 35 (bring):** *bring* and *take* follow the same deictic logic as *come* and *go*: toward vs. away from the reference point.

> **Connection to Lesson 43 (dependent prepositions):** *look at / look for / look after* are dependent-preposition patterns; *look forward to + -ing* is the classic example of preposition + gerund.

> **Connection to Lesson 47 (COME/GO):** *come up with* (invent) vs. *bring up* (raise); *go through* (examine) vs. *look through* (scan); *go on* vs. *carry on* (both continue, but *carry on* is slightly more British and deliberate).

> **Connection to Lesson 48:** *turn out* (prove) vs. *bring about* (cause); *set up* (establish) vs. *bring in* (introduce).

## 12. Real-Life Examples (Progressively Harder)

**Level 1 — Basic**
- **Look at** this photo.
- I'm **looking for** my phone.
- Can you **look after** my dog?
- **Carry on**, please.
- **Bring** me a glass of water.

**Level 2 — Everyday**
- **Look up** the word in a dictionary.
- **Look out!** The floor is wet.
- I'll **bring up** the idea tomorrow.
- She was **brought up** in Cali.
- He **carried on** working despite the noise.

**Level 3 — Work and social**
- I'll **look into** the issue and get back to you.
- They **carried out** a survey of 500 customers.
- This **brings** me **to** my next point.
- The new product **brought in** $2 million.
- I **look forward to** hearing from you.

**Level 4 — Abstract**
- Technological change **brought about** a complete transformation of the industry.
- She **looks up to** her mentor and **looks down on** nobody.
- The company **brought out** a new model last spring.
- The scandal **brought down** the minister.
- The unused vacation days can be **carried over** to next year.

**Level 5 — Native-like**
- "He nearly **carried off** the deal, but a last-minute objection was **brought up**."
- "Looking back, the tension had been **brought on** by months of overwork."
- "I **got carried away** and promised more than I could **carry out**."
- "The crisis **brought** the team **together**, **bringing out** qualities nobody knew they had."
- "We'll **look into** your claim, but it will **carry** more **weight** if you can **bring in** the receipts."

**Fixed phrases**
- **look on the bright side** — be positive
- **look the other way** — ignore deliberately
- **look before you leap** — be careful
- **look sharp** — hurry
- **carry the can** (UK) — take the blame
- **carry weight** — have influence
- **carry a torch for** — love someone secretly
- **bring home the bacon** — earn money
- **bring to light** — reveal
- **bring to mind** — remind
- **bring up the rear** — come last
- **bring something to the table** — contribute
- **bring someone up to speed** — update them
- **bring it on!** — I'm ready for the challenge

## 13. Collocations & Related Expressions

**Collocations**
- look **at** options / data / the big picture
- look **for** a job / a solution / a way to
- look **after** children / the elderly / yourself
- look **into** a complaint / a claim / a possibility
- look **up** a word / a number / an address
- look **forward to** a meeting / a reply / seeing you
- carry **out** a study / an experiment / a survey / a plan / orders / a threat
- carry **on** a conversation / a tradition / a business / working
- carry **over** vacation days / a balance / a habit
- bring **up** an issue / a topic / a child / a point
- bring **about** change / a result / an end / reform
- bring **in** revenue / a law / an expert / a verdict
- bring **down** prices / a regime / a government / inflation
- bring **out** a book / a product / the best in someone
- bring **together** people / ideas / a team

**Contrast with similar verbs**
| Idea | Verb | Difference |
|---|---|---|
| Investigate | look into / investigate / examine | *look into* conversational; *investigate* formal; *examine* means closer inspection |
| Take care of | look after / take care of / care for | *look after* UK-leaning; *care for* can imply affection |
| Perform | carry out / do / conduct | *carry out* = formal plan; *conduct* = surveys, experiments |
| Raise | bring up / raise / mention | *bring up* conversational; *raise* neutral-formal; *mention* lightest |
| Cause | bring about / cause / lead to | *bring about* formal change; *cause* neutral; *lead to* gradual |
| Continue | carry on / go on / keep on / continue | *continue* formal; *carry on* British flavor; *keep on* persistence |
| Admire | look up to / admire / respect | *look up to* informal and natural |

**Mini Quiz**
1. *Look at* = direct your eyes or consider; *look for* = search; *look into* = investigate.
2. Because *to* is a preposition here, so it needs a noun or gerund: *I look forward to seeing you.*
3. Inseparable: *look into, look after.* Separable: *bring up, carry out, look it up* (*look up* with a reference).
4. Raise a topic (*bring up an issue*); raise a child (*be brought up*); informally, vomit.
5. *Bring* = toward the speaker or listener; *take* = away from them.
6. For plans, orders, studies, surveys, experiments, procedures, or threats: formal or planned actions.
7. *Look up to* = admire (three-part, inseparable); *look up* = search in a reference or improve.
8. Become overexcited and lose control; formed with *get/be carried away (by/with)*.
