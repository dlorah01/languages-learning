# Lesson 34 — PUT / KEEP / HOLD
### Phase 2: High-Frequency Verb System | Lesson 6 of 8

---

## 0. Where this fits
Continuing Phase 2's chunk-based verb-mastery method, this lesson groups three related but distinct verbs of placement/possession-over-time: PUT (placing something somewhere), KEEP (maintaining a state or possession over time), and HOLD (physically or figuratively grasping/containing). These three are frequently confused because Spanish's *poner*, *guardar/mantener*, and *sostener/sujetar/mantener* don't split the semantic territory in quite the same places.

---

## 1. Intuitive Explanation

- **PUT** = a single, momentary action of placing something in a location.
- **KEEP** = maintaining something (a state, a possession, a habit) continuously over time — the "ongoing" counterpart to PUT's single-moment action.
- **HOLD** = physically or figuratively grasping/supporting/containing something, often with an implication of active effort or restraint.

Think of PUT as pressing a button once, KEEP as holding a switch in the "on" position over time, and HOLD as physically gripping something in your hands (literally or metaphorically).

---

## 2. The Linguistic Explanation

### 2a. PUT — Single Action of Placement
- "Put the book on the table." (physical placement)
- **Fixed collocations:** put an end to (stop something), put pressure on (someone), put emphasis on, put a plan into action, put someone at ease, put something into words, put something off (postpone — previewed here, fully covered as a phrasal verb in Phase 4)

### 2b. KEEP — Maintaining/Continuing Over Time
- "Keep the change." (maintain possession)
- "Keep quiet." (maintain a state)
- "She keeps a diary." (maintains an ongoing habit/practice)
- **Fixed collocations:** keep a promise, keep a secret, keep in touch, keep an eye on (watch over), keep track of, keep calm, keep going, keep up with (previewed here, fully covered as a phrasal verb in Phase 4)
- **Keep + verb-ing (continuous repetition):** "She keeps interrupting me." (repeatedly, continuously — a genuinely distinct grammatical pattern worth flagging: "keep" + gerund signals repeated/continuous action, often with a slightly frustrated or persistent connotation)

### 2c. HOLD — Physical/Figurative Grasping, Supporting, Containing
- "Hold my hand." (physical grasp)
- "This bottle holds one liter." (contains — capacity meaning)
- "Hold your breath." (a specific, fixed physical action)
- **Fixed collocations:** hold a meeting/conversation (conduct), hold a grudge, hold your ground (stand firm), hold someone responsible, hold on (wait — previewed here, fully covered as a phrasal verb in Phase 4), hold a position/opinion (maintain a stance)

---

## 3. The Key Diagnostic: Momentary Action vs. Ongoing State vs. Active Grasp/Support
- **Single, momentary placement action** → PUT
- **Ongoing maintenance of a state/possession/habit over time, without necessarily implying physical effort** → KEEP
- **Active physical or figurative grasping, supporting, containing, or conducting** → HOLD

This diagnostic resolves most confusion cases, though — consistent with this entire phase's running theme — many specific collocations (keep a promise, hold a meeting) are ultimately fixed by convention and require direct memorization on top of the general pattern.

---

## 4. Why Native Speakers Maintain This Three-Way Split
Each verb captures a genuinely distinct temporal/physical quality: PUT's single-moment placement, KEEP's passive continuity over time, and HOLD's active grasping/support/conducting. This distinction lets English communicate precisely how something relates to time and effort — whether it's a one-time action, an ongoing state, or an active, effortful engagement — without needing extra clarifying words.

---

## 5. When Each Matters Most
- **PUT** — everyday physical placement instructions, plus a valuable set of abstract collocations (put emphasis on, put pressure on) common in professional/argumentative contexts.
- **KEEP** — extremely common for describing habits, ongoing states, and promises/commitments — a high-frequency, everyday verb.
- **HOLD** — common for physical actions, formal collocations (hold a meeting, hold a position), and emotional/figurative states (hold a grudge).

---

## 6. Frequency & Register
All three verbs and their core collocations are used across every register. "Hold a meeting"/"hold a conversation" specifically lean slightly more formal/professional compared to more casual alternatives ("have a meeting" is also common and slightly more casual-neutral) — both are correct, with "hold" carrying a marginally more formal/official connotation.

---

## 7. American vs. British Note
No major systematic differences in core meaning or collocations across American and British English for put/keep/hold.

---

## 8. Common Mistakes Made by Spanish Speakers

| Mistake | Why | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| "I need to keep the book on the table." (intending a single placement action) | Confusing "keep" (ongoing) with "put" (momentary action) | "I need to put the book on the table." (single placement — "keep" would imply an ongoing state of the book remaining there, a subtly different meaning) |
| "Put a promise" / "Do a promise" | Not knowing the fixed collocation "keep a promise" | "Keep a promise." (maintain/fulfill a commitment over time — not a single placement action) |
| "Hold a secret" (intending "maintain a secret over time") | Confusing "hold" (active grasp) with "keep" (ongoing maintenance) for this specific fixed collocation | "Keep a secret." (the correct fixed collocation, even though "hold" might seem logical) |
| "She keeps to interrupt me." (attempting the repeated-action pattern with an infinitive instead of a gerund) | Applying the wrong verb pattern after "keep" | "She keeps interrupting me." (keep + gerund, not keep + infinitive) |

---

## 9. Common Misconceptions
- ❌ "Put, keep, and hold are interchangeable whenever something involves placing or maintaining something." — Each has a genuinely distinct core meaning (momentary placement vs. ongoing maintenance vs. active grasp/support), and specific collocations are fixed by convention, not freely substitutable based on rough logical similarity.
- ❌ "Keep always implies physical possession." — "Keep" extends to abstract states and habits (keep calm, keep a promise, keep a diary) far beyond physical object possession.
- ❌ "Hold only refers to physical, literal grasping." — "Hold" extends to many abstract/figurative uses (hold a meeting, hold a grudge, hold a position/opinion) beyond literal physical grasping.

---

## 10. Pronunciation Note
"Keep" + gerund (keep interrupting, keep trying) is typically produced as a fluent chunk with stress on the gerund's main content, not on "keep" itself — worth practicing as a unit, especially since the frustrated/persistent connotation this pattern often carries is partly conveyed through intonation (a slightly emphatic, sometimes exasperated tone on the repeated action).

---

## 11. Memory Technique
**The "button vs. switch vs. grip" image** from Section 1: PUT = pressing a button once (momentary action). KEEP = holding a switch in position over time (ongoing state/maintenance, without necessarily implying physical effort). HOLD = physically gripping something in your hand (active physical or figurative grasp/support/conducting).

---

## 12. Patterns (Not Isolated Rules)

> **Pattern:** PUT = single momentary placement action. KEEP = ongoing maintenance of a state/possession/habit over time (also used with + gerund for repeated/continuous action). HOLD = active physical or figurative grasping, supporting, or conducting. When uncertain, ask: "Is this a one-time action, an ongoing state, or an active grasp/effort?"

---

## 13. Real-Life Examples (Progressively Harder)

**Level 1:**
- "Put your keys on the table." (single action)
- "Keep your keys in your pocket." (ongoing maintenance)
- "Hold my keys for a second." (active, temporary grasp)

**Level 2 — fixed collocations, correctly matched:**
- "I always keep my promises." (not "hold" or "put")
- "They're holding a meeting tomorrow." (not "keeping" or "putting")

**Level 3 — keep + gerund, the repeated-action pattern:**
- "He keeps forgetting his password." (repeated, mildly frustrating pattern — a genuinely distinct grammatical structure worth mastering)

**Level 4 — combining all three verbs correctly in one natural passage:**
- "She put the documents on my desk, asked me to keep them safe until the meeting, and told me to hold onto the originals in case anyone asked for them." (put = single placement, keep = ongoing maintenance/safekeeping, hold onto = active grasp/retention)

---

## 14. Related Expressions / Terms
- **Put/keep/hold as phrasal verb bases** — many of this lesson's collocations (put off, keep up with, hold on) will resurface as full phrasal verbs in Phase 4, where the particle (off, up, on) adds further meaning layers.
- **Keep + gerund** — the repeated/continuous action pattern, a genuinely distinct grammatical structure worth its own memorization slot.
