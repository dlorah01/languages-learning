# Lesson 39 — BY / FOR / TO
### Phase 3: Prepositions | Lesson 3 of 7

---

## 0. Where this fits
Continuing Phase 3's three-layer method (Lesson 37), this lesson covers three prepositions whose core images are genuinely distinct from IN/ON/AT: BY (proximity/agency/means), FOR (purpose/benefit/duration), and TO (direction/destination/recipient). These three generate some of the highest-frequency grammatical structures in English (passive voice's "by," infinitive purpose clauses' "to," and duration expressions' "for").

---

## 1. Intuitive Explanation

- **BY** = core image: **proximity/nearness**, extending to **the agent/means by which something happens** (standing "by" something extends to being the "doer" of an action, or the method used).
- **FOR** = core image: **directed toward a purpose/beneficiary**, extending to **duration** (a length of time "aimed at" or "allocated for" an activity).
- **TO** = core image: **movement toward a destination/endpoint**, extending to **the recipient of an action** and the **infinitive marker** (movement toward a not-yet-realized action).

---

## 2. The Linguistic Explanation

### 2a. BY — Proximity, Agent, Means
| Layer | Examples |
|---|---|
| Physical | "by the window" (near), "by the river" (near/alongside) |
| Time | "by 5 PM" (before/no later than — a deadline, extending "proximity" to "arriving near/at a time limit"), "by the time..." |
| Abstract | "written by Shakespeare" (passive agent — Lesson 12 callback), "by car/by bus/by email" (means/method), "by mistake/by chance" (manner) |

### 2b. FOR — Purpose, Benefit, Duration
| Layer | Examples |
|---|---|
| Physical (less core, but relevant) | "a gift for you" (directed toward a recipient/beneficiary) |
| Time (duration) | "for two hours," "for a week," "for a long time" (a length of time, contrasted directly with "since," which marks a starting point — Lesson 7 callback) |
| Abstract | "for example," "for sale," "for free," "reason for," "responsible for," "known for," "famous for" |

### 2c. TO — Direction, Destination, Recipient, Infinitive Marker
| Layer | Examples |
|---|---|
| Physical | "go to the store," "to the left/right" (movement toward a destination) |
| Time | "from 9 to 5" (marking an endpoint of a range), "ten to three" (British time-telling: 2:50) |
| Abstract | "give something to someone" (recipient), "to my surprise," "according to," and critically: the **infinitive marker** ("I want to go," "she decided to stay" — movement toward a not-yet-realized/future action, a genuinely elegant metaphorical extension of the core directional image) |

---

## 3. The Critical FOR vs. SINCE Contrast (Duration vs. Starting Point)
This directly resolves and expands on Lesson 7's original introduction of this pair:
- **FOR** + a length/duration of time: "I've lived here for five years." (how long)
- **SINCE** + a starting point in time: "I've lived here since 2019." (when it started)
Both typically pair with present perfect (Lesson 7-8), but they answer genuinely different questions (how long vs. since when), and Spanish speakers frequently confuse them because Spanish *desde* and *desde hace* don't split this territory in quite the same clean way.

---

## 4. The Infinitive "TO" — A Genuinely Elegant Metaphorical Extension
The use of "to" as the infinitive marker (to go, to eat, to understand) is a beautiful illustration of Lesson 37's entire method: "to" represents movement toward a destination, and the infinitive represents movement toward an **action that hasn't happened yet** — a not-yet-realized destination in time/possibility, rather than in physical space. This connects directly to why infinitives so often express purpose, intention, or a future-oriented goal: "I called to ask a question" (the calling was undertaken as movement toward the not-yet-realized goal of asking).

---

## 5. Why Native Speakers Maintain These Distinctions
BY, FOR, and TO each encode a genuinely distinct relationship — nearness/means, purpose/duration, and direction/destination — that native speakers rely on to communicate precisely how one element relates to another (who did it, how long, for whose benefit, toward what destination). Confusing these prepositions doesn't just sound slightly off; it can genuinely obscure who's responsible for an action, how long something lasted, or who benefits from something.

---

## 6. When Each Matters Most
- **BY (passive agent, means)** — essential for passive voice (Lesson 12 callback) and describing methods of transport/communication.
- **FOR (duration, purpose)** — essential for describing how long something lasted and for explaining reasons/purposes — extremely high-frequency.
- **TO (infinitive marker, recipient, destination)** — arguably the single most grammatically essential preposition in this lesson, given its role as the infinitive marker across an enormous share of English sentence structures.

---

## 7. Frequency & Register
All three prepositions are used constantly across every register — genuinely foundational, high-frequency grammar with no formality distinction in core usage.

---

## 8. American vs. British Note
Minor lexical differences exist (e.g., "twenty to three" for telling time is more traditionally British, while some American speakers prefer "two forty" more often in casual speech), but the core BY/FOR/TO system functions identically in both varieties.

---

## 9. Common Mistakes Made by Spanish Speakers

| Mistake | Why | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| "I've lived here since five years." | Confusing "since" (starting point) with "for" (duration) | "I've lived here for five years." |
| "This book was write by Shakespeare." (grammar error aside, focusing on preposition use) | N/A — preposition itself is correct here, included to show the passive-agent "by" pattern reinforced from Lesson 12 | (correct preposition; verb form needs fixing: "This book was written by Shakespeare.") |
| "I want for go to the party." (inserting "for" before an infinitive) | Confusing purpose "for" with the infinitive marker "to" | "I want to go to the party." (infinitive marker is "to," not "for + to") |
| "I called for ask a question." | Same confusion — using "for" instead of the infinitive "to" for purpose | "I called to ask a question." |
| "This gift is to you." (should be "for," not "to," in this specific context) | Confusing recipient "to" (give something TO someone) with beneficiary "for" (a gift FOR someone) | "This gift is for you." (a gift intended for someone's benefit uses "for"; direct transfer to a recipient, like "give it to her," uses "to" — a genuinely subtle distinction) |

---

## 10. Common Misconceptions
- ❌ "For and to are basically interchangeable when talking about people." — As Section 9's last example shows, "for" (benefit/purpose) and "to" (direct recipient/destination) encode different relationships, even when both seem to involve "directing something toward a person."
- ❌ "The infinitive marker 'to' is just a random grammatical particle with no real meaning." — As Section 4 shows, it's a genuine, meaningful metaphorical extension of "to"'s core directional image, not an arbitrary grammatical filler.
- ❌ "For and since are interchangeable time markers." — They answer fundamentally different questions (duration vs. starting point) and require different types of following expressions (a length of time vs. a specific point/date).

---

## 11. Pronunciation Note
"To" as the infinitive marker reduces heavily in fast speech, often to "tuh" (/tə/), while "to" as a stressed destination-preposition retains more of its full vowel sound in careful speech — this distinction can help you track which function "to" is serving in a given sentence when listening.

---

## 12. Memory Technique
**The "Nearby, Purposeful, Directional" image**: BY = standing near something (proximity, extending to agency/means). FOR = reaching out toward a purpose or person's benefit (extending to duration — time "reached toward" an activity). TO = walking toward a destination (extending to recipients and the not-yet-realized infinitive "destination").

**For FOR vs. SINCE specifically:** "FOR = how long (a length), SINCE = since when (a point)" — a simple, memorable contrast pair.

---

## 13. Patterns (Not Isolated Rules)

> **Pattern:** The infinitive marker "to" is not a separate, arbitrary grammatical rule — it's the same core directional image (movement toward a destination) applied metaphorically to actions not yet realized, directly connecting infinitive purpose clauses ("I called to ask") to "to"'s literal directional meaning ("I went to the store").

---

## 14. Real-Life Examples (Progressively Harder)

**Level 1:**
- "I'll finish this by Friday." (deadline — proximity to a time limit)
- "This is a present for you." (purpose/beneficiary)
- "I'm going to the gym." (destination)

**Level 2 — for vs. since, direct contrast:**
- "I've been studying English for six years." (duration — how long)
- "I've been studying English since 2018." (starting point — since when)

**Level 3 — the infinitive "to" as movement toward an unrealized action:**
- "She worked hard to achieve her goals." (working = movement toward the not-yet-realized destination of achieving goals)

**Level 4 — combining all three prepositions correctly in one sophisticated passage:**
- "The report, written by the research team, was submitted for review by Friday, and it was sent to the board to ensure everyone had time to prepare for the meeting." (by = agent; by = deadline; for = purpose; to = recipient; to = infinitive; for = purpose again — six distinct BY/FOR/TO uses in one natural sentence)
