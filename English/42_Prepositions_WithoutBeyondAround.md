# Lesson 42 — WITHOUT / BEYOND / AROUND / AGAINST / DESPITE / THROUGHOUT
### Phase 3: Prepositions | Lesson 6 of 7

---

## 0. Where this fits
This lesson rounds out the individual preposition study before Lesson 43 closes Phase 3 with dependent-preposition collocations (adjective/verb + preposition pairs). These six prepositions cover absence (without), extent past a limit (beyond), circular/approximate position (around), opposition/contact (against), concession (despite), and comprehensive coverage (throughout) — a genuinely useful final set with several abstract/discourse-level uses.

---

## 1. Intuitive Explanation

- **WITHOUT** = core image: the **absence** of something — literally lacking it.
- **BEYOND** = core image: **past a limit/boundary** — further than a given point, extending Lesson 41's "within" (inside a limit) with its logical opposite.
- **AROUND** = core image: **circular position/approximate location** — encircling something, or loosely near a point (extending to approximate quantities/times).
- **AGAINST** = core image: **contact with opposition/resistance** — physically touching while opposing, or opposing an idea/position.
- **DESPITE** = core image: **concession** — acknowledging an obstacle while stating an outcome that happened anyway (a discourse-level preposition, connecting directly to Lesson 28's contrast markers).
- **THROUGHOUT** = core image: **comprehensive coverage** of an entire space or time period, extending Lesson 41's "through" (passage) to mean "the entirety of," not just passage.

---

## 2. The Linguistic Explanation

### 2a. WITHOUT — Absence
- Physical: "coffee without sugar," "a room without windows"
- Abstract: "without a doubt," "without permission," "without fail," "without further ado"
- **Grammatical note:** "without" + gerund is a common structure for describing an action that didn't accompany another: "She left without saying goodbye." (NOT "without to say" — without takes a gerund, not an infinitive, a common Spanish-speaker error given *sin decir* structurally parallels the gerund but learners sometimes default to infinitive patterns from other contexts)

### 2b. BEYOND — Past a Limit
- Physical: "beyond the mountains," "beyond the horizon"
- Abstract: "beyond doubt," "beyond belief," "beyond repair," "beyond your control," "go beyond expectations" (exceed)

### 2c. AROUND — Circular/Approximate Position
- Physical: "walk around the block," "sit around the table" (encircling)
- Time/quantity (approximate): "around 5 PM" (approximately), "around 100 people" (approximately) — a genuinely common, high-frequency use for imprecise estimates
- Abstract: "the conversation revolved around the budget" (centered on a topic)

### 2d. AGAINST — Opposition/Contact
- Physical: "lean against the wall" (contact, support)
- Abstract: "vote against the proposal," "argue against an idea," "go against the rules," "protect against risks," "against all odds" (a common fixed expression)

### 2e. DESPITE — Concession
- "Despite the rain, we went for a walk." (acknowledging an obstacle, stating an outcome that happened anyway)
- **Grammatical note:** "despite" is followed by a noun/gerund, NOT a full clause: "despite the rain" / "despite being tired" — NOT "despite it was raining." For a full clause, use "although/even though" instead (Lesson 28 callback): "Although it was raining, we went for a walk."
- **Despite vs. in spite of:** functionally identical, "in spite of" is a slightly more old-fashioned/formal-leaning synonym phrase, both followed by the same noun/gerund structure.

### 2f. THROUGHOUT — Comprehensive Coverage
- Time: "throughout the year," "throughout history" (the entire duration, not just passage through part of it)
- Space: "throughout the country," "throughout the building" (present in every part, not just moving through it)

---

## 3. The Critical DESPITE Grammar Trap
This is worth its own emphasis because it's an extremely common error: **despite/in spite of are followed by a noun or gerund, never by a full clause with its own subject and verb.** This directly parallels Lesson 39's "due to" trap (also requiring a noun phrase, not a clause). The fix, when you have a full clause, is to switch to "although" or "even though" instead — a genuinely important structural distinction that gets confused constantly because Spanish *a pesar de que* can introduce a full clause more flexibly.

---

## 4. Why Native Speakers Maintain These Distinctions
Each of these prepositions captures a genuinely distinct relationship: lacking something (without), exceeding a limit (beyond), circular/approximate position (around), opposition/contact (against), acknowledging an obstacle (despite), and comprehensive coverage (throughout) — precise tools for describing absence, extent, position, conflict, concession, and totality, respectively, without needing longer explanatory phrases.

---

## 5. When Each Matters Most
- **Without** — everyday, all registers, extremely high-frequency.
- **Beyond** — common in both literal descriptions and abstract/emphatic expressions (beyond belief, beyond repair).
- **Around** — extremely common for approximate time/quantity estimates in everyday speech.
- **Against** — common in argumentative/persuasive contexts (voting, arguing) and physical descriptions.
- **Despite** — essential for balanced, nuanced argumentative writing and speech (Lesson 28's contrast-marker family).
- **Throughout** — common in narrative, historical, and descriptive writing/speech emphasizing comprehensive coverage.

---

## 6. Frequency & Register
All six are used across registers, though "despite"/"in spite of" and "throughout" lean slightly more toward careful/formal speech and writing compared to their more casual alternatives (although/even though for despite; "the whole time"/"all over" for throughout in very casual speech).

---

## 7. American vs. British Note
No major systematic differences in these prepositions' core usage or frequency between American and British English.

---

## 8. Common Mistakes Made by Spanish Speakers

| Mistake | Why | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| "She left without to say goodbye." | Applying infinitive instead of gerund after "without" | "She left without saying goodbye." |
| "Despite it was raining, we went out." | Following "despite" with a full clause instead of a noun/gerund | "Despite the rain, we went out." / "Although it was raining, we went out." |
| "I'll arrive at around 5 PM" said as if "around" were somehow less precise/acceptable than giving an exact time | Occasionally avoiding "around" out of a mistaken sense that approximation is imprecise/unprofessional | "Around 5 PM" is completely natural, professional, and commonly used for reasonable estimates — no need to avoid it |
| "He argued for the proposal" when intending opposition | Confusing "against" (opposition) with "for" (support) — a basic but real error under time pressure | "He argued against the proposal." (if intending opposition) |

---

## 9. Common Misconceptions
- ❌ "Despite and although are completely interchangeable, just choose whichever." — They require different grammatical structures (despite + noun/gerund; although + full clause), not free stylistic variation.
- ❌ "Around is only used for physical, circular movement." — As Section 2c shows, "around" extends heavily into approximate time/quantity estimates, an extremely common, everyday abstract use.
- ❌ "Beyond only means physical distance." — It extends significantly into emphatic abstract expressions (beyond doubt, beyond belief, beyond repair) that are highly idiomatic and frequently used.

---

## 10. Pronunciation Note
"Throughout" shares the same "-ough" pronunciation pattern as "through" (/θruːˈaʊt/) — worth connecting to Lesson 41's pronunciation note about this irregular spelling family. "Despite" and "beyond" retain relatively stable, clear pronunciation even in fast speech, as they're less commonly reduced than pure function words like prepositions of location.

---

## 11. Memory Technique
**Six-image reference set:** WITHOUT = an empty hand (absence). BEYOND = a line on the ground, stepped over (past a limit). AROUND = a circle drawn around something (encircling/approximate). AGAINST = two hands pushing against each other (opposition/contact). DESPITE = a raincloud with a person walking anyway (concession). THROUGHOUT = a color filling an entire shape completely (comprehensive coverage).

---

## 12. Patterns (Not Isolated Rules)

> **Pattern:** DESPITE/IN SPITE OF + noun/gerund (never a full clause) — this directly parallels DUE TO's identical grammatical restriction from Lesson 28, reinforcing a recurring structural category: certain preposition-like connectors require a noun phrase object, while their clause-taking counterparts (although, because) require a full subject-verb clause.

---

## 13. Real-Life Examples (Progressively Harder)

**Level 1:**
- "I can't live without coffee." (absence)
- "We'll arrive around noon." (approximate time)

**Level 2 — despite vs. although, correct grammatical structures:**
- "Despite the traffic, we arrived on time." (noun)
- "Although there was heavy traffic, we arrived on time." (full clause)

**Level 3 — beyond in abstract, emphatic use:**
- "The results were beyond our expectations." (exceeding a limit, abstractly)

**Level 4 — combining all six prepositions correctly in one sophisticated, natural passage:**
- "Throughout the entire negotiation, the two sides argued against each other's proposals, and despite several attempts at compromise, they remained beyond agreement — until, around midnight, and without any warning, one side finally gave in." (throughout = comprehensive time coverage; against = opposition; despite = concession + noun; beyond = past a limit, abstractly; around = approximate time; without = absence)

---

## 14. Related Expressions / Terms
- **In spite of** — a synonym for "despite," slightly more formal/old-fashioned, same grammatical restriction (noun/gerund, not clause).
- **Although / even though** — the clause-taking counterparts to "despite," directly connecting to Lesson 28's discourse markers.
- **Due to** — the identical grammatical-restriction pattern from Lesson 28, worth reviewing alongside "despite" for reinforcement.
