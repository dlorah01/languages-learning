# Lesson 47 — Phrasal Verbs with COME and GO
### Phase 4: Phrasal Verbs | Module 4 of 7

---

## 0. Where this fits
Lesson 33 taught COME and GO as core verbs, including the "toward the speaker / away from the speaker" distinction. Lessons 45 and 46 gave you separability and three-part verbs. This lesson uses all of that, and adds one idea: **COME and GO are almost entirely *intransitive*** (no direct object), so separability barely matters. What matters instead is **direction, perspective, and three-part patterns** such as *come up with*, *go through with*, *come down to*, and *get away with* (Lesson 44 connection).

---

## 1. Intuitive Explanation
COME and GO are two arrows on a map:

- **COME** → movement or change **toward a reference point** (you, the listener, the topic, the "normal" state).
- **GO** → movement or change **away from** that reference point, or **onward** along a path.

The particle then tells you *what kind of path*:

- **come/go + up / down** → vertical or scale (approach, appear, decrease, collapse)
- **come/go + in / out / over / back / across** → crossing boundaries (enter, appear in public, visit, return, find by chance)
- **come/go + on / off / through / along** → progress along a route (continue, happen, survive, accompany)
- **come/go + after / for / with / by** → relationship to a person or object (pursue, attack, match, pass)

A good test: **Who is the reference point?** If the thing is arriving at, or reaching, the speaker's mental "here" (or the "normal state"), you probably want COME. If it is leaving, ending, or failing, you probably want GO.

---

## 2. The Linguistic Explanation

### 2.1 Why COME vs GO is perspective, not geography
The choice is **deictic**: it depends on where the speaker places the center of the conversation. That is why these two are both correct:
- *"Are you coming to the party?"* (I will be at the party, or I imagine myself there.)
- *"Are you going to the party?"* (neutral, no assumption about where I'll be.)

The same logic extends to abstract change:
- **come** + adjective = toward a normal or hoped-for state: *come true, come alive, come clean, come undone*.
- **go** + adjective = toward a negative or unwanted state: *go wrong, go bad, go mad, go bankrupt, go missing, go off*.

This is the Lesson 35/36 "become-chart" in miniature. *Come true* (something wished arrives) vs *go wrong* (something drifts away from what you wanted).

### 2.2 Families: COME

| Family | Core idea | Phrasal verbs |
|---|---|---|
| **Arrival / appearance** | appear toward you | come **up**, come **out**, come **in**, come **over**, come **round/around** |
| **Return / origin** | where it begins or ends | come **back**, come **from**, come **down to** |
| **Discovery / ideas** | something reaches the mind | come **across**, come **up with**, come **to**, come **by** |
| **Progress / change** | development | come **along**, come **on**, come **through**, come **off** |
| **Conflict / relationship** | between people | come **after**, come **between**, come **down on** |

### 2.3 Families: GO

| Family | Core idea | Phrasal verbs |
|---|---|---|
| **Departure / ending** | away, over | go **away**, go **out**, go **off**, go **under** |
| **Continuation** | onward | go **on**, go **ahead**, go **along with**, go **through** |
| **Experience / examination** | passing through | go **through**, go **over**, go **into** |
| **Change / drop** | up or down | go **up**, go **down**, go **down with** |
| **Matching / choosing** | fit | go **with**, go **for**, go **by** |
| **Failure / collapse** | falls apart | go **under**, go **wrong**, go **off** |

### 2.4 COME meanings with examples

- **come up** — arise, be mentioned, or approach: *Something came up at work.* / *The topic came up in the meeting.* / *The sun comes up at six.*
- **come up with** — invent, think of (three-part, inseparable): *She came up with a great idea.*
- **come across** — find by chance; or seem: *I came across this old photo.* / *He comes across as arrogant.*
- **come out** — be published, revealed, or appear: *The film comes out in May.* / *The truth finally came out.* / *The stain came out.*
- **come back** — return: *When are you coming back?*
- **come over** — visit informally: *Come over for dinner.* (also: *What's come over you?* = what's affecting you)
- **come round / around** — visit; regain consciousness; change opinion (UK/US): *He eventually came round to our view.*
- **come down to** — be essentially about: *It all comes down to money.*
- **come along** — accompany or progress: *Why don't you come along?* / *How's the project coming along?*
- **come through** — survive or deliver: *She came through for us when we needed her.*
- **come by** — obtain (often "how did you come by that?") or visit briefly: *Come by anytime.*
- **come to** — total or regain consciousness: *The bill comes to $60.* / *He came to a few minutes later.*
- **come off** — succeed, detach, or happen: *The plan didn't come off.* / *The button came off.*
- **come down with** — catch an illness (three-part): *I think I'm coming down with a cold.*
- **come after** — pursue or attack: *The tax authorities came after him.*

### 2.5 GO meanings with examples

- **go on** — continue; happen: *Go on, I'm listening.* / *What's going on?*
- **go ahead** — proceed, permission: *Go ahead and start.*
- **go through** — experience a hard thing; examine: *She went through a divorce.* / *Let's go through the report.*
- **go over** — review: *We went over the numbers twice.*
- **go out** — leave home; date; stop burning: *They've been going out for a year.* / *The lights went out.*
- **go off** — explode, ring, spoil, or leave: *The alarm went off.* / *The milk went off.* (UK) / *He went off in a huff.*
- **go away** — leave: *Go away!* / *The pain didn't go away.*
- **go up / go down** — rise / fall: *Prices went up.* / *Sales went down.* (also: *The computers went down* = failed)
- **go for** — choose or attack: *I'll go for the soup.* / *The dog went for him.*
- **go with** — match or accompany: *That tie goes with your shirt.* / *I'll go with your suggestion.*
- **go by** — pass or follow: *Time goes by quickly.* / *Go by the rules.* / *She goes by Mia.* (known as)
- **go under** — fail financially: *The company went under in 2020.*
- **go along with** — agree with (three-part): *I'll go along with your plan.*
- **go through with** — complete a difficult action (three-part): *He couldn't go through with the wedding.*
- **go back on** — break a promise (three-part): *She never goes back on her word.*
- **go into** — discuss in detail, enter a field: *I won't go into details.* / *She went into medicine.*

### 2.6 Separability
Almost all COME/GO phrasal verbs are **intransitive** or **prepositional** (the particle works like a preposition), so they are **inseparable**:

| Verb | Type | Example |
|---|---|---|
| come across (find) | Inseparable | *I came across it.* (never: *came it across*) |
| come up with | Inseparable | *come up with a plan / come up with it* |
| go through | Inseparable | *go through the documents / go through them* |
| go over | Inseparable | *go over it* |
| go for | Inseparable | *go for it* |
| come after | Inseparable | *come after him* |
| go along with | Inseparable | *go along with it* |
| come down with | Inseparable | *come down with the flu* |
| go off, go on, come back | Intransitive | no object |

**Rule of thumb for this lesson:** if you can't picture the object being *moved by hand*, the verb is inseparable. You cannot "move" an idea, a path, or a disease.

(Rare exception: *go through* is sometimes seen as separable in the sense of "put someone through something," but treat it as inseparable.)

## 3. Why Native Speakers Use Them
Latinate verbs sound distant. Phrasal COME/GO verbs carry **motion imagery** that makes talk feel alive:

| Phrasal (natural) | Formal alternative |
|---|---|
| come up with | devise, conceive |
| come across | encounter, discover |
| come down to | amount to, depend on |
| go through | experience, undergo, examine |
| go over | review |
| go ahead | proceed |
| go on | continue |
| go under | become insolvent |
| go through with | carry out |
| go along with | acquiesce in, comply with |
| come about | occur, arise |

In an interview, *"I came up with a solution"* sounds confident and human; *"I devised a solution"* sounds slightly more formal and is fine in writing.

---

## 4. When to Use / When Not To
- **Use** *come up with* for ideas, plans, solutions, excuses, names. (Never for physical objects: you don't "come up with" a pencil.)
- **Use** *come across* in two senses: discovery (*I came across an article*) and impression (*she comes across as shy*). The second is very common in feedback and interviews.
- **Use** *go through* for hardship (*she went through a lot*), for examining (*go through the numbers*), and for processes (*go through customs*).
- **Don't** use *come out* in place of *go out* for "leave the house." *Go out* leaves your place. *Come out* means appear/emerge.
- **Don't** say *go back to home*; say *go back home* (no preposition before *home*).
- **Don't** use *come up with* + object that already exists (*I came up with a book*). It means invent, not obtain.

## 6. American vs. British Notes
- **go off** (spoil): UK *The milk has gone off.* US *The milk has gone bad / spoiled / turned.*
- **come round**: UK *come round for tea*. US *come over / stop by*.
- **go on holiday** (UK) vs. **go on vacation** (US). **go on a course** (UK) vs. **take a class** (US).
- **go to hospital / go to university** (UK, no article) vs. **go to the hospital / go to the university** (US uses *the* for hospital; *go to college/university* stays without).
- **go and + verb** is common in UK: *Go and ask him.* US often drops *and*: *Go ask him.*
- **come a cropper** (UK, fall or fail): *He came a cropper on the ice.* Rare in US.
- **go ahead** and **go for it** are universal.
- **Be gone / be going to** in speech: US *I'm gonna go*; UK *I'm going to go.*

## 7. Common Mistakes Made by Spanish Speakers

1. **Choosing COME vs GO from Spanish *venir/ir* perspective**
   - Spanish uses *ir* more flexibly. In English, *come* shifts to the listener's destination: *"I'm coming!"* (not *I'm going!*) when someone calls you.
   - ❌ *I'm going!* (answering a call)
   - ✅ *I'm coming!*
   - ❌ *Can I go to your house tomorrow?* → ✅ *Can I come over to your house tomorrow?* (natural when moving toward the listener)

2. **Adding "to" before home**
   - ❌ *I went to home.* / *Come back to home.*
   - ✅ *I went home.* / *Come back home.*

3. **Using *come up with* for physical things**
   - ❌ *I came up with a new phone* (meaning I bought one).
   - ✅ *I got a new phone.* / *I came up with a new idea.*

4. **Confusing *come across* (find) with *cross***
   - ❌ *I crossed this article yesterday.*
   - ✅ *I came across this article yesterday.*

5. **Separating inseparable verbs**
   - ❌ *I came it across.* / *Let's go the numbers through.*
   - ✅ *I came across it.* / *Let's go through the numbers.*

6. **Mixing *go on* (continue) with *go in* or *go ahead***
   - ❌ *Continue to talk, go in.*
   - ✅ *Go on, I'm listening.* / *Go ahead.*

7. **Using the infinitive after *go along with / come down to***
   - ❌ *I go along with to do it.* / *It comes down to decide.*
   - ✅ *I'll go along with doing it.* / *It comes down to deciding.* (or: *It comes down to whether we decide.*)

8. **Translating *salir* with the wrong verb**
   - ❌ *The film goes out in May.*
   - ✅ *The film comes out in May.*
   - ✅ *The lights went out.* (stopped)
   - ❌ *The sun goes out at six.* → ✅ *The sun comes up at six.*

9. **Translating *dar* + preposition**
   - ❌ *He gave up with a great idea.* → ✅ *He came up with a great idea.*

10. **Mispronouncing *come* as /kəʊm/**
    - ❌ /koʊm/ ("comb-eh")
    - ✅ /kʌm/ ("cum" with a short vowel). *Come* and *some*, *love*, *done* share this irregular vowel.

---

## 8. Common Misconceptions
- ❌ "COME means to arrive and GO means to leave, always." — It's perspective: *"I'll come to your office at 3"* uses COME because I'm moving toward the listener's location.
- ❌ "*Come up with* is just a casual way to say *invent*." — It also covers excuses, solutions, money (*come up with the cash*), and names. It's wider.
- ❌ "*Go off* always means explode." — It also means ring (alarm), spoil (food, UK), leave angrily, or stop liking someone (UK: *I've gone off him*).
- ❌ "Phrasal verbs are optional." — Many have **no** non-phrasal equivalent in everyday use: nobody says *discover a solution* when they mean *come up with one*.
- ❌ "*Go through* only means to experience pain." — It also means to examine, to be approved (*the law went through*), and to follow a process.

---

## 9. Pronunciation Note
1. **COME /kʌm/** — short vowel (like *cup*, *luck*). Not /koʊm/.
2. **GO /ɡoʊ/** (US) or /ɡəʊ/ (UK) — a diphthong, not a pure vowel. Spanish speakers often say a pure /o/. Slide the lip into /ʊ/ at the end.
3. **Particle stress:** *come ACROSS*, *go THROUGH*, *come UP with*. In *come up with*, stress is on UP: /kʌm ˈʌp wɪð/. *With* is reduced: /wɪð/ or even /wɪ/.
4. **Weak forms:** *go on* often links as /ɡoʊˈwɒn/ ("go-won") with a glide /w/; *come on* links as /kʌˈmɒn/. These are real phonetic phenomena, not sloppiness.
5. **Going to → gonna:** a rhythm marker. *I'm gonna go through it* /aɪm ɡənə ɡoʊ θruː ɪt/ is the natural US rhythm. Use it for listening, and in speaking if the register allows.
6. **Noun forms stress the first syllable:** a **COME**back, a **GO**-between, a **BREAK**through (next lessons). Compare *to come BACK* (verb) vs. *a COMEback* (noun).
7. **Practice the "th" in *through*:** /θruː/ (tongue between teeth, then rounded lips). Not /tru/ or /fru/.

## 10. Memory Technique

### Draw two arrows
On a page, draw yourself in the center.
- **COME** arrows point **toward** you: *come in, come back, come over, come up, come across*.
- **GO** arrows point **away** or **onward**: *go out, go away, go off, go on, go under*.
Add the particle on each arrow. Review this picture, not a word list.

### Positive vs negative state (the "come/go adjectives")
| COME + adjective (toward normal/good) | GO + adjective (away/bad) |
|---|---|
| come true | go wrong |
| come alive | go bad |
| come clean | go missing |
| come undone | go mad |
| come naturally | go bankrupt |

Memory rule: **things come *true*, things go *wrong*.**

### Three-part "COME/GO" ladder
- come **up with** (invent) → go **along with** (agree) → go **through with** (complete) → come **down with** (catch) → go **back on** (break a promise)
Notice the sequence: invent a plan, agree to it, carry it out, get sick from stress, then break your promise. A silly story is easier to retrieve than five separate cards.

### Spaced retrieval
Front: *She ___ ___ ___ a brilliant idea.* → **came up with**
Front: *He couldn't ___ ___ ___ the wedding.* → **go through with**
Review on days 1, 3, 7, 14, 30. Say the sentence aloud each time.

### Pairs with TAKE and PUT
| Idea | Verb pair |
|---|---|
| Postpone vs happen | put **off** / come **up** (*something came up*) |
| Accept vs agree | take **on** / go **along with** |
| Remove vs leave | take **out** / go **out** |
| Start | take **up** / come **to** (*come to like it*) |

## 11. Patterns (Not Isolated Rules)

> **Pattern 1:** **come up with + idea/plan/excuse/solution/money.** If you can invent or produce it, you can *come up with* it.

> **Pattern 2:** **come across as + adjective.** *She comes across as confident.* This is the standard way to describe the *impression* someone gives. (Not: *she comes across confident*.)

> **Pattern 3:** **go through + hardship/process/documents.** Three uses, one pattern: passing through something to its end.

> **Pattern 4:** **it comes down to + noun/gerund/clause.** *It comes down to trust. / It comes down to whether we can afford it.* This is a very natural way to summarize a complex issue in discussions and interviews.

> **Pattern 5:** **be going on** for present events: *What's going on?* **Go on** + gerund for repeated or continued action: *She went on talking for an hour.* (vs. *go on to + verb* = do something next: *She went on to become a doctor.*)

> **Pattern 6:** **come/go + adjective** for states: toward normal/positive = come; toward bad = go.

> **Connection to Lesson 44 (GET):** *get away with*, *get through*, *get along with* parallel *go along with* and *go through*: the same three-part logic. Compare *get along with* (have a good relationship) with *go along with* (agree).

> **Connection to Lesson 43 (dependent prepositions):** *comply with, agree with, deal with* correspond to *go along with, come to terms with*; the final preposition must be memorized with the verb.

> **Connection to Lesson 45 (TAKE):** *take over* (assume control) and *come after* (pursue) both express change of control, but only the first is under your control.

## 12. Real-Life Examples (Progressively Harder)

**Level 1 — Basic**
- Come **in**, please.
- Go **away**!
- When are you coming **back**?
- Go **on**, I'm listening.

**Level 2 — Everyday**
- Something **came up**, so I'll be late.
- The alarm **went off** at six.
- Can you **come over** tonight?
- Let's **go over** the plan again.
- Prices **went up** again.

**Level 3 — Work and social**
- Who **came up with** this idea?
- I **came across** your résumé yesterday.
- She **comes across** as very confident.
- We're **going through** the documents now.
- Sure, **go ahead** and send it.

**Level 4 — Abstract**
- It all **comes down to** timing.
- He **went through** a lot after the layoffs.
- The company **went under** during the recession.
- I'm **coming down with** a cold.
- The plan **didn't come off**.

**Level 5 — Native-like**
- "I'll **go along with** it, but I want to **go over** the details first."
- "She **came through** when everyone else had given up."
- "He couldn't **go through with** the surgery, so they **went back on** the date."
- "I **came across** as defensive, which wasn't my intention."
- "After the product **came out**, interest **died down**, but it later **took off**."

**Fixed phrases**
- **come to think of it** — now that I remember
- **come what may** — whatever happens
- **come to terms with** — accept a reality
- **go without saying** — be obvious
- **go the extra mile** — do more than expected
- **go back to square one** — start over
- **go downhill** — decline
- **go out of your way** — make a special effort
- **it goes without saying that**
- **come in handy** — be useful

## 13. Collocations & Related Expressions

**Collocations**
- come **up with** an idea / a plan / an excuse / a name / the money
- come **across** an article / a problem / as honest
- come **out** (film, book, truth, secret)
- come **down to** money / trust / luck / timing
- come **to** a conclusion / an agreement / a decision
- go **through** a process / a crisis / customs / a phase / the numbers
- go **over** the details / the notes / the plan
- go **for** a walk / a run / a drink / the cheaper option
- go **ahead** with a plan / a project
- go **wrong** / go **bad** / go **missing** / go **bankrupt**

**Contrast with similar verbs**
| Idea | Verb | Difference |
|---|---|---|
| Find by chance | come across / run into / stumble on | *come across* is neutral; *run into* often people; *stumble on* stresses accident |
| Examine | go through / go over / look over | *go over* = review quickly; *go through* = in detail, step by step |
| Continue | go on / carry on / keep on | *go on* is neutral; *carry on* is UK-leaning; *keep on* emphasizes persistence |
| Arise | come up / crop up / turn up | *come up* is neutral; *crop up* is UK/informal; *turn up* = appear unexpectedly |
| Invent | come up with / devise / think of | *think of* is simplest; *come up with* implies effort |

## 17. Speaking Practice

**Drill 1 — Linked chunks (five times each, aloud):**
> "Go on, I'm listening." / "I'm coming!" / "It all comes down to trust." / "Who came up with that?" / "She comes across as confident."

**Drill 2 — COME or GO?** Say the sentence with the right verb, aloud:
- "(Phone) I'll ___ to your office at 3." (come)
- "The prices ___ up every year." (go)
- "My dream finally ___ true." (came)

**Drill 3 — Diphthong GO.** Say: *go, no, show, slow, don't go.* Slide to /ʊ/ at the end. Compare with Spanish *no* (pure vowel). Record yourself.

**Drill 4 — 60-second story.** Tell a story about something that went wrong and how you came up with a fix. Include *come up, go wrong, go through, come across,* and *come down to*. Check for: no *to* before *home*; gerund after *with*.

**Drill 5 — Role-play.**
Partner: "We've got a budget problem. Any ideas?"
You: "I think it comes down to timing. I came across a cheaper supplier last week, and I can go through the numbers with you if you like."

**Mini Quiz**
1. Because COME is used when moving toward the listener's location. Say "I'm coming!"
2. *Come across* = find by chance (*I came across a photo*). *Come across as* = give an impression (*she comes across as shy*), followed by an adjective.
3. Three-part: *come up with, go along with, go through with, come down with, go back on*. They are inseparable.
4. It means "is essentially about / depends on." A noun, gerund, or *whether* clause can follow.
5. Any two: explode, ring (alarm), spoil (food, UK), leave, stop liking (UK).
6. *Home* works as an adverb here, so no preposition is needed: *go home*.
7. COME + adjective = toward a good or normal state (*come true*); GO + adjective = toward a bad state (*go wrong*).
8. /kʌm/, a short vowel. Spanish speakers often say /koʊm/ following spelling.
